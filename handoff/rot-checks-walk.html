Culture Vulture — checking the new safeguards yourself

This preview cannot copy for you or save your notes. Open this page in your web browser instead: in Finder, go to Documents → Projects → Claude projects → bay-week → handoff and double-click rot-checks-walk.html. If you stay here, click a grey box to select its text and press Cmd+C, and keep this page open until you have copied your notes out.

What this is: four short commands that let you watch the new safeguards work. Each week now passes through an automatic check before it is published — called the gate below. The gate can do two things: refuse a week, which stops it being published, or warn, which lets the week through and puts the warning in the weekly report. About 10 minutes.

Nothing you do here can harm the site. The first three commands make a throwaway copy of the site, damage the copy on purpose, and run the gate on the copy. The fourth changes nothing at all. Nothing is published by any of them.

How sure is this page: I ran all four commands on this Mac on 9 October and each gave the result shown here. Each command also ends with a line saying whether it matched, so you do not have to compare text by eye.

Where to look hard: not at whether it works — the last line of each step tells you that. Look at whether the messages make sense to you and whether these are the right things to stop a week for. Those are the two things only you can judge, and what the note boxes are for.

Before you start

Open Terminal in the project folder

Press Cmd+Space, type Terminal, press Return. A window with a line to type on appears.

Paste this and press Return (it moves you into the project folder):
cd ~/Documents/Projects/Claude\ projects/bay-week

What you should see: nothing much — a new line to type on, now ending in bay-week.

Step 1

A venue spelled a new way is refused

The problem this catches: the same room written two ways — “The Fillmore” one week, “Fillmore” the next — splits into two venues, and each half looks quieter than the room really is.

What the command does: in the throwaway copy, it renames this week's five Fillmore listings to “Fillmore”, then runs the gate.

Paste this and press Return. It takes about 20 seconds.
bash handoff/rot-checks-walk.sh 1
What you should see, among other lines:
FAIL drift.py — a rot check is blocking this week BLOCK “Fillmore” (San Francisco, 5 listing(s)) is a new spelling of “The Fillmore”. Use the established spelling. passed 10 failed 1 skipped 0 warnings 4 VERIFICATION FAILED — do not merge. Escalate with the failures above. RESULT: the week was REFUSED. MATCHES WHAT THIS STEP SHOULD SHOW: YES — refused, and the message names both spellings.

The highlighted BLOCK line is the one that matters: it names the new spelling, the old one, and what to do. The four WARN lines about ODC Theater, Filoli, Felton Music Hall and Sweetwater Music Hall are real and are explained in Step 3; ignore them here.

Note: is the BLOCK message clear? Would you know what had gone wrong from it alone?
Step 2

A venue far below its usual count is refused — until the write-up explains it

The problem this catches: a venue that normally has several listings suddenly has one, and nobody says whether the venue was quiet or the research missed it. The gate now refuses the week until the weekly write-up has a line, under the heading Thin this week, saying whether the venue's calendar was reached or unreached and what was checked.

What the command does: this week has a real example — the Regency Ballroom, 1 listing after 2, 2, 4 and 5 — and the write-up already has its line. In the throwaway copy the command deletes that line and runs the gate, then puts the line back and runs the gate again.

Paste this and press Return. It runs the gate twice, so about 40 seconds.
bash handoff/rot-checks-walk.sh 2
What you should see — first half, the line deleted:
FAIL drift.py — a rot check is blocking this week BLOCK Regency Ballroom: 1 listing(s) after 2, 2, 4, 5. 2026-10-05.md does not account for it under “Thin this week” — add a line naming the venue, saying reached or unreached, and what was checked. VERIFICATION FAILED — do not merge. Escalate with the failures above. RESULT: the week was REFUSED.
Second half, the line put back (the command prints the line itself, then):
ok Regency Ballroom: 1 listing(s) after 2, 2, 4, 5 — accounted for passed 11 failed 0 skipped 0 warnings 4 VERIFICATION PASSED — safe to merge. RESULT: the week was LET THROUGH. MATCHES WHAT THIS STEP SHOULD SHOW: YES — refused without the line, let through with it.
What this does not do: the gate checks that the line exists and says reached or unreached. It cannot check that the line is true. Read the Regency line the command prints and judge whether that is the kind of answer you want a thin week to carry.
Note: is the Regency's line a good enough answer? Is “2026-10-05.md” in the BLOCK message clear enough, or should it say “this week's write-up”?
Step 3

The two warnings appear, and nothing stops

The problems these catch: two slower kinds of rot that are worth saying out loud but not worth holding a week for.

  • A watched venue gone quiet — a venue the project promises to check every week that has had no listing for four weeks.
  • A relied-on venue nobody wrote down — an indoor venue that has appeared in three or more weeks but is missing from the list of venues the weekly research is told to check.

What the command does: the first kind is already there for real, four times over. For the second, the command removes Oakland Arena from the venue list in the throwaway copy, then runs the gate.

Paste this and press Return. About 20 seconds.
bash handoff/rot-checks-walk.sh 3
What you should see, among other lines:
passed 11 failed 0 skipped 0 warnings 5 WARN ODC Theater is declared tier 1 and has produced no listing in 4 weeks WARN Filoli is declared tier 1 and has produced no listing in 4 weeks WARN Felton Music Hall is declared tier 1 and has produced no listing in 4 weeks WARN Sweetwater Music Hall is declared tier 1 and has produced no listing in 4 weeks WARN Oakland Arena (Oakland) has been listed in 4 weeks and is not in sources.yml VERIFICATION PASSED — safe to merge. RESULT: the week was LET THROUGH, with warnings. MATCHES WHAT THIS STEP SHOULD SHOW: YES — both kinds of warning printed and the week still passed.

“Tier 1” means a venue the research checks every week. “sources.yml” is the file that holds the list of venues to check.

Why the first four are there. They are real, and expected this week. Filoli, Felton Music Hall and Sweetwater Music Hall were each being read from a web page that did not show the venue's full calendar; all three were corrected on 9 October and no week has been researched since. ODC Theater was added to the list the same day and has never been listed. Monday's run should clear some of them. Felton and Sweetwater may keep theirs, because they are far enough out that only a top-three event of the week is listed from them — a question I will bring you once two or three weeks have run.
Note: are the two warnings understandable? Is a warning, rather than a refusal, the right strength for each?
Step 4

The live week, untouched, passes

What the command does: nothing is copied and nothing is changed. It runs the gate on the site exactly as it is published today.

Paste this and press Return. About 20 seconds.
bash handoff/rot-checks-walk.sh 4
What you should see, among other lines:
passed 11 failed 0 skipped 0 warnings 4 VERIFICATION PASSED — safe to merge. RESULT: the live week PASSED. MATCHES WHAT THIS STEP SHOULD SHOW: YES — passed. Warnings may be listed; they do not stop a week.
If you run this after Monday 12 October: Steps 1 to 3 always show the week of 5 October, because the throwaway copy is taken from the day the safeguards were finished. This step shows whatever week is live, so the venues named and the number of warnings may differ from the box above. The last line should still say YES.
Overall: do these four safeguards do what you wanted? Anything missing?
