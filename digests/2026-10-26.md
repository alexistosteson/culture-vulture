# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 26 October 2026

Monday 26 October – Sunday 1 November. 462 listings, ordered by remove
from Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**This is Halloween week and Día de los Muertos week at once, and the two are
pulling the digest in opposite directions.** The Halloween end is enormous,
free, and mostly in the street: a free block party in the Financial District on
Friday, trick-or-treat trails in Hayes Valley and North Shattuck and Oakland
Chinatown, the 12th Chinatown Halloween Festival on Waverly Place on Saturday,
and a naked bike ride from Rincon Park at ten in the morning. The Día de los
Muertos end is institutional and better made: **Oakland Ballet's *Luna
Mexicana*** at the Paramount on Friday and Sunday, a free Día de Muertos street
fair in the Tenderloin on Sunday afternoon, a free fiesta with cumbia DJs in
Fruitvale running nine hours, and **son jarocho and an ofrenda at La Peña** in
Berkeley on Sunday evening.

**The week's one unmissable concert is on a Monday and costs less than any other
listing of its stature.** **Bobby McFerrin** plays Freight & Salvage on the
26th — ten Grammys, one novelty hit everybody misremembers him for, and a body
of solo improvised vocal work almost nobody else attempts, in a 600-seat
listening room with the lights up. Nothing else this week is as unlikely.

**Thursday to Saturday belongs to Davies.** **Elim Chan** conducts the San
Francisco Symphony in Brahms's *Nänie* with the chorus, Pärt's *Magnificat*,
Renaud Capuçon in the Mendelssohn concerto, and John Adams's *Doctor Atomic
Symphony* — two of those are first performances by this orchestra, and
programming *Doctor Atomic* on Halloween night is not an accident. Tickets
started at $45 and orchestra seats at $59 when this was compiled, which for this
programme is the bargain of the autumn. There is a 10am open rehearsal on
Thursday as well.

**Saturday is the biggest day the digest has recorded: 110 listings, 25 of them
free and 13 outdoors.** The problem on Halloween night is not finding something,
it is choosing. **The Mummies** at Bottom of the Hill is the pick — San Mateo's
budget-rock originals, in bandages, in a room that closes for good at the end of
2026. **Kwaidan** at BAMPFA at half past six is the opposite kind of evening and
just as good. **John Scofield** is the second of three nights at SFJAZZ with
Larry Goldings, Oteil Burbridge and Adam Deitch. **Galactic** have Parlor Greens
supporting at the Fillmore. **Shpongle** play the second of two at the Fox with
live floral art. **Five hardcore bands cost five dollars at 924 Gilman.** And
the Paramount shows *Frankenstein* at three in the afternoon, which may be the
most sensible plan on the list.

**Sunday is unusually strong and almost entirely cheap.** Free first Sunday at
the Oakland Museum and at the Asian Art Museum, the free roller disco in Golden
Gate Park, Lindy in the Park on JFK Drive, the Castro Art Mart, Head West at the
Ferry Building, and two free Día de Muertos street events. The ticketed end is
good too: **The Psychedelic Furs** with We Are Scientists at the Guild in Menlo
Park, **Tom Jones** at the Masonic at eighty-six, Mozart's *Figaro* opening at
the War Memorial the night before, and **Katsura Sunshine's rakugo** at Rooster
T. Feathers in Sunnyvale — four-hundred-year-old Japanese comic monologues,
performed kneeling, in English, in a Sunnyvale comedy club.

**121 of the 462 listings are free and 55 are outdoors.** The free end is where
the week is deepest: three free comedy nights most evenings at The Function
alone, the free Japanese Tea Garden hour on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, free
rooftop stargazing at SF State on Wednesday, YBCA's galleries free all day
Wednesday, free video art on screens along Mid-Market for three nights running,
and the last weekend of Ocean Beach bonfires before the rings close for winter.

**The theatre is deep and almost all of it closes on 1 November.** *Dracula*
plays five performances at the Mountain View Center and shuts, with Halloween
night the obvious one to take. *Peter Pan Goes Wrong* runs eight at the
Playhouse. Word for Word's *Assimilation* runs five at Z Below. *Manon* gets
three at the War Memorial before *Figaro* opens over it. *Oh, Mary!* closes at
the Curran. *Gutenberg! The Musical!* closes at Hillbarn. The one thing opening
rather than closing is **Berkeley Rep's *Liberation***, whose press night is
Wednesday the 28th — and it is the one production this week whose times this run
could not confirm, because berkeleyrep.org answers with a redirect loop.

**The South Bay has 40 listings and two that matter.** **Caifanes** at the San
Jose Civic on Thursday is the better: the band that made rock en español sound
like its own tradition rather than an import, thirty-five years on. **Mdou
Moctar** at The Ritz the same night is the other, and in a 500-capacity room it
is arguably the better ticket — Tuareg desert blues at rock volume. They are the
same night and twenty minutes apart by car, which is this week's cruellest
conflict.

**The Peninsula has 27 listings and the Guild is carrying the region.** Blues
Traveler twice, French stand-up on Wednesday, a Dead tribute on Halloween and
the Furs on Sunday — four nights out of five, all a four-minute walk from
Caltrain Menlo Park. Stanford Live has one booking and it is a good one: **Timo
Andres, Taylor Mac and the San Francisco Girls Chorus on Glass and Ginsberg** in
Bing on Friday. Filoli's *Nightfall* and *Autumn Days* both close on 1 November.

**The outer region has one listing and that is a decision, not a gap.**
**Etana** at Sweetwater Music Hall on Sunday clears the brief's bar for the
drive: two Grammy nominations, a roots-reggae catalogue sung rather than
toasted, and a 300-capacity room she would not normally play in this country.
Everything else was weighed and did not clear it — the Catalyst has a Halloween
rave, Felton Music Hall has three shows including Great Lake Swimmers, who also
play Café du Nord on Wednesday, and Sweetwater itself has six more nights of
local booking. None of it is among the three best things in this week were it
local, which is the test.
