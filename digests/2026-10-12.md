# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 12 October 2026

Monday 12 October – Sunday 18 October. 415 listings, ordered by remove
from Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**Saturday is the week, and Saturday is a pile-up.** Ninety-four listings on one
day, which is the most this digest has recorded, and the top four of them are
mutually exclusive. The first **Market Street Arts & Theater Festival** takes six
blocks of mid-Market, three public plazas and 104 partner organisations from four
in the afternoon until eleven at night, and nearly all of it is free: the kickoff
at UN Plaza has Dear San Francisco, BANDALOOP dancing down the side of a
building, and drag laureate D'Arcy Drollinger. **Joe Bataan** plays Yerba Buena
Gardens free at two — the man who invented Latin soul in Spanish Harlem in 1967,
still fronting the band at eighty-four. **T-Pain and Ari Lennox** are in Golden
Gate Park from one. **Day Trip Festival** takes a downtown San Jose park for eight
hours. **The resolution, if you want one:** Bataan at two, Market Street from four,
and *heavens to betsy* at Bottom of the Hill at half eight.

**That last one is the booking of the week.** Corin Tucker and Tracy Sawyer's
pre-Sleater-Kinney band, two records of riot grrrl at its least compromised, two
nights in a 350-capacity room. It has not happened in thirty years. Street Eaters
support on Saturday, Cut-Rate Druggist on Sunday; thirty-five dollars either night.

**Four touring acts are playing two or three nights each, which is this week's
real signal.** Lila Downs takes SFJAZZ's main hall for three (15th, 16th, 17th),
Neurosis take the Fillmore for two (16th, 17th), Jessie Ware added a second
Warfield night (16th, 17th) and Rush play SAP Center twice (15th, 17th). Residency
length is a better measure of demand than ticket price, and this is the deepest
week of it since the digest began.

**The free count is 101 of 415, and the best of them are not the big names.**
Friday Nights at OMCA marks **sixty years since the Black Panther Party was
founded in Oakland** — the museum holds the archive, so the anniversary falls to
it — with MacArthur Maze on the Garden Stage, galleries open, five to nine, free.
Monday opens with **Jeremy Dutcher** in Yerba Buena Gardens, a Wolastoqiyik
composer writing new work from 1907 wax cylinders of his own community's songs.
**Shipyard Open Studios** opens three hundred artists' studios at Hunters Point
both weekend days. **Sunday Streets** closes Mission Street through the Excelsior
for five hours with the Salvadoran **Salvi Soul** block party folded into it, and
it is the season's last. The de Young and the Legion of Honor are both free to Bay
Area residents on Saturday; YBCA's galleries are free on Wednesday as always.

**Friday and Saturday carry 177 of the 415 listings between them.** Thursday has
54, Sunday an unusually heavy 80, Wednesday 50, Tuesday 36 — and Monday 18, which
is the thinnest night the digest has recorded and is real rather than a research
failure. The week totals 415 listings against 359 last week, which makes it the
largest this digest has covered; the drivers are a fully switched-on autumn
theatre season, two multi-night residencies in the city's two best halls, and a
Saturday carrying three separate outdoor festivals.

**Three separate arguments for being in Civic Center on Thursday.** San Francisco
Opera opens **Manon** at the War Memorial, seven performances from the 15th, from
thirty dollars. Davies has **Gautier Capuçon, Lisa Batiashvili and Jean-Yves
Thibaudet** playing chamber music together, which is the format where you hear
whether three soloists actually listen to each other. And Lila Downs opens at
SFJAZZ four blocks away. All three within walking distance, all on one night, and
nothing like it again this month.

**The dance is better than it has been all autumn and it is all in one building or
one street.** Smuin's *French Kiss* plays the Cowell Theater at Fort Mason four
more times (15th, 16th, 17th, 18th) — a hall no public calendar covers, reachable
only through the company's own pages. Printz Dance Project close a twenty-year
retrospective at ODC over the weekend. Word for Word open *Assimilation* at Z
Below on Saturday after three previews, staging Doctorow and Tolstaya without
cutting a sentence.

**The Peninsula has eighteen listings and two that matter.** MUNA at Stanford's
Frost Amphitheater on Sunday is the one event south of the city that would headline
anywhere, with Hemlocke Springs opening. The **54th Half Moon Bay Art & Pumpkin
Festival** closes Main Street for both weekend days, free, and the coast road is
the whole problem — Highway 92 is the bottleneck and the park-and-ride shuttle is
the answer. Monday's pumpkin weigh-off is the same festival's absurd overture.
Every summer series is long finished: Music on the Square, the Colma concerts and
the Palo Alto Twilight series are all done for the year.

**The outer region has two listings and that is a decision, not a gap.** Bonnie
Raitt plays the Santa Cruz Civic on Wednesday with Jon Cleary's New Orleans piano
band opening, which clears the brief's bar for the drive easily. Alborosie at
Moe's Alley the same night is the other — a Sicilian who moved to Kingston at
twenty-four and makes roots records the Jamaican establishment stopped
patronising. Everything else north and south was weighed and did not clear it:
Felton Music Hall has six shows this week and Sweetwater has seven, and none of
them would be among the top three events of the week if they were local. Both
venues' calendars were read in full. See **What could not be reached** for why
that distinction matters.

**Two bars are carrying more of this digest than some theatres.** Kilowatt and The
Knockout have twenty-three listings between them, every one of them reached
through a ticketing widget because neither venue's own page carries a single date.
A punk memorial roast starting at four in the afternoon, a one o'clock Sunday
matinee with Imaad Wasif, a live dating show, a Cowboy Bebop screening — none of
it reaches an aggregator, and that is the whole case for the route.

**The week's quiet failure is DNA Lounge, for the third week running.** Two
listings where the club's own calendar would show eight or ten. See **Thin this
week**.

## Monday 12 October

*18 listings.* The thinnest night of the week by a wide margin, and genuinely so. **Best of the night:** Jeremy Dutcher at Yerba Buena Gardens, free, at midday — a Wolastoqiyik composer who builds new work out of 1907 wax cylinders of his own community singing. Nothing else free this month is in that class. After dark the city belongs to the clubs: Triclops! at Bottom of the Hill if you want it loud, Shiner at the Ivy Room if you want it louder.

### South Bay

**TAEMIN** — SAP Center, San Jose, 7:30pm. The SHINee member's solo tour, which leans harder into choreography and a darker synth-pop palette than the group ever did. An arena booking on a Monday, which tells you how deep the K-pop audience runs here.

### Peninsula

**53rd Safeway World Championship Pumpkin Weigh-Off** — I.D.E.S. Grounds, Half Moon Bay, 7am, free. Competitive horticulture at its most absurd and most sincere: growers haul one-tonne squash in on flatbeds and a crane settles each on the scale. In-person entry is for growers; everyone else watches the livestream. Half Moon Bay is in no region list in the brief, filed Peninsula by proximity.

### San Francisco

**Indigenous Peoples' Day Sunrise Gathering** — Alcatraz Island, San Francisco, 4:15am, free. The gathering descends from the 1969-71 occupation of the island, and it begins in the dark on the parade ground with drum groups and speakers. The ceremony is free; the boat is $12 and the only way on. Dress for wind you will not believe.

**Japanese Tea Garden free admission hour** — Japanese Tea Garden, San Francisco, 9am, free. The oldest public Japanese garden in the United States, free for the first hour on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays. Non-residents otherwise pay $12-16; San Francisco residents are always free with ID. Go at nine on a weekday and you will share it with almost nobody.

**SF Indigenous Peoples Day with Jeremy Dutcher** — Yerba Buena Gardens, San Francisco, noon, free. Jeremy Dutcher is a Wolastoqiyik composer and tenor who builds new pieces around 1907 wax-cylinder recordings of his own community's songs, singing in a language with a few hundred speakers left. Free, outdoors, and the best thing on any Monday this month.

**Bistro Moustache: French Cafe Music** — Scopo Divino, San Francisco, 5pm, free. Accordion-and-guitar musette and gypsy jazz in a Fillmore wine bar, no cover. The repertoire is the Paris cafe songbook played straight, which is rarer here than you would think.

**Cyberpunk Cinema Night** — The Knockout, San Francisco, 6:30pm, free. A Cowboy Bebop episode at half six and then a feature, projected in a Mission dive bar for nothing. The free, unticketed end of The Knockout's calendar, which its ticketing widget does not carry.

**Hayes Valley Comedy Night** — The Function, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Free with an RSVP, $20-25 without one, two-drink minimum either way. A standing Monday showcase that pulls better names than a free room should, because it books the nights the clubs do not.

**Motown on Mondays** — Madrone Art Bar, San Francisco, 7pm, free. All-vinyl Motown and northern soul, running weekly since 2009 and still the most reliably good Monday in the city. Free before eight, five dollars after.

**Triclops!, Wife & Gumby's Junk** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 7:30pm, $15. Triclops! bill themselves acid-noise-prog-post-hardcore and that is not a joke at their expense — Johnny Geek's band genuinely does all of it in one song. Wife is noise rock, Gumby's Junk art-punk. One of the last autumns in this room before it closes at the end of 2026.

**KiNG MALA with Jayden Hammer** — The Independent, San Francisco, 8pm. Areli Castro writes sharp, slightly sinister alt-pop and sings it in a voice that keeps slipping toward a snarl. The live show is louder and more guitar-forward than the records suggest.

**Love Spells with Orca** — August Hall, San Francisco, 8pm. Hazy guitar-pop with a 1990s shoegaze inheritance worn openly. Two routes disagree on the room: August Hall's own page and the ticketing sweep both put it here, while the Café du Nord calendar also carries it as sold out. Going with the venue's own listing.

**MARO** — Bimbo's 365 Club, San Francisco, 8pm. Portuguese singer-songwriter with a Berklee jazz training she mostly hides — close-harmony layering over almost nothing, which is why the move to Bimbo's from the Castro suits her. Relocated show; original tickets are valid.

**Ludovico Einaudi** — Davies Symphony Hall, San Francisco. Minimalist piano loops at scale, somewhere between Satie and film score, playing to a 2,700-seat hall on the strength of streaming numbers no living composer can match. Davies has been dark since 4 October; this is the room reopening. No start time published on the route that reads.

**R-Evolution sculpture: final day** — Embarcadero Plaza, San Francisco, free. Marco Cochrane's 55-foot nude steel figure comes down after a long run on the plaza. The listing's own description refers to a celebration earlier in the month, so treat the de-install date as unconfirmed — worth a look if you are passing, not worth a trip.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Town Biz Comedy Night with HellaFunny** — Mesa Maguey, Oakland, 7pm, free. Free with RSVP in a Temescal mezcaleria, and the lineups lean local rather than touring. HellaFunny runs most of the Bay's free comedy and this is the best-run of its Monday rooms.

**Makenzie** — Yoshi's, Oakland. Found only in the ticketing sweep; Yoshi's own calendar does not carry this date, and no set time or billing is published. Descriptor is lower-confidence — verify before going.

**Shiner with Fotocrime** — Ivy Room, Albany. Allen Epley's Kansas City band made the loudest, most mathematical post-hardcore of the late 1990s and reformed without softening it. Fotocrime is Ryan Patterson of Coliseum doing cold, drum-machine post-punk. A 150-cap room for a band that should not fit in one.

## Tuesday 13 October

*36 listings.* Four good small-room bills at once and no obvious winner. **Best of the night:** Militarie Gun at the Chapel — four bands for twenty dollars, and Ian Shelton writes hardcore with pop choruses better than anyone currently doing it. Behind it, Provoker at the Great American is the better-looking room and the better local story. The Georgian National Ballet at the California Theatre is the week's one chance to see sword dancing in a 1927 picture palace.

### South Bay

**RIN with Sabrina Sterling** — The Ritz, San Jose, 7pm. German rapper whose cloud-rap melodies and Auto-Tune made him the biggest thing in Bietigheim-Bissingen and then in the country. A 500-cap room for an artist who plays arenas at home.

**The Phantom of the Opera** — San Jose Center for the Performing Arts, San Jose, 7:30pm. Broadway San Jose's touring Phantom, mid-way through a twelve-day stand. The 2,677-seat barn flatters the chandelier and does the quiet scenes no favours; book the front half of the orchestra or do not bother.

**Georgian National Ballet: Sukhishvili** — California Theatre, San Jose, 8pm. Eighty years of Georgian folk dance built on sword work, knee-drops onto bare boards and men landing on their toes in soft boots. Not ballet in the Western sense and all the better for it. The California Theatre is a 1927 picture palace and part of the draw.

**San Jose Sharks vs Boston Bruins** — SAP Center, San Jose, 8pm. The Bruins in town on a Tuesday, which is the cheapest way to see a visiting Original Six side all season. Listed for completeness; the brief deprioritises arenas.

### San Francisco

**San Francisco Botanical Garden free day** — San Francisco Botanical Garden, San Francisco, 7:30am, free. Second Tuesday of the month, free to everybody rather than residents only. The cloud-forest section is the reason to come and is at its best in damp October light.

**Civic Center Soundtrack: free concerts and food trucks** — Fulton Plaza, San Francisco, noon, free. Three hours of free lunchtime music on the plaza between the library and City Hall, with trucks. The programming is uneven and the point is that it is there at all, in a plaza that badly needs the footfall.

**Lunch Rhyme with Radio XII and Paola Ramirez** — Mechanics Monument Plaza, San Francisco, noon, free. Poetry read aloud at lunchtime on a downtown plaza, free, no RSVP. Downtown SF has been quietly running this kind of thing all year and nobody aggregates it.

**Walking Tour: Ferry Building** — Ferry Building, San Francisco, noon, free. An hour on the building itself — the 1898 clock tower, the nave, the freeway that hid it for thirty years. Free and better than the self-guided version.

**Ha-Chan, Shake Your Booty!** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Japanese independent comedy, one night only, part of the run of new Japanese cinema the Roxie has been threading through October.

**NAZA** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Playing nearly every day this week at the Roxie, which is the Roxie telling you it believes in it. Second of its seven-day run here.

**Litquake: Writing The Body, sports and identity** — The Treeline, Sports Basement Bryant, San Francisco, 6:30pm. Michael Copperman, Swetha Amit and Yi Shun Lai on writing about bodies that are being measured. Taken from a search index rather than Litquake's own schedule, so confirm the time.

**BRAINROT: a punk rock film fundraiser with live music** — The Knockout, San Francisco, 7pm. A screening with bands attached, raising money for the film itself. The Knockout does this better than rooms four times its size, mostly by not making it precious.

**Circus By The Bay: history panel and live acts** — Cowell Theater, San Francisco, 7pm, free, ticketed. A panel on Bay Area circus history with acts performing between the talking, in the hall at Fort Mason. Community-supported tickets are free; otherwise from $15. The kind of evening that exists nowhere else and gets announced nowhere.

**Militarie Gun with Softcult, Shady Nasty & DAZY** — The Chapel, San Francisco, 7pm, $20. Ian Shelton writes hardcore songs with power-pop choruses and refuses to pick a side, which is why this band outgrew the basements. Four acts for twenty dollars and DAZY alone is worth that.

**Peter Pan Goes Wrong** — San Francisco Playhouse, San Francisco, 7pm, $60-145. Mischief's disaster-comedy Peter Pan, in which a company of catastrophically bad actors attempt the play and the set fights back. Running to 28 November on two stages' worth of machinery. The Playhouse's own calendar and ticketing pages both returned server errors this run; these times come from the production's own page, which reads.

**Provoker with Topographies & Dalmatia** — Great American Music Hall, San Francisco, 7pm, $27.50-30. Bay Area darkwave with a trip-hop undertow — slow, humid, more Portishead than Sisters of Mercy. Topographies are local and genuinely good at the same trick. A hometown bill in a gilded hall that suits the mood.

**Comedy Allstars** — The Punch Line, San Francisco, 7:30pm. The club's standing midweek showcase. Reliable, chain-booked and ranked low by the brief for exactly that reason.

**Automatic with Donzii** — The Independent, San Francisco, 8pm. Bass, drums and a Juno, no guitar: the LA trio's minimal cold-wave has more in common with Suicide than with anything on a guitar-band bill. Sold out.

**Dani Offline: Lover's Discourse Live, with Amanda Magana** — Brick & Mortar Music Hall, San Francisco, 8pm, $25.50. Bedroom-pop built around spoken fragments and guitar loops, staged here as a themed show rather than a set list. Amanda Magana opens with something quieter and sadder.

**Isabel LaRosa** — Café du Nord, San Francisco, 8pm. Whispered, bass-heavy dark pop made with her brother in a bedroom and then streamed a billion times. A basement room is where this actually works.

**Office Killer (4K restoration)** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8pm. Cindy Sherman's only feature, a 1997 office-horror comedy that was dismissed and is now the most interesting thing a major photographer has done with a camera that moves. Restored.

**Wolves of Glendale with Cor64** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 8pm, $25-30. A comedy-adjacent trio who play pop-punk country straight enough that the jokes land harder. Cor64 does synthwave, which makes for an odd pairing and a well-sequenced night.

**Zolita with Earth to Emily** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 8pm, $26. Zoli Adams makes narrative queer pop and films it as serialised short cinema, so the live show is staged rather than played. The songs hold up without the videos.

**The House With Laughing Windows** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8:35pm. Pupi Avati's 1976 rural giallo, the one that swaps city glamour for a damp Emilian village and is genuinely frightening because of it. Part of Gialloween VI.

**Echos with Lana Del Rabies** — DNA Lounge, San Francisco. Echos make widescreen electronic pop; Lana Del Rabies makes industrial noise under a name that tells you the whole joke. DNA's own calendar would not open for this run, so this came off its two mirrors.

**Litquake: Out on the Table, writers on food** — Nosh Box, San Francisco. Garrett Schlichte hosts food writers reading work that is about appetite rather than restaurants. One of a dozen Litquake nights this week that could not be read in full — the festival's schedule host refuses this run's fetches.

**Litquake: Passages of Babel** — San Francisco Center for the Book, San Francisco. Translation and book arts together — letterpress work made from translated text, with the translators present. Servane Briand and Samuel Mignot. Time unconfirmed for the same reason as the other Litquake listings.

**Live Model Tuesday Sketch** — Madrone Art Bar, San Francisco, free. Bring a pad; a model sits, nobody teaches, the bar keeps serving. Weekly, free, and the oldest thing on Madrone's calendar.

**Loathe with Fleshwater & Prostitute** — The Masonic, San Francisco. Liverpool's Loathe put Deftones atmospherics on top of genuine metalcore violence and made the most-copied heavy record of the decade. Fleshwater is the Vein side project that goes full 1990s alt-rock. No start time on the route that reads.

**MICO with Will Linley** — The Regency Ballroom, San Francisco. Filipino-Australian singer-songwriter whose soft-grunge pop has outrun its TikTok origins. A 1,400-cap ballroom on a Tuesday is a statement of confidence. No published door time.

**Oh, Mary! (opening)** — Curran Theatre, San Francisco. Cole Escola's Mary Todd Lincoln farce opens a three-week A.C.T. run, and plays the Curran rather than either of A.C.T.'s own houses. A.C.T. publishes no curtain times anywhere on its site, which is its choice and not a fetch failure; the run is 13 October to 1 November.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Mezcal & Mole Comedy Night** — Mesa Maguey, Oakland, 7pm, free. Free with RSVP, Temescal, and the Tuesday lineup is usually the loosest of the week. Food and drink are extra and the mole is the reason to arrive early.

**Acoustic Alchemy** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7:30pm, $46-89. Forty years of twin-guitar instrumental jazz — one nylon-string, one steel — that sits exactly where smooth jazz and English folk overlap. An unfashionable band who have never played a sloppy set.

**Bumpin Uglies with Artikal Sound System** — Cornerstone, Berkeley, 8pm. Annapolis ska-punk that grew into American reggae without losing the shouting. Artikal Sound System are the stronger band of the two and go on first.

**Drive-in movie night** — Solano 2 Drive In, Concord, 8:30pm, $6. Six dollars an adult, two for a child, two screens, and one of the last operating drive-ins in the Bay Area. The double bills are first-run; the experience is the listing.

**Jon McLaughlin** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. Piano-driven singer-songwriter with a Broadway sideline, which is audible in how hard he leans on a seated room. He plays Menlo Park the following night; this is the better-sounding hall.
