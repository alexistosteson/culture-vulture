# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 24 August 2026

Monday 24 August – Sunday 30 August. 132 listings, ordered by remove from
Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**The event of the week is also the closest one to home.** The Roots, Nas and
De La Soul share a bill at Shoreline on Saturday — three foundational acts, with
the best live band in hip-hop backing the whole night, twenty minutes up
Amphitheatre Parkway. That almost never happens; the big things are usually the
far things. Take the bike path.

**Shoreline has three consecutive nights and they are the week's real centre of
gravity** — Train on Wednesday, Muse on Thursday, 5 Seconds of Summer on Friday,
then the Roots bill on Saturday. Four nights in a row at a 22,000-capacity
amphitheatre a mile from the origin. Plan traffic around it whether or not you
are going.

**Saturday is the crowded night by a distance** — 32 listings against 26 on
Friday — and it forces a genuine choice of bay. In the East Bay, Brad Mehldau
plays the Paramount and Tori Amos plays the Greek within two miles of each
other. In the city, the Castro throws a free block party in the afternoon and
both the de Young and the Legion of Honor are free to Bay Area residents all
day. Downtown Richmond closes six blocks of Macdonald Avenue for the 17th Spirit
& Soul Festival. Pick a side by lunchtime; the bridges will decide the rest.

**Sunday is unusually strong for a Sunday, and Golden Gate Park is most of it.**
Lindy in the Park at eleven, the Golden Gate Park Band at one, the roller disco
at one, and BonPOP's Bon Odori festival in the Celebration Garden from two — all
free, all within a few hundred metres, all on the same afternoon. Then Great
American Music Hall stages a seven-act Black Music Festival at four, and Diana
Ross plays a 2,500-seat winery in Saratoga. That is a better Sunday than most
weeks get on a Saturday.

**The best single ticket is fifty dollars for seven acts.** Great American's
Black Music Festival puts Miss Faye Carol, Tuck & Patti, Paula West, Rico Pabon,
Professor Kee, Ian Kelly and Tia Nomore on one stage — four generations of Bay
Area Black music, in the gilded balcony room, for the price of one club show.

**Monday is the quiet night — seven listings — and one of them is Bobby
McFerrin.** A 600-seat Berkeley listening room, ten Grammys, a voice used as an
entire rhythm section. He was here last Monday as well; that is the Freight's
doing, not a duplication.

**Jazz survives this week only because SFJAZZ presents elsewhere.** Miner
Auditorium is dark — Summer Sessions closed in mid-August and the fall season
does not start until Christian McBride on 10 September — so the organisation's
Brad Mehldau date lands at the Paramount in Oakland instead. Yoshi's carries the
rest.

**One thing in the outer region clears the bar, and it clears it easily.**
W.I.T.C.H. — the Zambian band who invented Zamrock, reconstituted around
surviving frontman Jagari Chanda — play Felton Music Hall on Friday. Nothing
else over a hill was worth the drive this week; details at the end.

**A conflict worth naming:** Sunday at seven, *Rear Window* in 4K at the Castro
against *Eyes Wide Shut* at BAMPFA. Both are the correct answer and you cannot
have both.

## Monday 24 August

*South Bay*

**Garden Bros Nuclear Circus** — West Wind Capitol Drive-In, San Jose, 4:30pm
and 7:30pm, from $18. A touring circus pitched in a drive-in lot, two shows a
day through Thursday. Listed for completeness rather than recommended: this
operator's reviews are mixed and nothing beyond the aggregator listing could be
verified. *Low confidence.*

*San Francisco*

**Built to Spill, PLAYDEAD** — The Chapel, 8pm, $44.50–50. Doug Martsch's long
guitar lines still unwind the way they did on *Perfect From Now On* — patient,
slightly out of tune, building to something. Two nights here, Monday and
Tuesday; the band also plays Santa Cruz on Wednesday.

**GLU** — Brick & Mortar Music Hall, 8pm, $22.94–32.21. Bedroom-scale electronic
pop with the seams left showing. A Monday booking at Brick & Mortar usually
means a band on the way up rather than one passing through.

**Death Guild** — DNA Lounge, 18+. The longest-running goth night in the United
States, six DJs across two rooms, still going every Monday. If you have never
been, this is the standing fixture the rest of the country's goth nights were
modelled on.

**American Doctor**, with filmmaker Poh Si Teng in person — Roxie Theater,
8:30pm. Documentary on a physician's working life, with the director present for
the Q&A. She is at the Roxie repeatedly this week — Monday, Tuesday, Friday and
Saturday.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Bobby McFerrin** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley, from $65. **Featured.** Ten
Grammys and a voice used as a whole rhythm section: overtone singing, body
percussion, improvised call-and-response with whoever is in the room. A 600-seat
listening room is an absurd place to catch him. The start time is not printed on
the mirror the Freight is reachable through — check before you go.

**Berkeley Mt Zion 80th Anniversary Celebration** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7:30pm,
$75. An East Bay Black church marking eighty years by taking over a jazz club
for the night. Gospel in a room built for close listening rather than a
sanctuary — a different thing entirely from hearing it on a Sunday.

## Tuesday 25 August

*South Bay*

**Sips & Sounds of Summer** — Santana Row Park, San Jose, 6–8pm, free. Free
Tuesday-evening music on the lawn near Left Bank, running through September. The
series is confirmed for August Tuesdays; this week's act was not published
anywhere, so treat the booking as unverified. *Low confidence.*

**San Jose Giants vs. Visalia Rawhide** — Excite Ballpark, 6:30pm, from $11.
Single-A ball in a 4,200-seat park where you can hear the infield chatter. First
of a four-game homestand.

*San Francisco*

**Black Marble, Serfs** — Rickshaw Stop, 8pm, $25–30. Chris Stewart's one-man
cold-wave project: cheap drum machines, buried vocals, melodies that sound like
they were recorded through a wall. Warmer live than on record. All ages.

**Brainstory, BOLERO!, Radio Veloso** — Bottom of the Hill, 8pm, $20 advance.
Rialto trio on Big Crown doing loose, unhurried soul with a jazz player's
harmony underneath — closer to Shuggie Otis than to the retro-soul revival. The
best-value bill of the night anywhere in the city.

**Death Lens, Heart to Gold, Grave Secrets, Garage Spiders** — Brick & Mortar,
8pm, $27.58. Four bands for under thirty dollars, LA garage-punk at the top.
Doors at seven, so this one ends at a reasonable hour.

**Molly Santana, NORTH WEST** — The Midway. Atlanta rapper working the woozy,
half-sung end of trap. The Midway is a warehouse in Dogpatch with real parking,
which is rare enough in this city to matter.

**SF StorySLAM: Serendipity** — Public Works. Open-draw storytelling: names into
a hat, five minutes each, no notes. Half the value is watching people fail
gracefully in front of a room.

**Civic Center Soundtrack** — Fulton Plaza, 12–3pm, free. Free lunchtime music
and food trucks on the plaza between the library and City Hall. The lineup is
announced late and was not published at compile time.

**People in Plazas: Jazz Concert** — 101 California Plaza, 12–1pm, free. A free
hour of jazz in a downtown privately-owned public space — the programme that
keeps those plazas legally public. Performer not named. *Low confidence.*

**Noe Valley Night Market** — Noe Valley Town Square, 5–8pm, free. Food and
maker stalls in the town square on a Tuesday evening: small, local, and over by
eight.

**Peter Relic with Jeff Chang: Bust a Move** — Book Passage, 5:30pm, free. Two
serious hip-hop writers in conversation; Chang wrote *Can't Stop Won't Stop*,
still the standard history. The Ferry Building store is small enough that
arriving late means standing.

**SF Politics 101: Wild History and How City Hall Works** — SPUR Urban Center,
6pm, free. SPUR explaining the machinery — supervisors, ballot measures, why
nothing gets built. More entertaining than it sounds, given the city's actual
history.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**David Byrne: Who Is the Sky Tour** — Greek Theatre, UC Berkeley, 8pm.
**Featured.** The *American Utopia* staging turned Byrne's band into a marching,
barefoot, wireless organism, and the current tour keeps that logic. Doors 6:30.
The Greek is a stone bowl on a hillside and it gets cold after dark regardless
of the afternoon.

**Keiko Matsui** — Yoshi's, Oakland, $44–85. Contemporary-jazz pianist with a
large international following and a lyrical, unhurried touch. Two nights,
Tuesday and Wednesday.

**HLLLYH, Dauber, Squishers** — Thee Stork Club, Oakland. The Mae Shi's noisier
descendant, in a 150-capacity Telegraph Avenue bar that books the shows nobody
aggregates. Transit is the sane way to downtown Oakland after dark.

## Wednesday 26 August

*South Bay*

**Train, Barenaked Ladies, Matt Nathanson** — Shoreline Amphitheatre, Mountain
View, 6:45pm, from $21. A three-act nostalgia package that will fill the lawn
cheaply. Bike the path from Mountain View rather than pay $40 to park; the
Shoreline lot exit after a sold-out show is its own event.

**Nonpoint, Spineshank, Drawing Heaven** — The Ritz, San Jose, 6:30pm, from $40.
Two survivors of the late-nineties nu-metal wave on a co-headline. Spineshank's
industrial edge has aged better than most of that scene.

*San Francisco*

**OSEES ft. Brigid Dawson, Traps PS** — The Chapel, 8:30pm, $40–44. John Dwyer's
band running four consecutive nights in a 500-cap room, with Brigid Dawson back
on the bill — her harmonies are the thing the later, heavier lineups lost.
Wednesday through Saturday; Friday adds Gumby's Junk. All ages.

**Courtney Barnett, Zoh Amba** — The Fillmore. Three nights, and the support is
the news: Zoh Amba is a free-jazz tenor player out of the Tennessee mountains,
which is not a pairing anyone would have predicted. Barnett's deadpan and her
guitar have both got heavier since *Tell Me How You Really Feel*.

**Los Cenzontles** — 4 Star Theater. San Pablo's forty-year-old Mexican
traditional-arts academy, playing son jarocho and norteño as living repertoire
rather than heritage display. They have recorded with Los Lobos, David Hidalgo
and Taj Mahal without ever leaving the East Bay.

**The Hot Takes, The DisRespectors, Neon Ships** — Bottom of the Hill, 8:30pm,
$13 advance. The cheapest ticket in the digest this week. Bottom of the Hill has
announced it closes at the end of 2026, so the arithmetic on nights like this is
finite now.

**Nox Novacula, The Discussion** — Kilowatt. Seattle darkwave in a 150-capacity
Mission bar. Rooms this size are where the genre actually works — the reverb has
nowhere to go.

**We Can Love It Together: Creativity Explored Short Film Fest** — Yerba Buena
Center for the Arts, 6–8pm, free. Two decades of films and animation made by
artists at Creativity Explored, the Mission studio for artists with
developmental disabilities. Wednesday is YBCA's free gallery day, so GaHee
Park's *Behind the Curtain* upstairs costs nothing either.

**Free Art Workshop: Watercolor Flower Paintings** — YBCA, 2–4pm, free with
registration. A collaborative still life in the Grand Lobby, materials provided.

**CatVideoFest 2026** — Roxie Theater, 6pm. Seventy minutes of curated cat
footage, proceeds to local animal welfare. Precisely as advertised, taken
entirely seriously by the audience. Also Saturday at 4:55.

**Almost Midnights: After Hours** — Roxie Theater, 9pm. Scorsese's one comedy,
and the most anxious film ever made about trying to get home from SoHo.

**San Francisco Time Machine with Peter Hartlaub** — Gray Area. The Chronicle's
culture critic digging through the paper's own archive — photographs, ephemera
and the city's forgotten embarrassments, presented live. Time not published.

**The Faight Community Art Jam** — The Faight, 6:30–9:30pm, free with RSVP. Open
studio night in the upstairs gallery room; bring your own project or use what is
there. Artist-run and unpolished in the way that makes these worth attending.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Winter Animals, Apprehenchmen, We Are Leaves** — Thee Stork Club, Oakland.
Three Bay bands on a midweek bill at Telegraph's smallest working stage. No
lineup information beyond the names — go for the room.

## Thursday 27 August

*South Bay*

**Muse, Portugal. The Man, The Temper Trap** — Shoreline Amphitheatre, Mountain
View. Maximalist stadium rock delivered without irony, plus a genuinely strong
pair of openers. The largest booking within twenty minutes of the origin all
week.

**Ledisi** — Mountain Winery, Saratoga. Oakland-raised, thirteen Grammy
nominations deep, and a voice built for an outdoor room this size. The hillside
gets cold once the sun drops behind the ridge.

**Mamamoo: 2026 US Tour** — SAP Center, San Jose. The K-pop group that actually
sings live, and has for a decade. An arena show, so listed rather than featured.

**Hermanos Espinoza** — San Jose Civic. Regional Mexican at the Civic — the
strand of South Bay programming that fills a 3,000-seat hall and rarely appears
in an English-language listings column.

**San Jose Giants vs. Visalia Rawhide** — Excite Ballpark, 6:30pm, from $11.
Third game of the homestand. Weeknight crowds are thin enough to move seats
after the second inning.

*Peninsula*

**Movies on the Square: free double feature** — Courthouse Square, Redwood City,
6–10pm, free. Family film at six, main feature at half past eight, on the square
outside the old courthouse. Twentieth year. Caltrain Redwood City is three
minutes' walk, which beats hunting the garage.

**Town of Colma Free Summer Concert Series** — Colma Community Center, 6–8pm,
free. On the grass behind the community centre, August Thursdays. BART Colma
genuinely beats driving from anywhere south. This week's band was not named in
any listing found. *Low confidence.*

*San Francisco*

**Son Rompe Pera, Inti Mystica** — The Independent, 8pm. **Featured.** Three
brothers from Naucalpan who inherited their father's marimba and pointed it at
punk: cumbia sonidera played with distortion pedals and a garage band's tempo.
One of the few acts that genuinely earns the word riotous. The best show in the
city on a crowded Thursday.

**Nerf Herder, Pulsars, Dog Party** — Bottom of the Hill, 8pm, $25 advance.
Santa Barbara nerd-punk lifers, best known for a theme tune they wrote in an
afternoon. Two nights with different support — Sacramento's Dog Party on
Thursday are the reason to take this one.

**OTHA** — Rickshaw Stop, 9pm, $20–25. Swedish pop with an unusually dry
production ear: sparse, rhythmic, closer to Robyn's colder records than to the
Scandinavian export sound.

**Strangepride, Big Afternoon, Collision Brothers** — Brick & Mortar, 8pm,
$22.94.

**How to Become a Silicon Valley Creep (In 10 Easy Steps)** — Swedish American
Hall, 7:30pm, 21+. Adrian Daub (Stanford, and the author of *What Tech Calls
Thinking*) with Moira Donegan, taking apart the valley's self-mythology in front
of an audience that mostly works in it.

**SOMA Nights Block Party and Night Market** — Folsom St, 6th to 12th, 5–9pm,
free. Six blocks closed for the fourth-Thursday street party: food, vendors,
live music and more than twenty businesses running their own activations. August
adds a plant swap and a petting zoo at Hallam. The best free thing midweek.

**Castro Magazine Gala** — Public Works.

**BYOB Pop-Up Projector Show** — Gray Area. Bring your own beamer; the room
becomes whatever the projections make it.

**R.O.I. (Return on Investment)** — San Francisco Playhouse, 7pm, from $41. New
work on the Playhouse's second stage, running to 5 September. The company runs
both its stages concurrently all summer, so there is theatre here on nights when
the bigger houses are dark.

**American Pachuco: The Legend of Luis Valdez** — Roxie Theater, 6:20pm.
Portrait of the founder of El Teatro Campesino, who built Chicano theatre out of
the Delano grape strike and then made *Zoot Suit* and *La Bamba*. Local history
in the strongest sense.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Annual Freight Fiddle Summit** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. The Freight's
yearly gathering of fiddlers across traditions — Irish, old-time, Cajun,
klezmer, whoever they have got — trading tunes on one stage. Lineup announced
late and not posted at compile time.

**Miko Marks residency, with Kiazi Malonga** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7:30pm, $25–49.
Marks left Nashville when country would not have her and rebuilt a career out of
Oakland; her records now do what the genre claims to. Kiazi Malonga is a
Congolese master drummer, which makes this the most interesting pairing on the
East Bay bill this week.

**Sugar Candy Mountain, Sandy's** — UC Botanical Garden, Berkeley. Oakland
dream-pop in a hillside botanical garden at golden hour. The setting does most
of the work; it is a canyon and it gets cold early.

**Very Paranoia, Stepmother, Replica Watch** — Thee Stork Club, Oakland. Local
post-punk in a room that holds a hundred and fifty people. Descriptors here are
inferred from the booking pattern, not from hearing the bands.

## Friday 28 August

*South Bay*

**5 Seconds of Summer, The Band Camino** — Shoreline Amphitheatre, Mountain
View. Second of three consecutive Shoreline nights. Expect the Amphitheatre
Parkway approach to be solid from six.

**Usher, Chris Brown, Mario, Eric Bellinger** — Levi's Stadium, Santa Clara.
Playing Friday and Saturday. Listed for completeness — findable without any help
from this digest, and the brief deprioritises stadiums for exactly that reason.

**San Jose Giants and post-game fireworks** — Excite Ballpark, 7pm, from $11;
fireworks free from outside. Friday fireworks with a musical theme, visible free
from the streets around the park. Parking at Excite is genuinely hard; VTA Race
St is an eight-minute walk.

**House Party** — The Ritz, San Jose, 9pm. A recurring club night with no DJs
named on the listing. Included because South Bay club programming is thin enough
to be worth knowing about, not because the booking is known. *Low confidence.*

*Peninsula*

**Bob Schneider, Matt Jaffe** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 8pm. Austin
songwriter with a twenty-year local monopoly on Monday nights at the Saxon Pub,
playing a 500-seat Peninsula room. Matt Jaffe is Bay Area and sharper than the
billing suggests. Caltrain Menlo Park is four minutes away.

**Music on the Square: Starman SF** — Courthouse Square, Redwood City, 6–8pm,
free. A Bowie tribute act closing out the twentieth season of free Friday
concerts. A tribute band, so never the headline recommendation — but it is free,
it is outdoors, and the square fills with families by half past five.

*San Francisco*

**The Greyboy Allstars** — Castro Theatre, 8pm. San Diego's boogaloo and
deep-funk revivalists, playing a movie palace rather than a club. Karl Denson on
saxophone is the engine.

**Sweet Pill, Deux Visages, Stereosity** — August Hall. Philadelphia emo with a
vocalist who can actually belt, which is rarer in the fifth wave than it should
be. Time and price were not posted.

**Nerf Herder, Ridel High, The Kegels** — Bottom of the Hill, 8pm, $25 advance.
Second Nerf Herder night with a different undercard. If you are choosing,
Thursday's bill is stronger; this one is for the Ridel High reunion.

**My New Band Believe** — Rickshaw Stop, 9pm, $20–25. No information available
beyond the booking; the descriptor would be the room's habit, not the band's.
*Low confidence.*

**Pokémon Rap Concert: Genwunner vs. Cam Steady** — Brick & Mortar, 9pm, $33.24,
18+. Two Pokémon rappers, timed precisely to the World Championships two miles
away at Moscone. Filed under oddities without apology.

**Öona Dahl & David Hohme** — Public Works. Deep, hypnotic, slightly psychedelic
house across a long set. Public Works runs two rooms; Casa Tulum is on the other
one the same night if you want something brighter.

**Feed Me** — The Midway. Jon Gooch's long-running electro-house alias, all
serrated basslines and cartoon menace. A big warehouse room suits it.

**Visitacion Valley Night Market and Block Party** — Leland Ave, Bayshore to
Peabody, 4–8:30pm, free. The neighbourhood's first-ever night market, run by All
My Usos with the Samoan Community Development Center and Pasifika By Design:
Pacific Islander food, music and street dancing. The T Third is a short walk
from Arleta or Sunnydale.

**Union Square Dances** — Union Square Park, 5–8pm, free. Live music and a
beginners' lesson, then open dancing; the style rotates weekly between tango,
salsa, swing, country-western and Bollywood. No partner or experience needed,
and nobody watching cares.

**San Francisco Critical Mass** — Embarcadero Plaza, 6pm, free. The last-Friday
mass ride that started here in 1992 and spread to sixty countries. Gathers at
Justin Herman Plaza, leaves around six. If you are driving downtown on Friday
evening, this is your street-closure warning.

**Pokémon XP: Fan Fest and World Championships** — Moscone Center, $90+ badge,
some activities free. The world finals, Friday to Sunday, with a free public
activity zone alongside the badged competition. Listed for the free half — the
brief excludes conferences except where there is open public programming, and
here there is.

**Shotz: Maxxing — seven world-premiere short plays** — Eclectic Box, 7–9pm, $5
pay-what-you-can. Seven brand-new short plays written to a monthly theme and
staged the same month. The failure rate is part of the format and so is the
occasional very good one.

**The Big Gay Sleepover** — Oasis, 10pm, $15. Drag and dancing at the city's
central queer cabaret, pitched as a slumber party. The room is small enough that
the performers are two feet away.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Bill Callahan, Wendy Eisenberg** — The UC Theatre, Berkeley, 8pm, $30 + fees —
**sold out**. The former Smog, singing in that low unhurried baritone about
dogs, rivers and marriage. Wendy Eisenberg's guitar playing goes somewhere else
entirely. Resale only, and be sceptical of any listing offering this one free.

**Richard Elliot** — Yoshi's, Oakland, $44–84. Tenor saxophonist out of Tower of
Power, now a fixture of the contemporary-jazz circuit. Know what you are buying:
this is the polished end of the form, played very well.

**Martyr Princess, DJ Goth Spice, Gremlita, LOBOTTOMY** — Thee Stork Club,
Oakland. Live acts and DJs sharing a Friday bill — the sort of mixed-format
night small rooms do well and larger ones cannot book. Take BART.

**Friday Nights at OMCA** — Oakland Museum of California, 5–9pm, free entry.
Block party in the garden: DJs, food trucks, half-price gallery admission and
Mildred Howard's *Poetics of Memory* in the Great Hall. BART Lake Merritt is two
minutes; downtown Oakland garages shut their gates at seven on weekdays.

**Elevator to the Gallows** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 7pm. Louis Malle's debut, with
the score Miles Davis improvised in one night watching the footage. From the
French Noir series. If you see one repertory screening this week, this is a
strong candidate.

**The Tale of the Princess Kaguya** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 4pm. Isao Takahata's
last film, drawn in charcoal and watercolour that appears to be dissolving as
you watch. Part of BAMPFA's Takahata retrospective.

*Worth the Drive*

**W.I.T.C.H.** — Felton Music Hall, Felton, 8pm. **Featured.** We Intend To
Cause Havoc: the Zambian band who invented Zamrock in the seventies, most of
them presumed lost, reconstituted around surviving frontman Jagari Chanda and
touring again. Fuzz-rock built on Zambian rhythm. The only thing over a hill
this week that justifies the drive, and it clears that bar easily.

## Saturday 29 August

*South Bay*

**The Roots, Nas, De La Soul** — Shoreline Amphitheatre, Mountain View.
**Featured.** Three foundational acts on one bill, with the best live band in
hip-hop backing the night. *Illmatic* and *3 Feet High and Rising* in the same
evening, twenty minutes from the origin. The event of the week, and it is the
closest thing on the list.

**Vanilla Ice, Rob Base, Young MC, C+C Music Factory** — Mountain Winery,
Saratoga. A late-eighties package tour on a hillside. Rob Base's *It Takes Two*
remains a genuinely great record; the rest is what it is. If you want hip-hop
this Saturday, Shoreline is the serious answer.

**El Tri** — San Jose Civic. Alex Lora's band have been the spine of Mexican
rock since 1968 — blues-based, unreconstructed, and still filling halls. Two
nights, Saturday and Sunday. A legacy act in the fullest sense.

**Sonu Nigam** — SAP Center, San Jose. One of the most recorded playback singers
in Hindi cinema, and a reliable arena draw in the South Bay. Deprioritised as an
arena show, but worth knowing it is on.

*Peninsula*

**Pub in the Park: giant inflatables and summer picnic** — Red Morton Park,
Redwood City, 11am–4pm, free. The city's end-of-summer park party: inflatables,
beer garden and food. Redwood City's third free public event of the week, which
is more than the whole Peninsula usually manages.

*San Francisco*

**Free Museum Day: de Young and Legion of Honor** — 9:30am–5:15pm, free to Bay
Area residents with ID. Both Fine Arts Museums free for the day. The single
highest-value free thing on the calendar this week, and it applies at Lincoln
Park as well as Golden Gate Park.

**Castro Carnival Block Party** — Noe St, Market to Beaver, 12–5pm, free. A free
block party of LGBTQ+ artists, live music and drag on a closed block of Noe.
Family-friendly and daylight-hours, which distinguishes it from most of what the
Castro throws. Muni Castro is a minute away and driving is pointless.

**Spirited Away, 25th anniversary** — Castro Theatre, 2pm. Miyazaki's bathhouse,
projected in a 1,400-seat picture palace with a Wurlitzer. The right way to see
it, and a rare matinee worth planning a Saturday around.

**Rocketman Sing-Along** — Castro Theatre, 8pm. Lyrics on screen and an audience
that does not need them. The Castro's sing-alongs are a genuine local
institution rather than a gimmick.

**Goodbye, Dragon Inn** — Yerba Buena Center for the Arts, 2pm, $15 including
gallery admission. Tsai Ming-liang's near-wordless elegy for a dying cinema.
Fifteen dollars covers the film, snacks and the galleries — the best-value
ticket of the weekend.

**Stanley Kwan's Center Stage, 4K restoration** — Roxie Theater, 3pm. Maggie
Cheung as the 1930s Shanghai film star Ruan Lingyu, in a biopic that keeps
stopping to interrogate its own project. One of the great performances, newly
restored.

**Scumdance Film Festival** — The Lost Church, from 2pm, $18. Nine hours of
underground and no-budget film in a fifty-seat storefront theatre. The name is
the mission statement.

**Union Square Free Outdoor Movie Night** — Union Square Plaza, 5–10pm, free. A
film projected in the plaza after dark. Downtown fog rolls in early even in
August — this is a blanket event, not a T-shirt one.

**Arnocorps, FleXx Bronco, The Memphis Murder Men** — Bottom of the Hill,
8:30pm, $20, 21+. An action-adventure hardcore band whose entire repertoire is
Schwarzenegger films performed in character and full costume. Absurd, committed,
extremely loud.

**Mini Trees** — Rickshaw Stop, 8:45pm, $20–22. Lexi Vega's project: quiet,
precise indie-pop written from the drum kit outward, which gives it an unusual
rhythmic spine for the genre.

**MarQ** — Brick & Mortar, 9pm, $35.72.

**Secret Psychedelica: Virgo World** — Public Works. A long night of psychedelic
electronic music with the lineup withheld until close to the door, which is the
party's whole conceit.

**Poetry in the Parks** — Crissy Field, 2–4:30pm, free with registration. Guided
writing on the shoreline with the bridge in view, run by the Parks Conservancy.
Also runs Sunday on landscape poetry.

**Mission Missives Zine Expo** — Youth Art Exchange LaFenix, 12–4pm, free. A
zine fair at the youth arts organisation's Mission space. Could not be verified
against Youth Art Exchange's own calendar — confirm before making the trip.
*Low confidence.*

**Ambrose Trataris: Daisy-Chain<><><>Star-Connection** — Gray Area. An
audiovisual performance at the Grand Theater, the only room in the city reliably
programming this kind of work. Neither time nor price was posted. *Low
confidence.*

**BATS Improv opens its 2026 run** — Fort Mason Center. The long-running improv
company beginning a run that carries through to December. Neither time nor price
was listed. *Low confidence.*

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Brad Mehldau: Ride Into the Sun** — Paramount Theatre, Oakland. **Featured.**
Mehldau's Elliott Smith project, reworking those songs through his own harmonic
language — closer to Schumann than to a covers set. SFJAZZ presents, which is
how there is any world-class jazz in the Bay this week at all with Miner
Auditorium dark. An art-deco palace is the right room for it.

**Tori Amos, Bartees Strange** — Greek Theatre, UC Berkeley. Thirty years on
from *Little Earthquakes* and still playing the Bösendorfer like an argument.
Bartees Strange is a genuinely interesting support choice rather than filler.
Second Greek show of the week; layers again.

**Bill Kirchen with Nina Gerber** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. The Telecaster
on *Hot Rod Lincoln*, and the man who more or less invented dieselbilly. Nina
Gerber is one of the finest accompanists in West Coast folk.

**Simon Phillips & Protocol 6** — Yoshi's, Oakland, $44–85. The drummer from
Toto and a hundred other sessions, leading his own instrumental fusion band. Two
nights. Technical to a fault, which is either the appeal or the objection.

**J. Cole** — Oakland Arena. Two nights, Saturday and Sunday. An arena booking,
so listed rather than featured — and it conflicts directly with the far better
hip-hop bill at Shoreline.

**In Shame, Dread Spire, Marrower, Rotterdam** — Thee Stork Club, Oakland. Four
heavy bands in a small room. This is the kind of bill Gilman and the Stork Club
keep alive in the East Bay when nobody else will book it.

**Tongues Untied** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 7pm. Marlon Riggs's 1989 essay film on
Black gay identity — poetry, documentary and confrontation at once, and still
the thing that got the NEA defunded.

**Arboretum Cycle — Nathaniel Dorsky & Jerome Hiler** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 3pm.
Silent 16mm from the two San Francisco filmmakers who have spent fifty years
making light and foliage into a devotional practice. There is no way to see this
outside a screening like this one.

**17th Annual Spirit & Soul Festival** — Macdonald Ave, Downtown Richmond,
12–5pm, free. Six blocks closed for live performance, a classic car show,
artisan stalls and a youth zone. Seventeen years old and free.

**Richmond Art Center 90th Birthday Party** — Richmond Art Center, 12–3pm, free.
Ninety years of one of the oldest community art centres in California, marked
with open galleries and free admission. A short hop from the Spirit & Soul
Festival on the same afternoon — do both.

**Crab Cove Fish Festival** — Crab Cove Visitor Center, Alameda, 10am–4pm, free.
East Bay Regional Parks doing what it does well: touch tanks, naturalists and
the bay shore. Genuinely good with children and not bad without them.

**Bon Marché 2nd Hand Market** — Abrams Claghorn Gallery, Albany, 11am–4pm,
free. Vintage and secondhand sellers in and around a Solano Avenue gallery.
Small, curated, and over by four.

## Sunday 30 August

*South Bay*

**Diana Ross** — Mountain Winery, Saratoga. **Featured.** Diana Ross on a
Saratoga hillside in front of 2,500 people. Whatever the voice does or does not
still do, the room is a tenth the size of where she would normally play, and

**El Tri** — San Jose Civic. Second night.

*San Francisco*

**Black Music Festival: Miss Faye Carol, Tuck & Patti, Paula West, Rico Pabon,
Professor Kee, Ian Kelly, Tia Nomore** — Great American Music Hall, 4pm, $45–50.
**Featured.** Seven acts spanning four generations of Bay Area Black music: Faye
Carol has been the Dean of it for fifty years, Paula West is the finest jazz
singer in the city, and Tia Nomore comes from a completely different Oakland.
Fifty dollars for all of it, in the gilded balcony room. The bill of the week.

**BonPOP 2026: 5th Anniversary Bon Odori Festival** — Celebration Garden, Golden
Gate Park, 2–6pm. Taiko, Bon Odori dancing, memorial lanterns, yukata dressing
and a vendor fair — a reimagined ancestral festival opened to anyone, whatever
their faith. **Free to SF residents with ID** and to under-4s, veterans and
Gardens members; $17 at the gate otherwise. State the mechanism, because it
catches people out.

**Golden Gate Park Band: Symphony in the Park** — Spreckels Temple of Music,
1–2:45pm, free. A free municipal band playing the Music Concourse bandshell
every Sunday since 1882 — the 144th season. Bring something to sit on; the
benches go early and the concourse is stone.

**Lindy in the Park** — JFK Drive, 11am–2pm, free. Free swing dancing on the
closed drive every Sunday since 1997, with a beginners' lesson at the start.

**Golden Gate Park Sunday Roller Disco** — Skatin' Place, 1–5pm, free. Forty
years of a sound system on a patch of asphalt off JFK and people in skates. No
skill floor, and one of the genuinely unrepeatable things about this city.

**Chinatown Free Sunday Family Block Party** — Grant Ave, Sacramento to Clay,
11:30am–2:30pm, free. Grant Avenue closed to traffic for the morning:
performances, games and food on the neighbourhood's main street. Muni 30 or 45
stops two minutes away; parking in Chinatown is a $10-an-hour garage and a
wasted half-hour.

**Cyberdelia: Hackers Screening and Cyberpunk Dance Party** — DNA Lounge, 18+.
The club is redressed as the nightclub from the 1995 film, the film is screened,
and then everyone dances to electro and big beat in costume. Exactly the sort of
thing DNA Lounge exists for.

**Raiders of the Lost Ark, 45th anniversary in 4K** — Castro Theatre, 2pm. Shot
in Marin, more or less. Forty-five years on and the boulder still works better
with 1,400 people reacting at once.

**Rear Window, 4K restoration** — Castro Theatre, 7pm. Hitchcock's single-set
masterpiece, restored. A double bill with *Raiders* earlier the same day makes
for a very good and slightly absurd Sunday at the Castro.

**Tom Kenny & the Hi Seas, Michael Michael Motorcycle, Cape Fury** — Bottom of
the Hill, 7:30pm, $20 advance. Yes, that Tom Kenny — the voice of SpongeBob
fronting a surf-rock band, which he has done seriously for years. Silly premise,
competent band, all ages.

**DITZ, Strange Cities, Rabbit** — Brick & Mortar, 8pm, $33.24. Brighton
noise-rock: abrasive, tightly wound, and considerably heavier live than the
records let on. A strong Sunday booking.

**Kasbo** — The Midway. Swedish producer working the melodic, wistful end of
festival electronic music. Big-room sound, small-hours emotions.

**Josh Johnson** — The Masonic. The Daily Show writer and stand-up who released
his specials free on YouTube, now filling a 3,300-seat hall off the back of it.
Not a chain comedy club — a genuine theatre booking.

**Thick & Tired drag show** — Madrone Art Bar, 6–8pm, free, $10 suggested. Free
early-evening drag in a Divisadero art bar. Done by eight, so it works as a
warm-up rather than a whole night.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Simon Phillips & Protocol 6** — Yoshi's, Oakland. Second night.

**J. Cole** — Oakland Arena. Second night.

**Eyes Wide Shut** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 7pm. Kubrick's last film, from the

**Brazilian Day White Party and Lavagem Festival** — La Peña Cultural Center,

**Cape & Cowl Comic Con** — Faction Brewing, Alameda, 11am–6pm, free. 150-plus

**Free Oakland Up Market** — Oakland, 12–4pm, free. A free community market —
everything given away rather than sold. The listing gives no street address, so
confirm the location before setting out. *Low confidence.*

**Wicked Messengers** — Ivy Room, Albany. A 150-capacity Solano Avenue bar with
a real booker. The name suggests a Dylan connection but nothing was verifiable;
the descriptor is a guess from the room's usual programming. *Low confidence.*
