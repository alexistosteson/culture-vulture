## Week at a Glance

**Fleet Week is the week.** It runs 4–12 October and it is free from end to end: ships
alongside at Piers 27 and 35 from Wednesday, the Parade of Ships under the Golden Gate at
eleven on Friday, Fleet Fest across Fisherman's Wharf and Pier 27 all weekend, and the
Blue Angels over the bay between the bridge and Alcatraz from noon to four on Friday,
Saturday and Sunday. The F-22 Raptor and F-35B demo teams fly either side of them. Forty
free listings in this digest are Fleet Week or adjacent to it, most of them service bands
playing neighbourhood squares — the Ferry Building, Jane Warner Plaza in the Castro,
Cathay Post in Chinatown, Patricia's Green, a branch library in Glen Park. **The local
move is Thursday:** the Blue Angels practise from one o'clock, the aircraft and the bay
are the same, and the crowd is a fraction of the weekend's.

**If you can only do one thing that is not an aeroplane, make it Friday at the Regency.**
Dead Kennedys with Flipper and the Avengers puts three bands from the 1977–81 San
Francisco scene on one stage in the city that made them, which has not happened in years.
Flipper invented a tempo nobody else wanted; Penelope Houston remains the best singer any
of these bands had. Jello Biafra has not been in the Kennedys since 1986 — go knowing
that, and it is still the most historically loaded ticket of the week.

**Friday is the most congested day at seventy-two listings, with Saturday at sixty-nine,
Thursday at fifty-nine and Sunday at fifty-seven.** The week totals 350 listings against 305
last week, which makes it the
largest this digest has covered. The drivers are a nine-day military festival, the opening
weekend of Litquake's 27th year, Mill Valley Film Festival at its midpoint, and a fall
theatre season now fully switched on: A.C.T., Berkeley Rep, SF Playhouse and Z Space all
mid-run, *Phantom* opening a twelve-day stand in San Jose and TheatreWorks' *Dracula*
opening in Mountain View.

**Saturday is a genuine collision and the powwow is the right side of it.** Berkeley's
34th Indigenous Peoples Day Powwow runs ten to six, free, in Martin Luther King Jr Civic
Center Park — the city passed the first Indigenous Peoples Day resolution in the United
States in 1992 and this gathering descends directly from that vote. It overlaps the air
show almost exactly. The jets fly again on Sunday; the powwow does not, and Downtown
Berkeley BART puts you two blocks from it.

**Eighty-five of the 350 listings cost nothing, and the best of them are not the air
show.** The Chinatown Night Market closes five blocks of Grant Avenue on Friday from five
to nine — dim sum, skewers, lion dancing, wine gardens, voted the best night market in the
country two years running, and the penultimate one of the season. Dance Day at Yerba Buena
Gardens on Sunday is five free hours of companies performing and teaching. Friday Nights
at OMCA has CHIKA DI on the Garden Stage with the galleries open, and only three of those
remain before the series stops for winter. Valencia Street closes to cars on Thursday for
the last Valencia Live of 2026. The Butchertown Jazz Festival gives the Bayview six free
hours of jazz on Sunday. Four museums are free on Tuesday, two more on Saturday, and YBCA
is free every Wednesday as always.

**My Morning Jacket are playing the Fillmore six times.** The 4th, 6th, 7th, 9th, 10th and
11th, rotating the setlist nightly in a 1,315-capacity room. A residency of that length by
a band who could fill three times the space is the most generous thing any touring act has
done here this year, and five of the six nights fall inside this window.

**The Peninsula has nine listings and two that matter.** Kronos Quartet at Bing on Sunday
is the one event south of the city that would be the headline anywhere — fifty-three years
of commissioning the string quartet repertoire that did not exist yet, by a San Francisco
institution, in the best-sounding hall on this side of the bay. Stanford's Frost
Amphitheater also carries two large outdoor bills, Friday and Saturday. Every summer
series is finished: Music on the Square, Movies on the Square, the Palo Alto Twilight
concerts and Colma's Thursday nights are all done for the year, and the Golden Gate Park
Band closed its 144th season on 27 September.

**The jazz of the week is a Sunday and the jazz of the week is cheap.** Divine Offering at
the Great American — Sita Michelle Coltrane leading a tribute to her grandmother Alice's
devotional harp-and-organ music, the Ashram tapes material rather than the hits — is the
rarest booking on the calendar at $39.50. Behind it: Ethan Iverson drawing a line from
Harlem stride to *A Love Supreme* in an Oakland piano showroom for thirty dollars on
Friday, and Emmet Cohen at the Freight on Sunday.

**Monday is the dead night, and Tuesday is thinner than it looks.** Nineteen listings on
Monday and thirty-two on Tuesday, of which the Tuesday count leans heavily on free museum
days. There is nothing in the Peninsula at all on Tuesday and nothing in the outer region
on either day except the Flatlanders. That is real: the week front-loads nothing and
everything arrives from Thursday.

## Monday 5 October

*19 listings.* The thinnest night of the week, and genuinely so rather than through lack of looking. **Best of the night:** The Flatlanders at HopMonk in Novato — Joe Ely, Jimmie Dale Gilmore and Butch Hancock tour as a unit roughly once a decade, and that is the whole argument for an hour's drive north on a Monday. Closer in, the free string quartet at the Conservatory's Sol Joseph hall is the best thing in the city that costs nothing, and Bobby McFerrin at the Freight is the best thing that does.

### South Bay

**Soulfly, Nailbomb & Incite** — The Ritz, San Jose, 7pm, from $73. Max Cavalera playing both of his bands in one night: Soulfly's tribal groove metal and Nailbomb, the industrial side project with Fudge Tunnel's Alex Newport that barely ever tours. The Nailbomb half is the reason to go.

### Peninsula

**Toadies with Local H** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 8pm. Two bands who each had exactly one enormous 1990s single and then spent thirty years being better than it. Toadies' Rubberneck still swings harder than its reputation; Local H are a two-piece who sound like four.

**Jon Guerra & John Mark Comer** — Fox Theatre, Redwood City. Hushed devotional songwriting paired with a spoken-word segment — closer to a listening service than a gig. Found in the ticketing sweep; the venue did not publish a start time.

### San Francisco

**Unfiltered: What happened to the American Dream?** — Commonwealth Club, San Francisco, 6pm, $15. Fifteen dollars including dinner, which is the real news. The Club's panel format is reliably better than its titles suggest.

**Free string quartet concert** — Sol Joseph Recital Hall, San Francisco, 7:30pm, free. Led by the SF Ballet Orchestra's concertmaster, in the Conservatory's recital hall, for nothing. Free chamber music of this standard is rare enough that it belongs near the top of a Monday.

**The Rat Utopia Experiment, False Flag & Fatale** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 7:30pm, $15-18. The club bills the headliner as circus punk, which undersells how grimy it gets; False Flag and Fatale are straight hardcore underneath. One of the last autumns in this room before it closes at the end of the year.

**EsDeeKid with Rico Ace** — The Masonic, San Francisco, 8pm, from $47. Liverpool rapper on the Council House Rat tour — UK drill rewired for a nasal, almost comic delivery that has made him the most argued-about British rapper of the year. A 3,300-seat room is a big swing for a first headline run here.

**Mountain Grass Unit** — Café du Nord, San Francisco, 8pm. Sold out. Alabama bluegrass played by people in their early twenties at a tempo that suggests they have somewhere to be. The basement room is the right size for it.

**She & Him with Jordana** — Castro Theatre, San Francisco, 8pm. Sold out. Zooey Deschanel and M. Ward doing Brill Building pastiche with real affection, which the picture palace suits better than a club would.

**Breadboarding modular synthesizers** — Gray Area, San Francisco. Hands-on workshop building oscillators from loose components rather than buying a module. The practical end of Gray Area's remit and the only thing in the region tonight that sends you home with a circuit.

**Derrick Gee's Radio Hour** — Gray Area, San Francisco. The Australian broadcaster who turned taste-explaining into a genre, doing it live. Gray Area's Grand Theater is the right room for an hour that is half lecture, half listening party.

**Lee DeWyze with August Lee Stevens** — The Chapel, San Francisco. The ticketing sweep puts this here tonight; the Chapel's own calendar shows nothing on the 5th. One of the two is wrong — check before you go. *Low confidence — verify before you go.*

**Nick Hakim with Arima Ederra** — August Hall, San Francisco. Smeared, narcotic soul in the lineage of Shuggie Otis more than of anyone currently working — guitar played like a rumour. August Hall publishes no door times, so confirm before you plan the evening around it.

**Psychotic Reaction & Bongfather** — The Knockout, San Francisco. Garage-punk and stoner riffs in the Mission's smallest serious stage. Lineup detail came from the ticketing sweep only, so treat the billing as provisional. *Low confidence — verify before you go.*

### Berkeley & Oakland

**American Pachuco: The Legend of Luis Valdez** — Berkeley Public Library, Berkeley, 5:30pm, free. Free screening on the founder of El Teatro Campesino, who built a theatre out of the Delano grape strike and then wrote Zoot Suit. The library programmes this kind of thing better than anyone charges for.

**@ and YHWH Nailgun** — Cornerstone, Berkeley. YHWH Nailgun make the most physically disorienting rhythm music of the last two years - drums played like a malfunction, vocals somewhere between yelp and siren. From the ticketing sweep; Cornerstone's own calendar did not list tonight.

**Bobby McFerrin** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. Four octaves and no instrument. McFerrin at the Freight is a seated listening-room proposition, not a greatest-hits set, and he improvises most of it on the night. Sourced from the JamBase mirror because thefreight.org blocks automated reads.

**Euge Groove** — Yoshi's, Oakland, $44-84. First of two nights. Smooth jazz played straight by a saxophonist who did his time in Tower of Power's horn section, so the funk underneath is real.

### Worth the Drive

**The Flatlanders with Jaimee Harris** — HopMonk Tavern, Novato. Joe Ely, Jimmie Dale Gilmore and Butch Hancock have been the best songwriting trio in Texas since 1972 and tour as a unit perhaps once a decade. That is the whole argument for driving to Novato on a Monday.

**Sufi Sessions San Francisco** — Public Works, San Francisco. Sufi music reframed for a club, which is either a continuation of the tradition or a departure from it depending on who you ask. The second Sufi-adjacent booking in the city tonight, which is its own kind of coincidence.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**All Italian Day car and motorcycle show** — Alameda, Alameda, 10am, $15. Fifteen dollars a head, twenty-five for a family: Alfas, Lancias, Moto Guzzis and Fiat 500s on an Alameda street, on the same day as the Italian Heritage parade across the bay. Enthusiast-run and all the better for it.

**Free Oakland Up market** — Free Oakland Up, Oakland, noon, free. Free: a giveaway market rather than a sale — goods redistributed at no cost. Mutual aid organised as a flea, which is a genuinely different thing from a flea.

**Jean-Luc Godard's early short films** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 1pm. MVFF at BAMPFA: the pre-Breathless shorts, which are rarely programmed and show him working out in public what he would spend sixty years refining.

**The Cook (closing)** — Berkeley Rep, Berkeley, 2pm. Final performance, with a post-show discussion. Five weeks of one of the better scripts the Rep has staged recently, closing quietly on a Sunday afternoon.

**A Girl Unknown** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 3pm. MVFF's Sunday afternoon slot in Berkeley.

**The Dreamed Adventure** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 6:30pm. Last of the week's six MVFF screenings at BAMPFA, and the last chance to do the festival without driving to Marin.

**626 Night Market** — Alameda County Fairgrounds, Pleasanton, $8. Final night of three. Sunday closes earlier than Friday and Saturday — check before making the drive out to Pleasanton.

**Emmet Cohen** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. The best young stride and bebop pianist working, who built his audience on a weekly livestream from his Harlem apartment and plays like someone who learned from the records rather than the conservatory.

**Musiq Soulchild** — Yoshi's, Oakland, $79-145. Third of eight nights. The Sunday show in a long residency is usually the loosest.

**TLC with Salt-N-Pepa & En Vogue** — Toyota Pavilion at Concord, Concord. Three acts who between them sold something like sixty million records in the 1990s, outdoors in Concord. En Vogue are the best singers on the bill and go on first. BART Concord plus the shuttle is twenty minutes and spares you the lot.

### Worth the Drive

**Welcome Back Monarch Butterfly Festival** — Natural Bridges State Beach, Santa Cruz, 11am, free. Free admission, $10 to park: the annual welcome for the overwintering monarchs in the eucalyptus grove above the beach, with guided walks into the roost. One of the last reliable monarch groves in California, which is both the appeal and the sad part, and it clears the drive on strangeness alone.

## What could not be reached

A skip is not a pass, and a venue that looks empty is more often a fetch problem than a
quiet week. This week's failures:

- **San Francisco Symphony — resolved against this digest, see the correction at the top.**
  sfsymphony.org sits behind a queueing redirect and cannot be read on any path. Two
  sources that can be read disagreed, the generic ticketing sweep was wrong, and two
  listings were published in error before being removed on 7 October. The lesson is
  recorded here rather than buried: **a venue-scoped source beats a generic sweep on the
  question of whether an event exists at all**, because a sweep can date-shift a closed run
  into the live window and nothing about the result looks wrong. Davies is dark 5–11
  October. No Symphony listing should be published from a sweep alone again.
- **DNA Lounge.** The October monthly calendar page returned HTTP 503 on three separate
  attempts, as it did last week. The single DNA listing here came from the ticketing sweep
  and there are certainly others missing.

  returned exactly one concert for the window — Yellowjackets with Kurt Elling on Friday -
  which for a fall-season week is implausibly thin. Assume SFJAZZ has more on than this
  digest shows. The same problem was reported last week; see the proposal below.
- **A.C.T.** *North by Northwest* is mid-run to 18 October but the theatre publishes no
  per-date curtain times on its listings page, so all five A.C.T. listings carry no time
  and medium confidence. Same for **San Francisco Playhouse**, where the house schedule
