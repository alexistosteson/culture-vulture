# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 7 September 2026

Monday 7 September – Sunday 13 September. 189 listings, ordered by remove from
Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**San Francisco Opera gives away the best thing on this list.** Opera in the
Park lands on Sunday at 1.30pm in Robin Williams Meadow — Eun Sun Kim, the full
orchestra, and the principals from the season that opened days ago, doing two
hours of arias and duets on the grass. Fifty-first year, no ticket, no lottery,
first come and sit where you like. There is a hockey arena in San Jose charging
serious money for Andrea Bocelli the same evening; the comparison is not close.

**Saturday is the crowded day by a distance — 49 listings against 35 on
Friday — and Mountain View wins it.** The 54th Art & Wine Festival closes a mile
of Castro Street for two days: 400 artists, two stages, nineteen food vendors,
free, and roughly a thousand metres from where this digest measures everything
from. Take Caltrain or VTA to the Transit Center. Parking is not a plan.

**The week's spine is riot grrrl, thirty-five years late and mostly sold out.**
Bikini Kill play the Warfield on Tuesday with Alice Bag — who fronted The Bags
in 1977 Los Angeles and predates the whole scene — and Bratmobile take Bottom of
the Hill on Friday and Saturday. Both Bratmobile nights are gone. That room
closes for good at the end of 2026, which gives a sold-out sign there a weight
it did not use to have. If you can only get into one of the three, the Warfield
is the one still selling.

**SFJAZZ comes back on Thursday and takes four nights to do it.** Christian
McBride opens the fall season with a Thursday-to-Sunday stand in Miner
Auditorium, and he is a bandleader who changes format night to night, so the
four programmes will not be the same show. Set times are not published anywhere
fetchable — sfjazz.org blocks us and the JamBase mirror carries dates only — so
ring the box office before you plan an evening around it.

**Friday is the free night, and Oakland has it.** Friday Nights at OMCA runs
five to nine with Duniya Dance and Drum Company and Bongo Sidibe's djembe on the
garden stage, galleries open, no admission. Across the bay the same evening,
Grant Avenue closes for the Chinatown Night Market and Union Square has its
weekly free dance — country western this week, taught by Sundance Saloon. Three
good free evenings in three places, and the bridges will decide which one you
get.

**Two Sundays' worth of programming collide inside Golden Gate Park.** The
municipal band plays Spreckels at 1pm; the opera starts at 1.30pm nine minutes
away; Lindy in the Park runs 11 to 2 on JFK Drive; Flower Piano has twelve
pianos going in the Botanical Garden from ten; and the Inner Sunset Flea is two
blocks off the eastern edge. The park will be full. Lindy finishing as the opera
begins is the one clean double-header.

**Flower Piano is the week's best answer to "not today, then."** Eleven days,
twelve pianos, 130 scheduled performances and open play on all of them between
sets, running to 20 September — free for San Francisco residents with ID.
Opening night on Thursday is a $75 ticket; every other day is ordinary garden
admission. Saturday and Sunday will be the two worst days to go.

**Monday is the dead night — eleven listings, and Labor Day accounts for most of
them.** The oddest of those is genuine: six men with reindeer antlers, a hobby
horse and a boy with a bow performing the Abbots Bromley Horn Dance, a ritual
documented since 1226, in a redwood grove in Joaquin Miller Park, for nothing.

**Two named tributes to Dolly Parton, two nights apart, sharing a title.**
The Castro has *The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas* on Thursday; the Chapel has
*The Best Little Whorehouse in San Francisco* on Saturday, hosted by Vanilla
Meringue. Different shows, different rooms. Buy carefully.

**The Peninsula is genuinely thin — ten listings — and the reason is seasonal,
not a research failure.** Music on the Square finished its twentieth season on
4 September; Movies on the Square and the Palo Alto Twilight Concert Series both
ended in August. The Peninsula's whole free-summer-evening apparatus shut down
the week before this one. What is left is good but small: Fiestas Patrias free
in Courthouse Square on Sunday, Kepler's block party and an evening street
closure in Menlo Park on Saturday, and the 2,500-piece 50|50 Show opening in
Pacifica.

**Nothing in the outer region clears the bar, and all three venues were
reached.** The Catalyst has a $10 Y2K dance party and a PawPaw Rod atrium show —
and PawPaw Rod plays The Independent on Tuesday, forty miles closer. Sweetwater
has an open mic and a folk singer. Felton Music Hall's calendar path 404s but
its events page reads clean and shows nothing in the window. Santa Cruz, Marin
and the North Bay are empty this week by `coverage.outer_threshold`, not by
omission.

## Monday 7 September


*Peninsula*

**Kings Mountain Art Fair (final day)** — Kings Mountain Community Center, Woodside, 10am, free. Sixty-third year, and the setting is the point: 125 juried makers under second-growth redwoods on Skyline, run by the volunteer fire brigade. Labor Day is the last of three days. Skyline is a slow, beautiful drive and the lot fills by eleven.


*San Francisco*

**Giro di San Francisco, 50th running** — Levi's Plaza, San Francisco, 8am, free. A half-century-old criterium on a tight circuit under Telegraph Hill — riders come past every ninety seconds or so, all day, and you can stand a foot from the barrier for nothing. The best free spectating in the city and almost nobody goes.

**Japanese Tea Garden free admission hour** — Japanese Tea Garden, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, 9am, free. Standing arrangement, not a one-off: the first hour is free every Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and $12–$16 otherwise. Listed once here rather than three times. The garden is genuinely empty at nine.

**Golden Gate Park Band: Labor Day Symphony in the Park** — Spreckels Temple of Music, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, 1pm, free. The 144th season of a municipal band that has played this bandshell since 1882. Marches, light classics and the occasional show tune, played straight. Bring a coat — the Music Concourse holds fog well past one.

**Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6:15pm. Roxie Staff Picks. A slasher-comedy that plays the summer-camp form for parody rather than pastiche. Descriptor is second-hand — the Roxie listing carries no synopsis and I have not seen it.

**Mad Caddies, Diesel Boy, Stay Out** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 8pm, $27 adv / $30 door. Doors 7:30. Santa Barbara ska-punk with a dixieland streak — horns played for swing rather than volume, which is what separated them from the 1997 crowd. Diesel Boy are the Bay's own pop-punk lifers.

**The Charlatans UK** — Castro Theatre, San Francisco, 8pm. Doors at seven. Thirty-six years of Hammond-organ Manchester indie, and one of the few bands from that scene whose late records are worth hearing. A rock show under the Castro's ceiling is a strange, good pairing.

**Death Guild** — DNA Lounge, San Francisco, 9:30pm. The world's longest-running weekly goth-industrial night, every Monday since 1993, six DJs across two rooms. LOW CONFIDENCE on date and time: dnalounge.com returned 503 on three attempts today and this is reconstructed from a search snippet. Verify before going.


*Berkeley & Oakland*

**$5 Day at the Lawrence Hall of Science** — Lawrence Hall of Science, Berkeley, 10am, $5. Admission drops from about $30 to five dollars. Worth it for the terrace alone, which has the best free-standing view of the bay in the East Bay hills, plus a working seismograph and the whale sculpture every Berkeley child has climbed.

**Abbots Bromley Horn Dance & Picnic** — Joaquin Miller Park, Oakland, 11am, free. Six dancers carrying reindeer antlers, a hobby horse, a fool and a boy with a bow, performing an English ritual dance documented since 1226 — in a redwood grove above Oakland, on a Monday, for free. The oddest thing on this week's calendar by a wide margin.

**Aimee Nolte** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7:30pm, $30–$59. Nolte built an audience by explaining harmony on YouTube, which undersells the playing — a warm, unhurried pianist and singer in the Blossom Dearie line, more interested in voicings than in fireworks. Good Monday-sized music.


## Tuesday 8 September


*South Bay*

**Sips & Sounds of Summer** — Santana Row Park, San Jose, 6pm, free. Free Tuesday-evening sets on the lawn by Left Bank, covers and local soul-funk bands. LOW CONFIDENCE: the series is billed as August–September but Santana Row publishes no dated September lineup, so tonight's booking is unverified and the season may already have closed.

**Beauty and the Beast** — San Jose Center for the Performing Arts, San Jose, 7:30pm, $77–$219. The touring Broadway production, opening a six-day run. Large, loud, competent and expensive; it is here because the South Bay gets few book musicals, not because it will surprise anyone. Cheapest seats are Tuesday and Wednesday.


*San Francisco*

**San Francisco Botanical Garden free day** — San Francisco Botanical Garden, San Francisco, 7:30am, free. Second Tuesday of the month, free to everyone rather than to residents only. Fifty-five acres and the cloud forest collection, two days before Flower Piano turns the whole garden into a concert.

**Civic Center Soundtrack** — Fulton Plaza, San Francisco, 12pm, free. Free lunchtime sets and food trucks on the plaza between the library and City Hall. Rotating local bookings, usually soul, Latin or brass. Also runs Thursday this week.

**Lunch Rhyme: Radio XII & Darius Rashad Parker** — Mechanics Monument Plaza, San Francisco, 12pm, free. An hour of live poetry and music on the Market Street plaza, part of the downtown-activation programming that has quietly become the best reason to be in the FiDi at noon.

**American Doctor** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Co-presented by the Arab Film & Media Institute and the Jewish Film Institute, which is itself the news — those two organisations sharing a bill in 2026 is not a small thing. Content unverified beyond the co-presentation.

**Filipiñana** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. FiliKula series. A golf-course tee girl in Manila, class observed at ground level. Descriptor is lower-confidence — the Roxie listing gives no synopsis and I am going on the title and the series framing.

**History of Chileans in SF: Gold Rush, Poetry and Wine** — Museum of San Francisco, San Francisco, 6:40pm, free. The Chilean arrival of 1849 is one of the least-told chapters of the Gold Rush — thousands came, were run out of the diggings, and settled the slope that became Little Chile. Donations requested, not required.

**Scott Ostler: The Sportswriting Life** — KALW, San Francisco, 7:30pm, free, ticketed. Forty-odd years of Chronicle columns, and the rare sportswriter people read for the sentences. Free with RSVP; the KALW studio audience is small enough that it fills.

**Bikini Kill with Alice Bag** — The Warfield, San Francisco, 8pm. **Featured.** The band that gave riot grrrl its grammar, still playing the songs at the tempo they were written. The support is the real coup: Alice Bag fronted The Bags in 1977 Los Angeles and predates everyone on this bill by fifteen years. Go early.

**PawPaw Rod with femdot** — The Independent, San Francisco, 8pm. Picture Day Tour. Loose, sung-rapped soul with a bedroom-recording softness that gets bigger live than the records suggest. femdot is a Chicago writer's rapper — dense verses, no hooks to speak of.

**The Bug Club with Lunchbox, Preschool** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 8pm, $20. Doors seven. Welsh trio playing terse, deadpan garage-pop — two-minute songs, no solos, closer to early Feelies than to anything current. Lunchbox are Oakland's long-serving bubblegum-fuzz act and a genuinely good pairing.

**Union County, with post-film panel** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8:05pm. Presented by Shatterproof, the addiction-policy nonprofit, with a panel afterward — so a documentary screening in the advocacy mode rather than a repertory one. Worth knowing which you are buying a ticket to.

**The Hole (new 35mm print)** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8:15pm. Tsai Ming-liang's millennial plague musical, in which a leaking pipe between two apartments is the entire plot and Grace Chang numbers erupt out of nowhere. A freshly struck print of a film almost never screened. The pick of a four-film night.

**Slayyyter with Pearly Drops** — The Regency Ballroom, San Francisco. Wor$t Girl in the World tour. Maximalist hyperpop out of the SoundCloud lineage, all sugar and distortion. She plays Oakland's Fox the following night and that one is sold out, so this is the easier ticket for the same show.


*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Matt Schofield Trio** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 8pm, $29–$59. English blues guitarist working the organ-trio format — no bass, Hammond pedals holding the bottom, which puts every mistake in the open. Cleaner and jazzier than the British-blues label suggests.

**Debashish Bhattacharya and George Brooks** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. Bhattacharya plays Hindustani classical on a slide guitar of his own design; Brooks is the saxophonist who has spent thirty years finding the seam between raga and jazz without flattening either. A listening room, seated, and the right one for this. Start time not printed on the mirror.


## Wednesday 9 September


*South Bay*

**Hail The Sun, A Lot Like Birds, Blight Town, Murals** — The Ritz, San Jose, 6pm. Sacramento post-hardcore with a drumming vocalist and time signatures that change more than they need to. A Lot Like Birds share half a lineage with them. Four bands from six o'clock — an early night by Ritz standards.

**Beauty and the Beast** — San Jose Center for the Performing Arts, San Jose, 7:30pm, $93–$233. Night two of six. Midweek pricing is the lowest of the run; the weekend matinees are more than triple.


*San Francisco*

**GaHee Park: Behind the Curtain** — Yerba Buena Center for the Arts, San Francisco, 11am, free. Domestic interiors that go wrong slowly — flattened perspective, too many limbs, a cat watching. On view into January, but Wednesday is the free day, which is the reason to file it here rather than as a standing exhibition.

**Jazz on the Plaz(a): Lyons, Cubanos and Yumberos** — Mechanics Monument Plaza, San Francisco, 12pm, free. Two free hours of Afro-Cuban jazz on Market Street at lunchtime. The Yumberos work the rumba side rather than the salsa side, which means percussion breaks long enough to actually listen to.

**Free Art Workshop: Surreal Portrait Collage** — Yerba Buena Center for the Arts, San Francisco, 2pm, free, ticketed. Free with registration, in the Grand Lobby, and Wednesday is also YBCA's free gallery day — so the GaHee Park show upstairs costs nothing today either. Two hours of scissors and glue for adults, which is rarer than it should be.

**12th Annual Chinatown Night Out** — Willie "Woo Woo" Wong Playground, San Francisco, 4pm, free. Community night on the basketball court at Sacramento Street — food, booths, performances, and the neighbourhood organisations that never advertise anywhere else. Two days before the Grant Avenue night market, and a different, smaller thing.

**Rising Tides closing party** — 111 Minna Gallery, San Francisco, 5pm, free. Last night of the show, doubling as the Sketch Wednesday fundraiser. 111 Minna has been running the same free open-bar-adjacent gallery-party format since the first dot-com boom and it still works.

**Mapping Genocide, with the American Indian Cultural District** — San Francisco Main Library, Koret Auditorium, San Francisco, 5:30pm, free. A presentation on documenting the California killings of 1846–1873 as geography rather than as narrative. Heavy, specific, and the sort of programming the Main Library does well and publicises badly.

**Freeway (Arthouse 50)** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Matthew Bright's 1996 Little Red Riding Hood as exploitation satire, with Reese Witherspoon and Kiefer Sutherland. Deeply unpleasant, extremely well made, and almost never on a screen. The Arthouse 50 series has been quietly excellent.

**Mary Oliver: Saved by the Beauty of the World** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Documentary portrait of the poet, who spent most of her life refusing interviews — which makes the film either a real feat of access or largely archival. Unverified which.

**Storied: SF Season 9 + Intersections SF launch** — Madrone Art Bar, San Francisco, 6pm, free. Launch night for a long-running local oral-history project. Free, in a bar that is itself a small museum of Divisadero — the walls change every few weeks and nobody charges you to look.

**Kronos Quartet Creative Lab** — 836M, San Francisco, 6:30pm. Kronos in workshop mode in a small Montgomery Street gallery rather than on a concert stage — closer to watching the sausage made than to a recital. Fifty-plus years of commissioning new string-quartet writing behind it.

**Legend: Honoring Tim Curry** — Castro Theatre, San Francisco, 7:30pm. Doors 6:30. A tribute evening in the house that has shown Rocky Horror more times than anywhere in the country. Format not specified on the listing — assume clips and conversation rather than a straight screening.

**Blood Orange** — The Warfield, San Francisco, 8pm. Essex Honey tour, first of three consecutive nights — a booking that says the room could have been sold three times over. Dev Hynes writes soft, guitar-led soul that sounds slight on record and is anything but with a band behind it.

**Eihwar with Frayle** — Great American Music Hall, San Francisco, 8pm, $16–$45. Doors seven. French duo doing 'Viking war trance' — frame drums and throat singing over hard electronic bottom end, which is sillier on paper than in a room. Frayle are Cleveland doom with a whispered vocal; the pairing is smarter than the genres suggest.

**KELS with Ella Maddux** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 8pm, $26–$30. Doors seven. Descriptor is lower-confidence — neither act has much of a footprint beyond streaming, and the Rickshaw booking is the only real signal. The room's Wednesday bookings are usually early-career pop with a following.

**Kevin Atwater with Ruby Plume** — The Independent, San Francisco, 8pm. Blush Red Tour. Confessional guitar songs with a high, unguarded voice — the internet-folk register, done with better lyrics than most of it. A quiet show in a room that can be talked over; stand near the front.

**The Cycle of Love, with Orlando von Einsiedel in person** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8:30pm. Von Einsiedel made Virunga and won an Oscar for The White Helmets; he is a documentarian who puts himself where the story is rather than in front of it. Director in the room is the reason to pick this over the six o'clock show.

**The Kofy Brown Band, Skip The Needle, Black Gold Sun** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 8:30pm, $15. Fifteen dollars for three Bay Area bands led by Black women playing loud rock and soul — Skip The Needle in particular are a covers-and-originals band who treat Bad Brains and Nina Simone as the same tradition. The best-value bill of the week.

**Weezer with The Shins and Silversun Pickups** — Chase Center, San Francisco. An arena package of three 1990s-2000s alternative bands. Listed for completeness — chasecenter.com returned no readable calendar and this is off a ticketing aggregator, so confirm the date. You will find this without our help.


*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Exhibition tour: Maren Hassinger, Living Moving Growing** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 12:15pm. Included with admission. Hassinger has spent fifty years making sculpture out of wire rope, branches and plastic bags and calling it landscape. A guided lunchtime walk-through is the right way in — the work rewards being told what it is doing.

**Liz Phair & Sleater-Kinney with Soccer Mommy** — Greek Theatre, UC Berkeley, Berkeley, 6pm. The Flannel and the Fury. Doors five, music at six — early because the Greek has a curfew, so Soccer Mommy is on before most people have parked. Two co-headliners who came out of the same decade from opposite ends of it. Bring a layer; the Greek gets cold fast after sundown.

**Push Buttons and AI (Alternative Visions)** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 7pm. The Alternative Visions series is BAMPFA's experimental-film slot and has been running for decades. A programme on machine image-making, which is the least surprising subject for avant-garde cinema in 2026 and probably the most urgent.

**Slayyyter with Pearly Drops** — Fox Theater, Oakland, 8pm. Sold out. Doors seven. Same tour as last night's Regency show, so if you missed this one the city date is still open — an unusual bit of luck, since it normally runs the other way.

**Lisa Fischer & Orrin Evans Trio** — Yoshi's, Oakland, $54–$99. Twenty years singing behind the Rolling Stones before Twenty Feet from Stardom put her name on a marquee, and she has spent the decade since proving she was always the better musician. Evans is a hard, unsentimental pianist — no cabaret in this. Set time not listed.

**Scott Amendola** — Ivy Room, Albany. Amendola is the drummer half of most interesting Bay Area improvised music of the last thirty years — Nels Cline, Charlie Hunter, his own electronics-and-loops trios. A 150-cap bar in Albany is an absurdly good place to hear him. Lineup unannounced.


## Thursday 10 September


*South Bay*

**Free drive-in movie night** — West Wind Capitol Drive-In, San Jose, 6pm, free. The South Bay half of the same free night — films, music, bounce houses and no admission. Gates six, films eight. Capitol Expressway fills faster than Concord does.

**Beauty and the Beast** — San Jose Center for the Performing Arts, San Jose, 7:30pm, $85–$219. Night three of six.

**Soda Stereo** — SAP Center, San Jose. The Argentine band that made Latin American rock a continental form rather than a translation. Gustavo Cerati died in 2014, so this is the surviving members with guests — an arena show, deprioritised here, but a genuine event for anyone raised on Canción Animal.


*San Francisco*

**Chess on the Plaza** — Mechanics Monument Plaza, San Francisco, 12pm, free. Boards set out on Market Street at lunchtime, free, no club membership and no rating required. The Mechanics' Institute — the oldest chess club in the country — is a hundred yards away, which explains the standard of play.

**Civic Center Soundtrack** — Fulton Plaza, San Francisco, 12pm, free. Second of the week's two free lunchtime sets on Fulton Plaza. Lineup announced late, which is the series' one weakness.

**Flower Piano opening night** — San Francisco Botanical Garden, San Francisco, 4pm, $75. Eleventh year. Twelve pianos placed through fifty-five acres for eleven days, 130 scheduled performances and open play on all of them between sets. Opening night is the $75 ticket; from tomorrow it is ordinary garden admission, free for city residents.

**Fantastic Planet (Animation Obsession)** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 4:10pm. René Laloux's 1973 cut-out animation about giant blue Draags keeping humans as pets — a Cannes jury prize and still the strangest-looking feature animation ever widely released. A 4:10pm start is an odd ask on a Thursday, but this is the film to leave work early for.

**Valencia Live night market** — Valencia St, 18th to 21st, San Francisco, 5pm, free. Monthly street closure with live music, vendors and the restaurants spilling out. Five hours of Valencia with no cars on it, which is the whole appeal. BART to 16th and walk two blocks — there is no parking and there never was.

**Punk Rock at 50: exhibition opening, with NO MORE and Jack Boulware** — Goethe-Institut San Francisco, San Francisco, 6pm, free. German punk's fiftieth, opened with the band NO MORE in conversation with Jack Boulware, who wrote the oral history of San Francisco punk. Free, and the sort of cross-Atlantic framing nobody else here programmes.

**Free Film Crawl on Mission Street** — Four venues near Mission & 29th, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Short films across four storefronts and sidewalks in three hours, walk between them at your own pace, no ticket. There is a second crawl on Cortland tomorrow; this is the larger of the two.

**The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas: a Dolly Parton tribute** — Castro Theatre, San Francisco, 7:30pm. Doors 6:30. Note the collision: a drag Dolly tribute at the Chapel on Saturday borrows this exact title with 'San Francisco' swapped in. Different show, different room, two nights apart. This one is the film-anchored one.

**Your Arms Are My Cocoon, awakebutstillinbed, and more** — Great American Music Hall, San Francisco, 7:30pm, $15–$17. Doors 6:30. Four bands of the fifth-wave emo revival, all lower-case names and long ones, for fifteen dollars in a gilded 1907 ballroom. awakebutstillinbed are from San Jose and are the best thing on the bill.

**Pink Breath of Heaven, Topographies, Christina's Trip** — The Chapel, San Francisco, 8pm, $22–$26. Doors seven. Topographies are the draw — a local band doing cold, reverbed post-punk that owes more to Felt and the Chameleons than to the current revival. The Chapel's high wooden ceiling suits this music better than any club in the city.

**Fartbarf, Diesel Dudes, Edgar** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 8:30pm, $15 adv / $20 door. Three men in rubber caveman masks playing analogue synthesisers and drums very fast. The joke would have worn out in 2009; instead they got good at it. Fifteen dollars, doors eight, and the last ordinary night before the Bratmobile weekend.

**CHANPAN with SHEBAD** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 9pm, $22–$25. Doors eight. Descriptor is lower-confidence; both acts sit in the Rickshaw's late-Thursday slot, which is reliably club-leaning and reliably loud. Nine o'clock start means the room fills late.

**Rules** — The Independent, San Francisco, 9pm. A nine o'clock single-band billing with no support listed. LOW CONFIDENCE on who this is — the name is too generic to verify and the venue page carries no description. The Independent's Thursday bookings are usually touring indie with a modest following.

**African Stew, by Lisa B. Thompson** — Magic Theatre, Fort Mason Center, San Francisco. Lorraine Hansberry Theatre in the Magic's house, opening tonight and running to 27 September. LHT producing on a larger stage than its own is worth noting on its own; Thompson writes talky, funny family plays. Curtain times not published on the Fort Mason calendar.

**Christian McBride** — Miner Auditorium, SFJAZZ, San Francisco. **Featured.** Four nights, Thursday to Sunday, opening SFJAZZ's fall season after the Summer Sessions gap. McBride is the most-recorded bassist of his generation and a bandleader who changes format nightly — expect the four programmes to differ. Set times are not on the JamBase mirror; sfjazz.org blocks fetching, so ring the box office.


*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Free drive-in movie night** — Solano 2 Drive In, Concord, 6pm, free. A whole evening of films for nothing at one of the last drive-ins standing in the Bay Area. Gates at six, first feature at dark. The lot fills on free nights — arrive with an hour to spare or watch from the back row.

**On Call, with Alice Diop in person** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 7pm. Sold out. Diop's documentary from a Seine-Saint-Denis medical practice, made before Saint Omer made her name. She is in the room, which is why the tickets went. She returns Sunday for Danton's Death, and that one is still available.

**Dominique Fils-Aimé** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7:30pm, $40–$79. Montreal singer whose trilogy of albums traced blues to jazz to soul deliberately, one record per step. Layered, largely a cappella arrangements with minimal band — closer to a choral piece than a jazz set.

**Feefawfum with Grex, Naytronix** — Thee Stork Club, Oakland. Grex are one of the East Bay's most durable art-rock duos and Naytronix is Nate Brenner of tUnE-yArDs playing woozy solo bass-pop. A 150-cap room, three acts deep into the local experimental scene, and nothing else on this bill is findable elsewhere.


## Friday 11 September


*South Bay*

**Powerman 5000, Makes My Blood Dance, Ikki Crane** — The Ritz, San Jose, 7pm. Industrial metal from the late-1990s soundtrack era, still touring and still committed to the sci-fi costuming. A 500-cap room is a much better place to hear this than the sheds it was built for.

**Beauty and the Beast** — San Jose Center for the Performing Arts, San Jose, 7:30pm, $95–$249. Night four of six, and the price steps up for the weekend.

**Codiciado (two shows)** — San Jose Civic, San Jose, 8pm. Two sittings, eight and ten. Corridos tumbados — the requinto-and-tuba style that took over regional Mexican music this decade, sung in a lower, more conversational register than the norteño it displaced. Booking a 3,000-cap room twice in a night is the story here.

**Jesse McCartney** — Mountain Winery, Saratoga. Early-2000s teen pop, twenty years on, in a stone winery ruin above Saratoga. The setting does the heavy lifting; the show is nostalgia and knows it. Hillside shuttle from the lot, $20 parking, and it gets cold after dark.


*Peninsula*

**Marcus Rezak's Shred Is Dead** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 8pm. Doors seven. Grateful Dead material rearranged by a guitarist out of the jam-band circuit — an improvising band playing a repertoire, which is either the point or the problem depending on your tolerance. Caltrain Menlo Park is four minutes away.

**18th Annual 50|50 Show benefit preview** — Sanchez Art Center, Pacifica, $39.39. Fifty artists, fifty small works each, made in fifty consecutive days — 2,500 pieces on the walls at once, and the wall of them is the experience. Tonight and tomorrow are the ticketed benefit; from Sunday it is free. Pacifica is not in the region lists and is filed to Peninsula by proximity.

**Twanguero with Diego Garcia** — University Lutheran Church, Palo Alto. Diego García Blanco plays guitar instrumentals that sit between Spanish flamenco phrasing and Texas twang, and a church nave is the right room for it. Sourced from a listings feed rather than a venue calendar — confirm the time.


*San Francisco*

**Chinatown Night Market** — Grant Ave, California to Pacific, San Francisco, 5pm, free. Second Friday, May to October. Grant Avenue closed for four hours — lion dancing, Jest Jammin' playing covers, craft tables, and food that is better than the street-fair average because the restaurants are already there. Park nowhere; take the 30 or the 45.
