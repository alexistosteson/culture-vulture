# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 14 September 2026

Monday 14 September – Sunday 20 September. 199 listings, ordered by remove from
Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**San Francisco Opera is on strike, and the only grand opera in the region is in
San Jose.** The orchestra walked out minutes before the curtain on the 104th
season opener, Verdi's *Simon Boccanegra*, and Opera in the Park went with it.
Management's opening position was a 26% pay cut, since softened to 20%, which
would return musicians to what they earned in 2018. Nothing further has been
cancelled but nothing has been guaranteed either. Meanwhile Opera San José's
*Don Giovanni* opens Friday in the California Theatre, a restored 1927 movie
palace and the most beautiful room in the South Bay. A month ago that would have
been a regional footnote. This week it is the thing itself.

**Sunday is the heaviest day of the year so far and four free festivals are
fighting over it.** The Haight Ashbury Street Fair takes six blocks from 11 to
5:30 in its 47th year; the Chinatown Autumn Moon Festival closes eight blocks of
Grant for its second and busier day; Comedy Day puts fifty comedians in Robin
Williams Meadow for five hours; and Fiesta on the Hill has Cortland Avenue in
Bernal. All free, all overlapping almost exactly. Pick one. Attempting a circuit
means spending the afternoon on the 33 and seeing none of them properly. If you
want the least crowded good thing, it is the LGBTQ+ big band playing the AIDS
Memorial Grove at noon — a full horn section in a sunken redwood dell, and
nobody there.

**Saturday is the busiest by listing count (52) and the jazz is the reason.**
Ravi Coltrane marks his father's centenary at the UC Theatre — SFJAZZ presenting
it in Berkeley rather than in its own hall, which tells you something — and it
is sold out. Dianne Reeves plays SFJAZZ with Branford Marsalis on Friday and
Saturday both, billed as a partnership rather than a singer with a band. Kamasi
Washington closes a two-night run at Yoshi's on Monday for $74. Three of the
best living jazz musicians in three rooms in five days.

**The best single booking of the week is a 92-year-old in a Berkeley listening
room.** Alice Gerrard plays the Freight & Salvage on Friday with Justin Robinson
of the Carolina Chocolate Drops. Hazel & Alice cut their first record for
Folkways in 1965 and are the reason there were women singing lead in bluegrass
at all. There is no guarantee of another chance at this.

**Bottom of the Hill has seven shows in seven nights and closes at the end of
the year.** The Spits on Monday and Tuesday, a thirteen-dollar record release
Wednesday, Unsane Thursday, a Steel Pole Bath Tub reunion Friday, a sold-out
Spiritual Cramp Saturday and a Sunday matinee. The Friday and Saturday bills in
particular read like a scene saying goodbye to its own room. If you have been
meaning to go back, the calendar has stopped being patient.

**Free lands hard this week: 62 of the 199 listings cost nothing.** Beyond
Sunday's festivals — Flower Piano's last days in the Botanical Garden, free to
SF residents with ID; El Grito in Civic Center Plaza on Tuesday evening;
Friday Nights at OMCA with Cal Raijin Taiko; Muni Heritage Weekend running
1930s streetcars for free on both weekend days; the Peninsula's three free
Saturday festivals in Half Moon Bay, South San Francisco and Pacifica; and
Berkeley Jam, a free juggling festival in Willard Park that also runs a homemade
jam tasting contest, because of the name.

**South Bay's weekend is Bark in the Park, and it is not a joke listing.**
Thirtieth year, roughly 5,000 people and 2,000 dogs in William Street Park,
free with a $10 suggested donation, Saturday 10 to 5. The costume contest is the
main stage event. It is the largest thing happening inside ten miles of this
digest's starting point.

**Monday and Tuesday are the thin nights — thirteen listings each — and they are
still not empty.** Kamasi Washington on Monday and Squeeze with Adam Ant on
Tuesday would each anchor a quieter week.

**Nothing cleared the bar for Worth the Drive, and the list is empty on
purpose.** The Growlers played Santa Cruz on Thursday and then the Castro on
Friday and Saturday; Vieux Farka Touré plays Felton on Sunday and Berkeley on
Saturday; Shawn Mullins plays Novato on Sunday and Menlo Park on Saturday.
Everything worth the drive this week is also playing somewhere closer. Kaki King
at Sweetwater on Friday was the closest call and does not clear a week
containing Patti Smith, Dianne Reeves, Ravi Coltrane and Alice Gerrard.

## Monday 14 September

*13 listings. The quietest night of the week, with one genuinely large exception.*

### South Bay

**OMEGA X** — Montgomery Theater, San Jose. A K-pop group who left their
original label in a public labour dispute and rebuilt as an independent act; the
underdog story is most of the draw. A 500-seat civic theatre is an unusually
small room for this. *Descriptor and time are lower-confidence — found on a
ticketing feed only.*

### San Francisco

**The Spits, The Fleshies, The Pranks** — Bottom of the Hill, 8pm doors / 9pm
music, $30. Night one of two. Seattle-via-Michigan cretin punk: two chords,
cheap keyboards, masks. They have made the same record for twenty-five years and
it keeps working.

**The Tear Garden, Anthony Xavier Jackson** — The Independent, 8pm. Edward
Ka-Spel of the Legendary Pink Dots and cEvin Key of Skinny Puppy, a forty-year
side project that has outlasted most people's main ones. Slow, narcotic
psychedelia with industrial bones, and they tour rarely enough that this is an
event.

**Letty Lynton** — Roxie Theater, 6pm. Wild, Weird, Wicked: Films from Before
the Code. The 1932 Joan Crawford melodrama that lost a plagiarism suit and was
pulled from circulation for most of a century. You effectively cannot stream it,
which is the whole point of a repertory house. *Freeway* follows at 8:10 in the
Arthouse 50 strand — Matthew Bright's 1996 Red Riding Hood with Reese
Witherspoon, tonally deranged, and it took twenty years to be taken seriously.

**Haute & Freddy** — Castro Theatre, 7pm doors. Sold out, moved here from
Bimbo's. *Low confidence: the box office lists it without a genre line and I
could not find a reliable description of the act.*

**Death Guild** — DNA Lounge, 9:30pm, 18+. The longest continuously running
goth-industrial night in the United States, every Monday since 1993. Listed once
for the window rather than weekly.

**Motown on Mondays** — Madrone Art Bar, 7pm, free before 8 and $5 after. All-45s
soul in a small Divisadero room. Also a standing weekly; also listed once.

**Cyberpunk Cinema Night** — The Knockout, 6:30pm, free. Free projection night in
a Mission dive, programmed around neon and bad futures. Never reaches a listings
platform, which is exactly why a Monday is not automatically dead.

**Japanese Tea Garden free admission hour** — Golden Gate Park, 9–10am, free.
Standing arrangement: free the first hour every Monday, Wednesday and Friday,
$12–$16 otherwise. Genuinely empty at nine.

**Beyond the Bridge: film and conversation on homelessness** — downtown San
Francisco, 5pm, free with RSVP. *Low confidence: the aggregator did not name the
venue. RSVP and take the address from the confirmation.*

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Kamasi Washington** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7:30 and 9:30pm, $74–$99. **Featured.**
Closing night of a two-night run and the week's single best argument for leaving
the house. Washington plays big-band spiritual jazz in the Pharoah Sanders line —
choir, two drummers, horns stacked like weather. Yoshi's holds 400, a quarter of
the room he normally fills.

**Alphabet Soup** — Ivy Room, Albany. Long-running Bay Area live hip-hop band:
freestyle over a real rhythm section rather than a laptop. A 150-cap room on San
Pablo, and a Monday booking like this is the Ivy Room's whole character.

## Tuesday 15 September

*13 listings. Two big rooms, and the city's Mexican Independence eve.*

### San Francisco

**Gregory Alan Isakov with the San Francisco Symphony** — Davies Symphony Hall,
7:30pm. A one-off: Isakov's spare Colorado folk songs given full symphonic
arrangements. Songs this quiet usually lose in a 2,700-seat hall, so the
orchestration is the experiment. Expensive on resale. *Davies sits behind a
ticketing queue and could not be polled directly; date confirmed from three
independent sellers.*

**Squeeze, Adam Ant, Haircut 100** — The Warfield, 7pm. Three 1979–82 British
singles bands on one bill. Squeeze are the reason to come — Difford and Tilbrook
wrote some of the sharpest pop lyrics of the era and still sing them in key.
Starts early.

**El Grito de Independencia** — Civic Center Plaza, 4–9pm, free. The cry of
independence called at the hour it is called in Mexico City, with mariachi,
ballet folklórico and food stalls beforehand. Free, outdoors, and the most
genuinely civic thing happening this week. The plaza funnels wind — dress for it.

**Jeff Bernat: Luv Language Tour** — Castro Theatre, 7pm doors. Smooth bedroom
R&B that found its audience through streaming rather than radio, from a singer
with a much bigger following in Seoul and Manila than in the city he is playing.

**Starbomb with Epic Rap Battles of History** — The Regency Ballroom, 7pm doors /
8pm. Video-game parody rap from the Game Grumps orbit. Not a music
recommendation so much as a large, affectionate internet reunion. All ages.

**Magic Sword, Skylimit** — Kilowatt, 6pm doors / 7pm. **Moved.** This was billed
at The Chapel and is now at Kilowatt, a much smaller Mission bar. Masked
synthwave with a comic-book mythology attached. Check your ticket says Kilowatt.

**The Spits, The Fleshies, Garras Sucias** — Bottom of the Hill, 8pm doors, $30.
Night two, different support. If you are choosing one night, take tonight — the
Oakland opener is the better of the two undercards.

**Civic Center Soundtrack** — Fulton Plaza, 12–3pm, free. Lunchtime bands and
food trucks on the plaza between the library and City Hall. Runs Tuesdays and
Thursdays; listed once for the window.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Johnny Blue Skies & The Dark Clouds** — Greek Theatre, UC Berkeley, 5:30pm
doors / 7pm. Sturgill Simpson under the name he now records as, outdoors in the
Berkeley hills. **Sold out.** Early because the Greek has a hard curfew, and it
gets cold up there regardless of the afternoon.

**John Pizzarelli: Dear Mr. Bennett** — Yoshi's, Oakland, $35–$74. Two nights,
tonight and Wednesday. A seven-string guitarist and crooner doing a Tony Bennett
tribute — swing played straight, no irony, considerable technique.

**The Obsessed** — Ivy Room, Albany. Wino's band, one of the foundational
American doom acts, in a 150-capacity room. Slow, loud, blues-derived riffs
played by people who were doing this before the genre had a name. Absurdly small
room for it.

**No Right, withpaperwings and more** — 924 Gilman, Berkeley, 6:30pm, $5. Five
dollars, all ages, volunteer-run, no bar. Over early enough to get back across
the bridge.

**$6 Tuesday at the Solano Drive-In** — Solano 2 Drive In, Concord, 8:30pm, $6
adults / $2 children. One of the last operating drive-ins in the region. Sound
is over FM — bring a car with a working radio and something warm.

## Wednesday 16 September

*17 listings. A strong film night and the week's cheapest good show.*

### South Bay

**Five Finger Death Punch, Cody Jinks, Eva Under Fire** — Shoreline
Amphitheatre, Mountain View, 6:45pm, from $21. Mainstream metal with an
outlaw-country singer bolted on, which tells you where both audiences now
overlap. Cheap on the lawn. Shoreline parking is $40; the bike path from central
Mountain View is genuinely faster.

**Santana Row Farmers' Market** — San Jose, 4–8pm, free. Twenty-five growers and
makers, every Wednesday through the end of September, so this is one of the
last. Note that the Tuesday *Sips & Sounds* concert series that ran here through
August appears to have finished — I could find no September dates.

### Peninsula

**The Infamous Stringdusters, Silas Herman** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park,
6:30pm doors / 7:30pm. Grammy-winning progressive bluegrass: traditional
instrumentation pushed into jam lengths and jazz harmony. Four minutes' walk
from Caltrain Menlo Park, which beats parking on Santa Cruz Ave.

**Curating America's 250th** — Los Altos History Museum, 5:30–7pm, free (or $15
suggested). Museum professionals on how you mount a semiquincentennial
exhibition without producing either pageantry or a lecture. A small, serious
evening in a town that rarely gets one.

### San Francisco

**The American Astronaut (35mm)** — Roxie Theater, 6pm. Twenty-fifth
anniversary, on actual 35mm. Cory McAbee's black-and-white space-western musical,
made for almost nothing and unlike anything before or since. Prints barely
exist. The screening of the week for anyone who cares about format.

**Chelsea Wolfe, Jonathan Hultén** — Curran Theatre, 8:30pm. THE DARK World
Tour. Gothic folk that keeps drifting into metal weight — close-miked voice over
sub-bass and distortion. Hultén, formerly of Tribulation, opens solo and
acoustic. A seated Broadway house is an odd and probably excellent fit.

**Unity record release, Quaaludes, Yeoubi** — Bottom of the Hill, 8pm doors /
8:30, $13 advance / $15 door. A record release on a Wednesday for thirteen
dollars with two of the better Bay Area punk bands under it. The cheapest good
show of the week by a distance.

**When Chai Met Toast** — Great American Music Hall, 7pm doors / 8pm. Kerala
four-piece singing in Malayalam, Hindi and English over banjo and folk-pop
arrangements — cheerful, unfashionable, very good at it. **Sold out.**

**ALI, The Helltones** — Rickshaw Stop, 7pm doors / 8pm, $20–$25.

**Zee Machine, boy bowser** — Café du Nord, 8pm. Synth-pop with a queer
confessional streak, in the basement under the Swedish American Hall.

**American Doctor** — Roxie Theater, 6:15pm. Co-presented by the Arab Film &
Media Institute and the Jewish Film Institute — two organisations that do not
routinely share a bill, which is itself the news.

**Art Battle San Francisco** — Public Works. Competitive live painting: twelve
artists, twenty minutes a round, the audience votes. Absurd as a format and
consistently entertaining as an evening.

**People in Plazas: Emily Day Swingtet** — 425 Market Street plaza, 12–1pm, free.
Small-group swing in a downtown plaza at lunchtime; the series has been quietly
doing this since the 1970s. A soul and funk set runs the same hour at 555
California if you would rather have horns.

**Inside the 2026 SF Ballot: live Q&A with journalists** — Koret Auditorium, SF
Main Library, 6–8pm, free. Local reporters taking questions six weeks out from
the election, and considerably more useful than the voter guide.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Old Blind Dogs** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. Scottish trad, thirty-five
years in: fiddle and pipes over a percussion approach borrowed from West Africa,
which is less of a gimmick than it sounds. A seated listening room, not a club.

**Barbara Forever** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 7pm. Alternative Visions. Brydie
O'Connor's Teddy Award winner, which also plays the Roxie on Friday with the
director present — if you want the Q&A, take the Friday.

**Christian Molenaar, Adam Lion, Mike Meanstreetz, Kevin Murray** — FML Space,
Oakland. Four-set noise and free-improv bill in a warehouse space. *Time and
door price unpublished; check the space's socials on the day.*

## Thursday 17 September

*28 listings. Patti Smith, and an unusually good free evening in San Francisco.*

### South Bay

**The Temptations and The Four Tops** — Mountain Winery, Saratoga, 7:30pm, from
$35. Two Motown institutions on a hillside at sunset. Neither has an original
lead singer left, which is the honest caveat — but the arrangements and the
harmony discipline are the real inheritance and both groups keep them. $20
parking with a hillside shuttle.

**Constitution Day community conversation** — Mountain View, 6:30pm, free with
RSVP. *Low confidence: the room was not named. Most likely the library or the
community center; take the address from your RSVP confirmation.*

### Peninsula

**Phantom Planet, Arkells** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 7pm doors / 8pm.
Yes, that Phantom Planet, but the band reformed and the recent records are
better than the one song everybody remembers. Arkells are a big Canadian rock
act playing a 500-seat room, which is the anomaly worth noticing.

**Beats, Brews & Vines** — Downtown Millbrae, Broadway at Hillcrest, 6–8pm, free.
Third Thursday of the month, August to October, so this is the middle one.
Broadway closes, a band plays, beer and wine for sale. BART and Caltrain both
stop at Millbrae.

**Mark Karan's Budz** — Club Fox, Redwood City, 7:30pm doors / 8pm. Karan played
guitar in Bob Weir's RatDog and The Other Ones for years; this is his own band
in the same Dead-adjacent territory. Three minutes from Caltrain.

**Casey Gerald: The Great Refusal** — Kepler's Books, Menlo Park. His first
book refused every redemption arc available to it; the new one argues for
refusal as a political position. Kepler's events sell out at this scale.

### San Francisco

**Patti Smith: Songs & Stories** — Sydney Goldstein Theater, 7:30pm. **Featured.**
City Arts & Lectures, with Tony Shanahan and her son Jackson Smith playing. She
is here for the new memoir, *Bread of Angels*, but a Patti Smith reading has
never stayed a reading — it turns into a set. Some tickets include the book.

**American Aquarium, Buffalo Nichols** — Rickshaw Stop, 7pm doors / 8pm, $23–$27.
The support is the story: Buffalo Nichols plays spare, modern country blues that
refuses the genre's usual nostalgia. Best pound-for-pound bill of the night.

**Unsane, CNTS, Facet** — Bottom of the Hill, 8pm doors / 8:30, $20 advance.
New York noise rock at its most punishing — detuned, metallic, built on Chris
Spencer's shout. Thirty-five years and no softening. Earplugs are not a
suggestion.

**Mustard Service, Pity Party (Girls Club), Manwolves, Chicano Mosh** — The
Independent, 8pm. Miami indie with a bossa-nova lilt on a four-band bill that
leans Latinx across the board. The undercard is the interesting half.

**Cantonese Opera Festival with costume try-ons** — Chinatown, 10am–4pm, free
with RSVP. Performances plus the chance to be dressed in full Cantonese opera
costume and painted — the try-on is the part people queue for. An art form with
a deep local history and a shrinking number of public showings.

**NightLife at the California Academy of Sciences** — 6–10pm, $26 ($5 with EBT),
21+. Rainforest dome, aquarium and planetarium with a bar and DJs, minus the
school groups. Last entry 9pm — people miss the planetarium by arriving at 8:45.

**Fort Mason Art Walk, with Leah Rosenberg's *The Sea Is But Another Sky*** —
Fort Mason Center, free. The autumn walk across the campus galleries, timed to
the unveiling of a new Rosenberg wall work — she paints in stacked bands of
colour and the pier setting gives her a horizon to argue with. Haines Gallery's
*250 Years, Indigenous Futures* is also newly up.

**Big Art Loop: Waterfront Walkabout** — Pier 14 to the Exploratorium, 5–8pm,
free. A guided stroll of the Embarcadero's public art. Two miles, flat, and the
light on the bay at six is the actual argument.

**Opening night: Protest Photography Through the Decades** — SF Main Library,
6–7:45pm, free. Drawn from the library's own holdings, which are stronger on Bay
Area protest than any museum collection in the city.

**Yoon Chung Han: *Dreaming Babies: illi*** — Gray Area, free. Opens today, runs
to 22 October. Data-driven installation work in the Grand Theater — the one
venue in the city that reliably shows audiovisual and machine-made art as art
rather than as demo.

**ZYZZYVA Movie Night with Ingrid Rojas Contreras: *Songs of Earth*** — Roxie
Theater, 6pm, co-presented with Litquake.

**CritterFest film festival** — 4-Star Theater, 7pm, $15 ($5 with EBT/SNAP/WIC).
A new festival of short films about pets and animals in the Richmond's restored
4-Star. Exactly the sort of cheerfully narrow programming a neighbourhood cinema
should be doing.

**Out of Character: readings and an art talk** — Minnesota Street Project, 7pm,
free. A dozen galleries under one roof — come early and walk the building first.

**Echo by RAWdance** — 836M, 6:30–8:30pm. Contemporary dance in a small
Montgomery Street art space; the work is built around duets and close contact,
which suits a room this size. *Price not published.*

**Dutch Melrose, Benny Mayne, Pretty Havoc** — Brick & Mortar Music Hall, 7pm
doors / 8pm, $33.

**Boys Go To Jupiter, Abby Kenna** — Café du Nord, 8pm. Upstairs the same night,
Big Brain Lectures takes over the Swedish American Hall — different door.

**The Presidio's 250th birthday** — Presidio Chapel, 3pm, free with RSVP. Two
hundred and fifty years since the Spanish garrison was founded on this ground in
1776 — older than the United States by two months.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Bleachers, The Linda Lindas** — Greek Theatre, UC Berkeley, 5pm doors /
6:30pm. Jack Antonoff's band, with the far more interesting opener: The Linda
Lindas are several years past the viral library video and have turned into a
genuinely good punk band. Layers.

**The Bald and the Beautiful LIVE** — Paramount Theatre, Oakland. Trixie Mattel
and Katya taking their podcast into a 3,000-seat Art Deco cathedral; the 1931
interior is worth the ticket on its own. Downtown Oakland garages mostly close
their gates at 7pm — take BART to 19th St.

**Tony Lindsay, Nona Brown, Bryan Dyer** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. Lindsay
sang lead with Santana for two decades and holds several Grammys for it; here he
fronts a straight soul revue with two strong Bay Area vocalists beside him.

**OakTown Thursdays** — Latham Square, Oakland, 5–9pm, free. Vendors, DJs and
food on the pedestrianised block at Telegraph and Broadway. Downtown Oakland's
evening foot traffic has been the city's hard problem for years and this is the
deliberate answer. BART 12th St, one minute.

**Scissoring, Gwyniever, Lilotus** — Thee Stork Club, Oakland. Queer punk bill in
a 150-cap Telegraph dive with red flocked wallpaper and no pretensions.

**Rolling Quartz** — CryBaby, Oakland. Korean all-woman hard rock five-piece
playing actual instruments loudly — a rarity in a K-market built around
choreography. *Found on a ticketing feed; confirm the door time.*

## Friday 18 September

*42 listings. The strongest night of the week, and a real conflict at its centre.*

### South Bay

**Opera San José: *Don Giovanni*** — California Theatre, San Jose, 7:30pm, from
$65. **Featured.** Mozart in a restored 1927 movie palace, running to 27
September. It matters more than usual this month: with SF Opera's orchestra on
strike and the season opener cancelled, this is the only fully staged grand
opera on in the region.

**Music in the Park: Common Kings, Sammy Johnson** — Plaza de César Chávez, San
Jose, 6pm. Pacific-island reggae and soul outdoors in the plaza. *The series was
founded in 1989 as a free event and recent editions have been ticketed; I could
not confirm 2026 pricing, so assume a door charge.*

**Kany García** — San Jose Civic, 8pm, from $48. Puerto Rican singer-songwriter
with a shelf of Latin Grammys, working in the cantautora tradition rather than
the reggaetón one — guitar, voice, and lyrics that carry the weight.

**San Jose Bike Party** — start point announced the week of the ride, meet
7:30pm, roll 8pm, free. Third Friday, every month: a few thousand people ride a
themed route with sound systems on trailers. The start point goes up on the
group's socials days beforehand — do not plan around a fixed address.

**San Jose Harvest & Handmade Discovery Fest** — Almaden Lake Park, from 10am,
free. Three days, Friday to Sunday. *Details from a listings aggregator rather
than an organiser; hours may shift.*

**Carín León** — SAP Center, San Jose, 8pm, from $170. Arena show, listed for
completeness. León is the most interesting figure in regional Mexican music
right now — he has pulled the genre toward country and soul without losing the
sierreño core — and he is findable without this digest.

**Yellow Claw** — PURE Nightclub, Sunnyvale, 10pm, from $85. Late start. Street
parking is free after six and Caltrain Sunnyvale is a six-minute walk, but the
last northbound train goes long before this ends.

**Daft Disko** — The Ritz, San Jose, 9pm, from $30. A Daft Punk tribute night,
listed because The Ritz is the only real small club in downtown San Jose and its
calendar is worth knowing.

### Peninsula

**Nat King Cole Reimagined, with Bill Cunliffe** — Meyhouse, Palo Alto. Two
nights. Cunliffe is a Grammy-winning arranger and a serious pianist, and
Meyhouse is the closest thing the mid-Peninsula has to a proper jazz supper
room. The nearest good jazz to Mountain View this week by a distance.

**Moonalice, Bert's Brew** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 7pm doors / 8pm.
Bay Area psychedelic rock with deep Dead and Airplane lineage, and a poster
commissioned for every single show since 2007 — the archive is genuinely a
cultural artefact at this point.

**Ren Geisick** — Little Green, A Plant Bar, Redwood City, 8pm, free. A Bay Area
jazz singer in a plant shop that turns into a bar at night — a room of about
forty people, no cover listed. The kind of listing that justifies the whole
exercise. *Confirm on the day.*

**SONA: 70s and 80s rock covers** — Club Fox, Redwood City, 7:30pm doors. A
covers night, listed for completeness rather than championed.

### San Francisco

**Dianne Reeves with Branford Marsalis** — Miner Auditorium, SFJAZZ.
**Featured.** Two nights, Friday and Saturday. Five Grammys, the most complete
jazz voice working, and Marsalis beside her rather than behind her — a
duo-weighted billing, not a singer with a band. The fall season's real opening
statement. Muni Van Ness is four minutes; the garage is $40.

**Lido Pimienta, Chancha Via Circuito** — Bimbo's 365 Club, 7pm doors, 21+. The
best-matched double bill of the week. Pimienta sings Afro-Colombian song forms
through art-pop production; Chancha Via Circuito built digital cumbia into
something slow and hallucinatory. In Bimbo's 1931 supper-club room this should
be extraordinary.

**Sweeping Promises, Fake Fruit, System Exclusive** — Rickshaw Stop, 8pm doors /
9pm, $20–$25. Taut, treble-forward post-punk recorded in a single room on one
mic, and it translates live better than that method has any right to. Best
small-room bill on Friday.

**Steel Pole Bath Tub, Ovarian Trolley, Wife** — Bottom of the Hill, 7:30pm
doors, $35. A reunion of the Bay Area's own noise-rock institution, with mid-90s
art punk beneath them. Effectively a wake for a scene, in the room that housed
it, in the year that room closes. *This conflicts head-on with Sweeping
Promises and with Lido Pimienta — three good shows, three different reasons, one
Friday.*

**Daniel Avery (live), Draag** — The Independent, 9pm. Live, not a DJ set: Avery
builds long, corroded techno arcs out of hardware, closer to shoegaze than to a
club.

**The Exploited, Total Chaos, Dwarves** — August Hall. Scottish street punk from
1979, still led by Wattie Buchan, with San Francisco's own Dwarves third on the
bill. A rough, loud, very old-school night off Union Square.

**Citizen, Hotline TNT, Anxious, Cryogeyser** — The Regency Ballroom. Four-band
emo and fuzz-pop package where the support is unusually strong — Hotline TNT's
blown-out melodic guitar is the reason to arrive early.

**Shawn James, Gravedancer** — The Chapel, 8pm doors / 9pm, $30–$35. Big-voiced
dark Americana, heavier and more theatrical than the acoustic guitar suggests.
The Chapel is a former mortuary with a 40-foot arched ceiling, which flatters
exactly this.

**Âme (Innervisions), Rodriguez Jr.** — Public Works. Deep, melodic Berlin house
from the label that defined the sound for a decade. Public Works' main room is
wooden and warm and the sound system is the best argument for the venue. Not an
11pm-and-home booking.

**RUSSELL!, Nari, Darilø** — Café du Nord, 8pm. Three-act local pop bill in the
basement. *A ticketing feed also lists Russell Thompkins Jr. of The Stylistics
in the building the same night, presumably upstairs in the Swedish American
Hall; I could not confirm it against the venue, so do not count on it.*

**BIIRD (night 1)** — Brick & Mortar Music Hall, 8pm doors / 9pm. **Sold out**,
all ages. The Sunday night still had tickets.

**San Francisco Greek Festival** — Annunciation Cathedral, 11am–10pm, free.
Friday and Saturday 11 to 10, Sunday noon to 7, on the cathedral's hilltop above
the Mission. Dance troupes on the hour, a taverna that serves late, and a pastry
table that empties first.

**Oktoberfest on Front** — Front Street Entertainment Zone, 2–10pm, free with
RSVP. The street outside Schroeder's — a German restaurant on that block since
