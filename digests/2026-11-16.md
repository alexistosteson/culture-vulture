# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 16 November 2026

Monday 16 November – Sunday 22 November. 360 listings, ordered by remove from
Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**The dominant booking is a residency, not a show.** The Mountain Goats are
playing six consecutive nights at the Fillmore, each one built on a different
seam of a thirty-year catalogue, and five of the six fall in this window: the
Alpha songs on Monday, a requests night on Wednesday, *The Sunset Tree* whole on
Thursday, *Cast of Thousands* on Friday, and a closing night of album closers on
Saturday. No other act in the Bay Area this week is doing anything of that
shape. **Thursday is the night to take** — *The Sunset Tree* is the record most
of the room came for, and hearing it in sequence is not an experience the band
offers on an ordinary tour. The Fillmore publishes no door times for any of the
six, which is the one irritating thing about it.

**Two of the week's three most ambitious concerts are in the same Berkeley
hall on consecutive nights.** On Friday Cal Performances puts **all twenty
Philip Glass piano etudes** in Zellerbach Hall in one sitting, divided among ten
pianists — Timo Andres, Aaron Diehl, Daniela Liebman and Jason Moran among them.
On Saturday Moran is back in the same room with **the Harlem Hellfighters**, his
piece on James Reese Europe, who took ragtime into the trenches with the 369th
Infantry and was killed by his own drummer, with a seven-piece horn section and
Bradford Young's images behind it. Neither happens twice. If you can only make
one, the Glass is the rarer event and the Moran is the better concert.

**360 listings against 455 last week, and the drop is real rather than a
research failure.** Last week was a freak coincidence of punk anniversaries on
top of a Veterans Day holiday; this is the week before Thanksgiving, when touring
slows and the arena calendar empties — the Warriors are away from the 16th to the
19th and the only home game is Sunday, and the Sharks have the only other
in-window arena sport. **Saturday the 21st carries 103 listings and Monday the
16th carries 18**, which is the widest spread of any week this digest has
recorded. Monday has nothing at all in the South Bay.

**Saturday is also the day the holiday season starts, all at once.** The **42nd
Great Dickens Christmas Fair** opens at the Cow Palace — five acres of Victorian
London, a dozen stages, hundreds of costumed performers, ten till six, online
tickets only and twenty dollars to park. **Filoli's Holiday Lights** and holiday
daytime hours are both running. **Smuin's Christmas Ballet** opens its tour with
three performances at the Lesher Center in Walnut Creek. All of it arrives on the
same weekend, and none of it was there last week.

**The best free things are spread across Friday, Saturday and Sunday.** On
Friday the **Colma Asian Night Market** takes the community centre lot with a
Filipino parol lantern workshop in it — free, outdoors, and BART Colma genuinely
beats driving; the **From the E Harvest Night Market** does the same for Ocean
Avenue. Saturday has **Sábado Gigante**, a free Native arts and culture festival
on the 16th & Mission plaza from eleven till four, which is the best free thing
in the city this week; the **de Young** and the **Legion of Honor** are both free
to Bay Area residents with ID on timed tickets, and those go; and there is a
free **Native Plant Fest** in the Fort Mason gardens that sends you home with
seed. Sunday has the **Hawaiian Arts Fest** at the Oakland Asian Cultural Center,
noon till five of hula and chant with one of the region's senior kumu hula, free
with registration — and, as every Sunday, **Lindy in the Park** on JFK Drive and
the **roller disco** at Skatin' Place.

**Three bookings worth going out of your way for that are not in big rooms.**
**The Residents** perform *Eskimo* whole at the Palace of Fine Arts on Saturday,
which for a fifty-year-old anonymous San Francisco institution is as close to a
retrospective as they will give you. **Paul Collins** plays the Nerves, the Beat
and the Exploding Hearts songbook across two nights at Kilowatt, a 150-capacity
bar — he wrote *Hanging on the Telephone* before Blondie recorded it. And on
Sunday afternoon **Dylan Carlson of Earth**, who more or less invented drone
metal, plays an all-day benefit in the same bar.

**Opera and new work.** **Salt & Spirit**, Opera Parallèle's world premiere built
on Gullah-Geechee spirituals, has its last two performances on Thursday and
Saturday on Z Space's Steindler Stage — after Saturday it is gone in this form.
**Opera San José's Fiddler on the Roof** plays the California Theatre on Friday
and Sunday with opera voices and an orchestra in the pit, which changes the show
more than you would expect. San Francisco Opera's **Marriage of Figaro** has
Wednesday and Saturday performances at the War Memorial.

**Punk has a good week and Bottom of the Hill is the reason.** The club closes
for good at the end of the year and has four bills in this window, two of them
sold out: Finish Ticket's last ever show on Monday, **Ruby Ibarra** with a
four-deep Filipina-American hometown bill on Thursday, **Earthless** on Friday,
and **M.D.C. and Frightwig** co-headlining on Saturday — the Texas band and the
San Francisco one who were doing it here first. MDC also play the Ivy Room on
Friday, so there is no excuse.

**Two conflicts worth naming.** Saturday at half seven sets the Mountain Goats'
closing night against Jason Moran in Berkeley against *Salt & Spirit* in the
Mission against The Residents at the Palace of Fine Arts; all four are the best
thing in their category and the geography makes them mutually exclusive. And on
Sunday afternoon the New People cinema in Japantown has **two films at one
o'clock** — *In the Mood for Love* and the Miss Peppermint documentary — in the
same building.

**Practical notes.** Pretty Lights plays the Fox in Oakland three nights running
and Thundercat is there on Tuesday; downtown Oakland garage gates shut at seven
on weeknights, so take BART. Cal Performances warns of campus parking trouble
around the Zellerbach dates. The free DJ pop-up at Crissy Field on Saturday is
outdoors at the Golden Gate in November — bring a coat. Dickens Fair sells no
tickets at the door. And the Regency Ballroom still publishes no dates of its
own, which is why three of its listings this week are marked low confidence.
