# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 23 November 2026

Monday 23 November – Sunday 29 November. 416 listings, ordered by remove from
Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**Thanksgiving week is not the quiet week it looks like on a venue calendar.**
Nine of the region's tier-1 music rooms have nothing at all in the window — the
Warfield, Café du Nord, Rickshaw Stop, the Fox Oakland, the UC Theatre, the
Greek, Fox Theatre Redwood City, Heritage Theatre and Felton Music Hall are all
dark on their own listings — and yet the week comes to 416 listings against 360
last week. The difference is that the holiday programming arrived all at once:
light trails, tree lightings, free museum days, two four-day festivals and a
Thanksgiving Day with its own small, strange calendar. **Saturday the 28th
carries 97 listings and Thursday the 26th carries 15**, which is about the
spread you would expect, and Thursday's fifteen are more interesting than
Tuesday's thirty-four.

**The single most significant thing this week happens before dawn on
Thanksgiving.** The **Indigenous Peoples' Sunrise Ceremony on Alcatraz** marks
the 1969–71 occupation; boats leave Pier 33 from about 4.15am, the ceremony is
over by eight, the ceremony itself is free and the boat is twelve dollars. It is
cold, it is crowded, and nothing else in the window is in the same category.
Two smaller pieces of counter-programming sit beside it: a free **Indigenous
Freedom Quiz Contest** in Berkeley on Thursday afternoon, with cash prizes and
the proceeds going to a Mohawk-language school, and on Sunday **Kim Shuck's Poem
Jam** at the Main Library, built around Zitkala-Ša's *American Indian Stories*,
free.

**Black Friday is the best night of the week for music, and it is not close.**
**Emma Ruth Rundle** at the Great American for twenty-four dollars, with Cinder
Well opening — doom-weight songwriting and Irish-inflected drone folk, a pairing
somebody actually thought about. The **twentieth annual Sleepless Nights** at
the Chapel, nine Bay Area acts taking a Gram Parsons song each, which after
twenty years has stopped being a tribute and become a tradition. **San Francisco
Opera's *Marriage of Figaro*** at the War Memorial, the longest sit of the week
and the best-made thing in it. And across the bay, **BAMPFA screens *The Last
Waltz* at 5.30** — Scorsese's film of the Band's farewell, shot on Thanksgiving
Day 1976 at Winterland, screening fifty years and one day later. If you can only
take one, take the Rundle show; if you want the thing you will still be talking
about, take the Last Waltz screening. They do not conflict: Berkeley at 5.30
leaves you nothing at the Great American at 8, so pick.

**Sheila E. plays two nights at Yoshi's**, which is the week's best argument for
a ticket at any price: the Oakland-born drummer who was Prince's musical
director, in a 400-seat listening room, in her own town. Friday and Saturday,
$79–$134.

**Saturday is the crowded night.** **Porter Robinson** headlines Club Darc at
Pier 48, the only electronic booking this week that is genuinely hard to get
into; **Laurie Lewis** plays the Freight with Nina Gerber, fifty years into West
Coast bluegrass, in the room she helped build; **Journey** does the farewell
tour at Chase Center; and the **de Young and the Legion of Honor are both free
to Bay Area residents** on timed tickets that go. Also Saturday: **ODC/Dance
opens its fortieth *Velveteen Rabbit*** at YBCA at 11am, **Ousmane Sembène's
*Black Girl*** screens at YBCA at 2 with critic Ashley Clark, and the Roxie runs
the only **35mm** print in the Bay Area this week — *Women on the Verge of a
Nervous Breakdown*, 6pm.

**The free list is unusually long: 93 of the 416 listings are free or free with
an RSVP.** The ice rink in Union Square is **free all Thanksgiving Day**. The
**SJ Made Holiday Craft Fair** at the Santa Clara Convention Center is free in
and free to park, 350 makers over Friday and Saturday, and is the best free
thing in the South Bay. **Sweetwater puts on free live music on Black Friday**
(Josh Zee and Friends, doors 7). The **Marin County Fairgrounds' Holiday Light
Spectacular** is free and nightly from Friday. In the city, **Ghirardelli
Square's tree lighting** (Friday, 4.45), the **Yerba Buena Gardens tree and
carousel lighting with free skating** (Saturday, 4.30), a free **Magic of
Christmas** choral hour at Oakland Temple Hill (Saturday, 6), **Guajirón's free
Cuban jam** at the Boom Boom Room (Sunday, 7.30) and the standing Sunday pair of
**Lindy in the Park** and the **roller disco** at Skatin' Place.

**Two things worth knowing about Thursday.** Almost everything is shut — the
Roxie closes, BAMPFA closes, most galleries close — but **the Magic Theatre
plays *A Rashomon* at 8**, the **Independent has Jack Kays** at 9 (found on SF
Station; the venue's own calendar does not list it), the **Exploratorium runs
After Dark**, and the **Knockout holds its annual Thanksgiving 'Turkey'oke**.
The Thanksgiving-night *Peter Pan Goes Wrong* at SF Playhouse is listed at low
confidence — it comes from the standing weekly schedule and no aggregator
confirms it, so ring the box office.

**In the South Bay, the week belongs to mariachi and to the regional-Mexican
circuit.** **Mariachi Nuevo Tecalitlán** is at the San Jose Civic on Friday,
billed with Mariachi Vargas de Tecalitlán — the oldest continuously performing
mariachi in the world — and if that billing holds it is the most significant
concert south of the city this week. **Merry-Achi Christmas** runs three
performances at the Hammer, Los Gemelos de Sinaloa play the Civic on Thanksgiving
eve, and three more banda bills turn up in Concord and San Jose rooms that reach
no aggregator but Songkick. **Opera San José closes *Fiddler on the Roof*** at
the California Theatre with matinees on Saturday and Sunday. The **Earthquakes
have a home MLS playoff game** on Tuesday, which has not been true for some
years.

**Berkeley Rep closes *Liberation*** on Sunday after five weeks — eight
performances left, Tuesday through Sunday at the Roda. **SF Playhouse closes
*Peter Pan Goes Wrong*** on Saturday night after two months. Both are
last-chance.

**Dead nights:** Thursday outside the holiday programming, and the Peninsula all
week — eleven listings, of which eight are Filoli and two are tribute bands at
the Guild. That is a thin region rather than a research failure; see below.
