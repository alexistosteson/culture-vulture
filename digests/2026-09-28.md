# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 28 September 2026

Monday 28 September – Sunday 4 October. 305 listings, ordered by remove
from Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**Hardly Strictly Bluegrass takes Golden Gate Park from Friday to Sunday, it is free,
and it is the whole week.** Seventy-one sets over six stages and three days, no ticket
and no wristband, with something in the region of 750,000 people across the weekend.
Friday has Los Lobos, My Morning Jacket, Marty Stuart, Molly Tuttle and Lukas Nelson.
Saturday is the deepest day — Gillian Welch & David Rawlings at 3:50, Rilo Kiley
stepping in for Alison Krauss at 4:55, then Old Crow Medicine Show and Steve Earle &
the Hardly Strictly Dukes closing simultaneously at 7. Sunday closes with Robert Plant
at 4:40 and Emmylou Harris at 5:45. Gates are 11am Friday, 9am the other two days;
performances stop at 7 every day. No bikes, scooters or Onewheels inside the fence, no
umbrellas, no hard coolers, blankets under 6x8 only.

**If you can only do one thing this week, do Thursday night at the Masonic instead.**
An Evening Honoring Steve Earle puts Emmylou Harris, Jackson Browne, Gillian Welch &
David Rawlings, Buddy Miller, Elizabeth Cook and Hot Tuna on one stage, with proceeds to
Sweetwater Spectrum. It is the ticketed kickoff to the free festival, and most of those
people then play the park for nothing — which is exactly why the indoor version, seated,
with a running order and no wind, is the better night. Nothing else this week assembles
that many people who matter in one room.

**Saturday is the busiest day at fifty-seven listings, and Friday is the most congested
at sixty-nine.** The week totals 305 listings against 278 last week, which makes it the
largest this digest has covered. The driver is a free three-day festival landing on top
of a fall season that switched on a fortnight ago: A.C.T., Berkeley Rep and SF Playhouse
all mid-run, the Symphony opening a subscription week with Renée Fleming, SFJAZZ running
Hiromi twice in two nights, and the Opera closing *Mary, Queen of Scots* on Sunday.

**Sunday is a genuine collision and you should pick one side of it.** Hardly Strictly's
best day overlaps almost exactly with the 52nd Castro Street Fair, eleven to six at
Castro and Market, free with a $10–20 donation. Both are free, both are outdoors, both
are enormous, and they are four miles apart through a city whose west end is closed to
cars. Add the Opera's closing matinee at 2, the Symphony's Strauss matinee at 2, Litquake
Out Loud at Yerba Buena Gardens from 11, and Fleet Week technically opening on the
waterfront, and a circuit means seeing none of it. Commit.

**Seventy-five of the 305 listings cost nothing**, and several of the best are not
the festival. The 52nd Oakland Black Cowboy Parade & Festival at deFremery Park on
Saturday is free, starts at 10, and is the only event of its kind in the region —
fifty-two years of keeping the history of Black cowboys in the West visible, with horses,
line dancing and food until six. Friday Nights at OMCA has Shanté on the Garden Stage,
free, and the series ends this month. Oakland First Fridays closes twenty blocks of
Telegraph. Free museum Saturday covers the de Young and the Legion of Honor for Bay Area
residents, the Asian Art Museum is free for everyone on Sunday, and YBCA is free every
Wednesday.

**Bottom of the Hill sells out four of its seven nights, and one of them is Deerhoof
twice.** The club closes at the end of the year. Thirty-two years of the most joyful
difficult music made in this city, in the 350-capacity room that made them, in the last
autumn that room exists — Friday with Greyhound and Etch, Saturday with Las Sucias and
Otioxi. Both gone. What is still available in that room is Sunday's Acid Mothers Temple
with Cotton Casino, which is the surprise of the week and the best remaining ticket in
the building.

**Wednesday and Saturday carry the two strangest bills of the month.** Chat Pile with
Soul Glo and Virga at the Regency on Wednesday is the heaviest and best-sequenced
package to come through here this year. On Saturday, Igorrr at the Great American —
baroque harpsichord welded to breakcore and death metal — with Secret Chiefs 3 and
Violent Magic Orchestra underneath, is sold out and deserved to be. Neither of them is
in the park.

**The Peninsula has eleven listings and one that matters.** Hardly Strictly Out of the
Park brings Kathleen Edwards and Fantastic Cat to the Guild Theatre on Sunday night — an
official festival offshoot, seated, indoors, forty miles south. Every summer series in
the region has closed: Music on the Square, Movies on the Square, the Palo Alto Twilight
concerts and Colma's Thursday nights are all finished for the year, and the Golden Gate
Park Band wrapped its 144th season on 27 September. Otherwise the Peninsula is a gallery
opening in Palo Alto, a free museum day in Redwood City, and the Guild carrying the
region's touring music single-handedly.

**Monday is the thinnest night at sixteen listings, and that is real rather than a
research gap.** Bobby McFerrin & Motion at the Freight is the pick, and it is a noon
show — ticketing lists doors at 11:30, which is unusual enough that you should confirm it
before driving to Berkeley.

**Worth the Drive has five entries and only one of them clears the bar comfortably.**
The 49th Mill Valley Film Festival opens Thursday with Martin McDonagh's *Wild Horse
Nine* and runs eleven days across Mill Valley, San Rafael and Larkspur — ninety-four
features, eighty shorts, forty-five countries. Steel Pulse at the Catalyst on Thursday is
their only Bay date and is a legacy act in a club, which is the case the drive test was
written for. Santa Cruz Porchfest on Saturday is a borderline call I made in favour of
including: nothing would be a top-three event of a Hardly Strictly week, but a first-ever
free fifty-set porch crawl is unrepeatable. Everything else over the hill was skipped on
purpose — Lukas Nelson sold out Sweetwater on Monday and plays the park on Friday, Old
Crow Medicine Show are at Sweetwater on Saturday and headline the park the same weekend.
There is no argument for either drive.

## Monday 28 September

*16 listings.* The thinnest night of the week, and honestly thin rather than under-researched — Monday before a festival weekend is when nobody books anything. **Best of the night:** Bobby McFerrin & Motion at the Freight, if the noon start is real. If it is not, Fred Wesley's New JBs with Martha High at Yoshi's is the people who actually played on the records, and it starts at a normal hour.

### San Francisco

**Japanese Tea Garden free admission hour** — Japanese Tea Garden, San Francisco, 9am, free. Free before 10am on Monday, Wednesday and Friday; $12–16 otherwise. An hour in the oldest public Japanese garden in the country for nothing, if you can make an early start.

**Ha Chong-Hyun: Retrospective** — Asian Art Museum, San Francisco, 10am. The Dansaekhwa painter who pushed pigment through the back of hemp canvas rather than onto the front of it. Free for all on the first Sunday of the month, which this week means 4 October.

**Memorizing Alison** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Screening in the Coffee & Language series for the film's 25th anniversary. The Roxie's programming is the reason to read its calendar rather than its marquee.

**Motown on Mondays** — Madrone Art Bar, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Free before 8, $5 after, and running most Mondays for well over a decade. Straight 45s soul to a room that actually dances on a work night.

**Yerba Buena Illuminated** — Humboldt Bank Building, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Projection work thrown up the side of a 1908 skyscraper on Market. Free, outdoors, and better from the far pavement than from underneath it.

**Social Distortion, Descendents & The Chats** — The Masonic, San Francisco, 7:30pm. Three generations of the same impulse on one bill: Mike Ness's tattooed Bakersfield-punk, the Descendents' hyper-caffeinated melody, and Australia's Chats doing pub-rock as farce. They play the Fox tomorrow, so the room is the variable, not the set.

**Aldous Harding** — Castro Theatre, San Francisco, 8pm. Sold out. The New Zealand songwriter's voice shifts register between songs like a character actor changing coats, and the theatricality lands better in a 1,400-seat picture palace than in a club. Sam Burton opens with high-lonesome ballads.

**LUCKI** — The Warfield, San Francisco, 8pm. Chicago rapper whose lo-fi, narcotised delivery made him a reference point for a whole strain of internet rap. Sk8star opens.

**Phora** — Brick & Mortar Music Hall, San Francisco, 8pm. Anaheim rapper who built a following on confessional, melodic writing about anxiety and family. The venue's listing showed a $0.00 price, which is more likely a data error than a free show — check before you go. *Low confidence — verify before you go.*

**Stephan Jenkins (solo acoustic)** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 8pm, $20–25. Sold out. Third Eye Blind's frontman playing a 350-cap room he long outgrew, unplugged. The club closes at the end of the year, which is the reason this one sold out in an afternoon.

**Tokyo Tea Room** — Bimbo's 365 Club, San Francisco, 8pm. Sold out. English shoegaze-adjacent five-piece with more jangle than fuzz; Beach Vacation opens. A supper-club room with a curtain, which suits the reverb.

**Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8:30pm. Jane Schoenbrun in person. After We're All Going to the World's Fair and I Saw the TV Glow, a slasher from this director is the most interesting genre proposition of the month.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Bobby McFerrin & Motion** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley, 12pm. A daytime slot — ticketing lists doors at 11:30 for a noon start, which is unusual enough to confirm before you travel. McFerrin's Motion project is improvised vocal music with no net; he also played the Freight last Monday, so this is a residency rather than a repeat listing.

**Art Lande, Günter Wehinger, Tim Wendel** — Piedmont Piano Company, Oakland, 7pm, $25. Free-ish piano trio music in a piano showroom, which is the best-sounding accident in Oakland. Lande has been making quietly strange records since the seventies.

**Fred Wesley's New JBs with Martha High's Funky Divas** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7:30pm, $44–89. Wesley wrote the horn charts that define the word funk, and Martha High sang behind James Brown for eighteen years. This is the source material, played by the people who cut it.

**Devin Gray** — Tom's Place, Berkeley, 8pm. Brooklyn drummer-composer in a tiny Berkeley room. The kind of booking that exists only because someone runs a house series for love.

### San Francisco

**Lava Thomas: Lineage** — Jessica Silverman Gallery, San Francisco, 10am, free. Thomas works in tambourines, graphite and marble to make monuments to Black women the city never built. Free, and a commercial gallery you should walk into anyway.

**Civic Center Soundtrack** — Fulton Plaza, San Francisco, 12pm, free. Free lunchtime music and food trucks on the plaza between the library and City Hall. Runs Tuesdays and Thursdays; the bands are local and the point is the hour outdoors.

**Ice Cream Social with the Nova Jazz Band** — Mechanics Monument Plaza, San Francisco, 3:30pm, free. Trad jazz and free ice cream on a downtown plaza at half past three on a Tuesday. Downtown SF's programming is aimed squarely at office workers and is none the worse for it.

**Noe Valley Night Market** — Noe Valley Town Square, San Francisco, 5pm, free. Small, weekly, free, and genuinely a neighbourhood thing rather than a pop-up circuit. Makers and food on the square at 24th.

**Filipiñana** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Part of FiliKula. Rafael Manuel's short feature watches a golf-course tee-girl in Manila with a still, class-conscious eye.

**Gandahar (4K restoration)** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. René Laloux's 1988 animated science fiction, the last of the three features he made and the least seen. Rarely projected at all, let alone restored.

**Peter Pan Goes Wrong** — San Francisco Playhouse, San Francisco, 7pm. The Mischief company's disaster-panto, running to late November. The Playhouse's calendar does not publish per-date curtain times, so the 7pm is the house weekday default.

**Alfred Hitchcock's North by Northwest** — Toni Rembe Theater, San Francisco, 7:30pm. Emma Rice's stage adaptation opens A.C.T.'s season and runs to 18 October. Rice's work is built out of visible stagecraft, which is a good match for a film about a man pretending to be someone who does not exist.

**Mary, Queen of Scots** — War Memorial Opera House, San Francisco, 7:30pm. Thea Musgrave's 1977 opera, back at the house that gave it its American premiere. Musgrave is 98 and wrote the libretto herself; the score's Scottish court is all brass and menace. Two performances left, this and Sunday's matinee.

**American Doctor** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8pm. Co-presented by the Arab Film & Media Institute and the Jewish Film Institute — a pairing that is itself the statement. Also screens Wednesday at 6.

**Brian Fallon & The Painkillers** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 8pm, $40–45. The Gaslight Anthem's singer doing the Springsteen-by-way-of-hardcore thing in a 400-cap room, with Tim Barry — ex-Avail, now solo and rawer — opening. All ages.

**Chanel Beads** — Great American Music Hall, San Francisco, 8pm, $25–30. Sold out. Shane Lavers builds songs out of blown-out string samples and half-buried vocals — pop shapes heard through a wall. Horse Vision and urika's bedroom open.

**Ethan Regan** — August Hall, San Francisco, 8pm. With harf. The venue lists no times or prices on its calendar, so treat the 8pm as the house default rather than confirmed.

**KAL-EL** — DNA Lounge, San Francisco, 8pm. Norwegian stoner-metal with Goya supporting. The venue's own monthly calendar pages were returning 503s all day, so this is sourced from the ticketing feed rather than the venue.

**cupcakKe** — The Regency Ballroom, San Francisco, 8pm. Chicago rapper whose filthiest bars and her most tender ones sit on the same record, usually the same song. A 1,400-cap ballroom is the right scale now.

**Ghost in the Shell: 30th Anniversary Remaster** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8:15pm. Oshii's 1995 film in a new transfer. The rain-slicked Hong Kong sequence is the argument for seeing it projected rather than streamed.

**The Quiet Men, Greg Hoy & the Boys, Fast & Vengefully** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 8:30pm, $15–20. A local three-band bill at fifteen dollars, which is the club doing the job it exists for. Greg Hoy has been making scrappy power-pop records in this city for twenty years.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Joey Alexander** — Piedmont Piano Company, Oakland, 5:30pm, $35. The Indonesian pianist who was Grammy-nominated at eleven and is now a serious adult improviser, in a showroom seating maybe eighty. Thirty-five dollars is a mistake in your favour.

**Social Distortion, Descendents & The Chats** — Fox Theater, Oakland, 7pm. Doors 6. The second of two Bay nights — if you have the choice, the Fox is the better-sounding room and BART drops you at the door.

**The Cook** — Peet's Theatre, Berkeley Rep, Berkeley, 7pm. Postshow discussion tonight. Eduardo Machado's play about a cook's forty-year vow to keep a Havana mansion ready for owners who are never coming back. Runs to 11 October.

**Spyro Gyra** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7:30pm, $44–84. Fifty years of jazz-fusion by a band that has outlasted every genre label applied to it. Two nights. Smooth in the technical sense, not the pejorative one.

**LAUNDRY DAY** — Cornerstone, Berkeley, 8pm. Doors 7. Five New Yorkers who started as teenagers making bedroom pop and have thickened into something closer to a band. A beer hall on Shattuck with a real stage at the back.

**Last Tuesdays Comedy** — The Layover, Oakland, 8pm, free. Free with RSVP, monthly, and the Layover is the rare downtown Oakland bar that feels like a living room. Booked locally rather than by a chain.

**Thelma and the Sleaze** — Ivy Room, Albany, 8pm. Nashville's loudest, filthiest boogie band in a 150-cap Albany bar. Lauren Gilbert plays Southern rock like it owes her money.

**Drive-In Movie Night** — Solano 2 Drive In, Concord, 8:30pm, $6. Six dollars a head at one of the last operating drive-ins in the region. Concord is a haul from anywhere, and the price and the novelty are the argument.

## Wednesday 30 September

## Sunday 4 October

*47 listings, and two enormous free events on top of each other.* **Best of the day:** Hardly Strictly's closing day, with Robert Plant at 4:40 and Emmylou Harris at 5:45 — but the Castro Street Fair, 11 to 6, is the better afternoon if crowds of that size in a park are not your thing. **Conflict:** they overlap almost exactly and are four miles apart through a closed park. Also colliding at 2pm: the Opera's closing *Mary, Queen of Scots* with its post-show talkback, and the Symphony's final Strauss matinee. **Best of the night:** Acid Mothers Temple at Bottom of the Hill, which is the only night at that club this week you can still get into, or Octo Octa at the Castro at 6 if you are already at the fair. **Friction:** Lindy in the Park and the roller disco both sit inside the festival footprint today — they will run, with a much bigger audience than usual.

### San Francisco

**Fort Mason Farmers Market** — Fort Mason Center, San Francisco, 9:30am, free. Free, Sundays. Smaller and calmer than the Ferry Plaza, with the bay at the end of the row.

**52nd Castro Street Fair** — Castro Street at Market, San Francisco, 11am, free. **Featured.** Free with a $10–20 suggested donation, this year themed 'Where We Belong'. Harvey Milk started it in 1974 to show that gay-owned businesses were a neighbourhood economy; since 1998 it has given $1.6m back to community groups. Stages, DJs and hundreds of makers. It overlaps the festival almost exactly — Muni Castro puts you at the gate, and the Castro Art Mart at Market and Noe runs alongside it.

**Litquake Out Loud + Book Fair** — Esplanade, Yerba Buena Gardens, San Francisco, 11am, free. Free. Readings and a book fair on the Esplanade — the free outdoor curtain-raiser to Litquake proper, which runs 8–24 October. Small presses in the sun, which beats a bookshop reading.

Come back next week for the flying.

  and the venue is almost certainly programming Friday and Saturday. This is the week's

  Kreayshawn on Saturday) come from the ticketing sweep instead, and there are probably
