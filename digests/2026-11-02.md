# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 2 November 2026

Monday 2 November – Sunday 8 November. 387 listings, ordered by remove from
Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**This is the week after the big one, and the shape of it shows: 387 listings
against 462 last week, and Monday is the quietest day the digest has recorded at
22.** Halloween and Día de los Muertos took the crowds and the street closures
with them. What is left is a week with no single dominant festival and an
unusually good spread of one-off bookings — which, if you were defeated by
choice last Saturday, is the better problem.

**The dominant event is civic, free, and the first of its kind.** The
**Valkyries Championship Parade** runs along the Embarcadero from noon on
Wednesday, after the team took the title in its second season — the first
championship parade a San Francisco women's professional team has ever had. It
is free, it is outdoors, and the surface Muni lines will not cope; get north of
the Ferry Building early or come in on BART.

**Saturday is the biggest day at 99 listings, and the single best thing on it is
at three in the afternoon.** The **San Francisco Symphony's Día de los Muertos**
concert — the nineteenth — has José Luis Gómez conducting Chávez, Moncayo and
Márquez, Francisco Fullana in the *Fandango*, and the world premiere of Luis
Farfán Carrillo's *El Baile de la Catrina* for two salterios and orchestra, with
dancers from Casa Círculo Cultural through the hall. Free catrinas and altars in
the lobby beforehand. Note that the Songkick venue calendar this project uses
for Davies shows **nothing at all before 2 December**; between September and
June a blank week there means nothing.

**Monday night carries the best concert of the week and it is at the Great
American.** **Madre Cumbia** puts Los Gaiteros de San Jacinto, Fruko y sus Tesos
and Carmelo Torres on one bill: gaita flute tradition from the Montes de María,
the salsa orchestra that defined the Discos Fuentes sound, and cumbia played on
the accordion the way Andrés Landero played it. Thirty dollars. It would
headline a festival anywhere else and it is on a Monday in a 600-capacity hall.

**Wednesday is the crowded night.** **Ms. Lauryn Hill and Wyclef Jean** mark
thirty years of *The Score* at the Greek — two of the three Fugees, outdoors, in
November, which is a weather bet and still the biggest booking of the week.
Against it: **Team Dresch** with **The Third Sex** and **Longstocking** at Bottom
of the Hill, the queercore record that mattered most played by the band that made
it, in a room that closes for good at the end of 2026. They play two nights and
Wednesday is the one to take. Five hardcore bands including **Killing Time** are
at the Great American the same evening, **Mary in the Junkyard** are at the
Rickshaw Stop, and YBCA's galleries are free all day with a performance by
**Ryli** in Gallery 3 at seven. If you can only do one Wednesday thing and you
are not driving to Berkeley, take Team Dresch.

**Thursday has the best free music of the week in the middle of a street.**
**Downtown First Thursdays** has **Big Freedia** — New Orleans bounce, free with
RSVP, on Second Street between Market and Howard, five till ten. Everything else
on Thursday is indoors and ticketed: **Squirrel Flower** with Sour Widows at the
Chapel is the best-matched bill, **Danny L Harle** is at the Great American,
**Moby** DJs the Bill Graham, and the **30th Arab Film Festival** opens at the
Kabuki for ten days and forty films.

**Friday is the first Friday, which means four street festivals and no cover
charge.** **Oakland First Fridays** closes twenty blocks of Telegraph; **Oakland
Art Murmur** runs the galleries inside it and is still the better art.
**North Beach First Fridays**, **South First Fridays** in San Jose's SoFA
district and a free **Dirtybird Night Market** on the plaza outside Chase Center
fill out the evening. The ticketed end is strong too: **Genesis Owusu** at the
Rickshaw Stop for under $35 is the best-value ticket in the city all week,
**Joshua Redman** is in SFJAZZ's main hall, **King Buffalo** with L.A. Witch is
the loudest good show, and the **Lalo Guerrero Legacy Festival** at Freight &
Salvage — Mark Guerrero, La Doña and Dr. Loco's Rockin' Jalapeño Band
celebrating the father of Chicano music — is the most historically serious thing
in the region this week, and runs Friday and Saturday both.

**Sunday is unusually strong for a Sunday and most of it is free.** Four Diwali
celebrations (Yerba Buena Gardens, the Outer Sunset market, a Swaminarayan
temple and St. Mark's), the **season finale of the Inner Sunset Flea**, Lindy in
the Park, the Golden Gate Park roller disco, a free outdoor *Toy Story 5* at
Thrive City, a free dance-film screening at the Brava, free admission to The
Tech for National STEM Day, and a free Día de los Muertos festival on Courthouse
Square in Redwood City. The ticketed end: **Thao & The Get Down Stay Down**
play an album-release show at the Independent — sold out, in the 500-cap room
they came up in — and **Armand Hammer** are at Bottom of the Hill, which is the
second-best ticket in the city that night and the harder listen.

**141 of the 387 listings are free or free-ticketed and 51 are outdoors.** The
free end is deep again: the free museum days stack up unusually (de Young and
the Legion free on Tuesday for the first-Tuesday rule *and* on Saturday for Bay
Area residents, YBCA free Wednesday, the GLBT History Museum free Wednesday, the
SF Zoo free for residents Wednesday, BAMPFA free Thursday, the Museum of Craft
and Design free Thursday, the San Mateo County History Museum free Friday, The
Tech free Sunday), plus free rooftop stargazing at SF State, the free Japanese
Tea Garden hour on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and both peaks of the Taurid
meteor shower.

**The theatre is thinner than last week and the one unmissable thing is in
Berkeley.** **Liberation**, Bess Wohl's Pulitzer winner, plays six performances
at the Roda — a daughter restaging her mother's 1970s consciousness-raising
group — and **Covenant** begins previews at the Peet's on Friday. *Peter Pan
Goes Wrong* runs eight at the Playhouse, *The Marriage of Figaro* gets two at
the War Memorial, Palo Alto Players' *The Addams Family* gets four at the Lucie
Stern, and **SFDanceworks Season 9** runs four nights on Z Space's Steindler
Stage. Berkeley Rep's own site answers this run with a redirect loop, so the
Liberation times here come from a ticketing agent and no Covenant curtain time
could be confirmed at all.

**The South Bay has 41 listings and two outdoor festivals two days apart.**
**Good Vibez** — Rebelution, Waka Flocka Flame and Collie Buddz — fills Lake
Cunningham on Friday, rescheduled from September; **Total Vibez Live** with
Keyshia Cole and Ashanti fills the same park on Saturday. The best-value serious
music in the region is **Songs of the Fall**, the Symphony San Jose Chorale in
Mission Santa Clara on Saturday, an hour with no interval, $25 for students.
**Mon Laferte with Silvana Estrada** at SAP Center on Friday is the strongest
Spanish-language bill of the week and is in an arena, which the brief
deprioritises; the cheap counterpart is **Rock en Español Night** at The Ritz two
hours later.

**The Peninsula has 24 listings and the Guild has four of them.** Four nights out
of seven — the Psychedelic Furs on Monday, Henry Diltz's photographs on
Wednesday, Thomas Dolby on Thursday, a mentalist on Friday and Fitz and The
Tantrums twice at the weekend — all four minutes from Caltrain Menlo Park. The
pick of the region is **Yasmin Williams** in the Bing Studio on Saturday:
fingerstyle guitar played across the lap with a kalimba taped to the body of the
instrument, two shows, a couple of hundred seats. **Orpheus Chamber Orchestra**
with Isata Kanneh-Mason is in Bing proper on Sunday afternoon.

**The outer region has one listing and that is a decision, not a gap.** **The
Tannahill Weavers** at Sweetwater Music Hall on Saturday clear the brief's bar
for the drive: fifty-five years of Scottish traditional music, Highland pipes
indoors at close range, and a 300-capacity room they would not normally play on
this side of the Atlantic. Everything else was weighed and did not clear it —
the Catalyst has nothing at all until the 13th, Felton Music Hall has four
nights of local booking, Sweetwater has three more, the Marin Arts & Crafts Show
is free and good and is a craft fair, and Southern Culture on the Skids play
Mill Valley and Santa Cruz as well as Bottom of the Hill, where you should see
them. None of it is among the three best things in this week were it local,
which is the test.
