# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 21 September 2026

Monday 21 September – Sunday 27 September. 278 listings, ordered by remove
from Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**San Francisco Opera is back, and the season opens three weeks late on Tuesday.**
The orchestra's first strike in its forty-five-year history ended last Thursday with a
tentative four-year contract, after five cancelled performances took the 104th season
opener with them. *Simon Boccanegra* finally goes up at 7:30 on Tuesday, then Friday and
Sunday. Last week this digest led with the strike and pointed readers to San Jose
instead; this week both houses are running, and Opera San José's *Don Giovanni* closes
on Sunday afternoon. If you only do one, do the San José matinée — the company has not
staged this in twelve years and the California Theatre is the better room. But the
Opera House reopening is the news.

**Sunday is the most congested day this digest has covered, and two of the things
fighting over it are enormous.** The Folsom Street Fair takes thirteen blocks of SoMa
from 11 to 6 with seven stages and something like 400,000 people — free, with a $10–20
donation at the gates. Four miles south, Portola's second day at Pier 80 has Swedish
House Mafia, Tiësto, Four Tet and Zara Larsson behind a $250 wall. They overlap almost
exactly. Add Erykah Badu selling out the Greek with The Alchemist and Smino, and the
SoFA Street Fair putting eighty-odd bands on ten stages in downtown San Jose for
nothing, and the honest advice is to pick one and commit. A circuit means seeing none
of them.

**Saturday is the busiest by listing count — sixty-five — and Portola's first day is
the better one.** Robyn and Dog Blood headline, with Soulwax, Fatboy Slim, DJ Shadow,
Skepta, Groove Armada and Tricky underneath. It is deeper, stranger and less
stadium-shaped than Sunday. Note that Soulwax also play the Regency on Thursday with
actual drums, which is the version to see if you can only do one.

**The week is unusually busy — 278 listings against 199 last week — and the driver is
Leather Week plus a festival weekend landing on top of a fall season that has just
started.** SFJAZZ opens Tuesday, the Symphony opens twice (a $3,000 gala on Thursday, a
free-to-nonprofits concert on Friday, and Fantastic Negrito with the orchestra on
Saturday), Berkeley Rep is mid-run, SF Playhouse starts previewing, and the Opera is
back. September's second half is when everything in this region switches on at once.

**Seventy-five of the 278 listings cost nothing**, and the best of them are not the
obvious ones. The YBCA opening for Dread Scott's *The Body Politic* on Friday evening is
free with RSVP, with the artists in the room. Friday Nights at OMCA has Los Tranquilos
playing sixties and seventies Latin American soul in the garden, free, and the series
ends in October. Free museum Saturday covers both the de Young and the Legion of Honor
for Bay Area residents. The Golden Gate Park Band's 144th season continues at the
bandshell on Sunday at one, and German Day is at the same bandshell either side of it,
so you get both without moving.

**924 Gilman is running a five-night fortieth-anniversary benefit and every ticket is
five dollars.** Monday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday, all ages, volunteer-run.
Street Eaters play the Saturday and Sunday bills. Forty years of a model that every
professional venue said could not work.

**Bottom of the Hill sold out both nights of the Fat Wreck Chords 35th before the
lineups were announced, and the club closes at the end of the year.** The remaining
count of nights in that room keeps shortening. Wednesday's Cola bill and Thursday's Sam
Chase show are still available and are both good.

**The Peninsula is thin — eleven listings, and none of them featured.** Music on the
Square finished its season on 4 September and the summer concert series in Colma, Palo
Alto and Redwood City are all over. What is left is Saturday's cluster: the San Mateo
arts fest, Pacifica's Fog Fest, an art-and-lit fair in Daly City, and the SALA literary
festival at Menlo College. The Guild Theatre carries the region's live music almost
single-handedly, with Tommy Emmanuel on Wednesday and Samantha Fish on Sunday.

**Worth the Drive has exactly one entry, and that is not an oversight.** Maker Faire
returns to Mare Island for three days and clears the bar on its own terms. Nothing else
did. Sleep play Santa Cruz on Sunday and also play the Fillmore on Friday and Saturday,
so there is no argument for the drive. Sausalito's Art & Harbor Days, the Catalyst's
weekend and Sweetwater's four shows are all decent and none of them would be a top-three
event of this particular week if they happened locally.

## Monday 21 September

*16 listings.* The thinnest night of the week at sixteen listings, and it still has Bobby McFerrin in a six-hundred-seat listening room. **Best of the night:** McFerrin at the Freight. Nothing else this week puts a musician of that stature in a room that small.

### South Bay

**Circus Caballero** — Great Mall, Milpitas, from $78. Touring big top in the Great Mall lot, running nightly at 7:30 through Saturday with weekend matinees. Mexican circus tradition, horses and acrobats; expensive for what it is. Milpitas isn't in the region lists — filed South Bay by proximity.

### San Francisco

**Barbara Forever** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Documentary portrait running a short engagement at the Roxie. Also screens Tuesday at the same hour if tonight is crowded.

**Batch '81** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Part of FiliKula. Mike de Leon's 1982 fraternity-hazing thriller, long one of the hardest Philippine films of its era to see in a decent print — the restoration is the reason to go.

**Motown on Mondays** — Madrone Art Bar, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Free before 8pm, $5 after, and running most Mondays for well over a decade. Straight 45s soul and Motown to a room that actually dances on a work night.

**FRIKO** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 8pm, $26–29. Chicago duo who make loud, unembarrassed guitar music with the dynamics of a much older band — quiet verse, cathartic detonation. A 400-cap room is already small for them.

**Marlon Magnée (of La Femme)** — 4 Star Theater, San Francisco, 8pm. The Chapel moved this one to the 4 Star, so check your ticket for the address. Magnée is half the engine of La Femme, the French band that welds cold-wave synths to yé-yé melody; solo he leans further into the synth side.

**Peter Hook & The Light** — The Warfield, San Francisco, 8pm. Joy Division and New Order's bassist playing the catalogue he wrote the best parts of. He plays Mountain Winery tomorrow night too, outdoors and in Saratoga — pick by geography, the set is the same idea.

**Rose Window Laser Light Show** — Grace Cathedral, San Francisco, 8pm, free. Runs nightly this week, 8pm to midnight, and costs nothing — the cathedral's rose window rendered in light across the Nob Hill facade. Ten minutes of looking up, then go get dinner.

**Sondre Lerche** — The Independent, San Francisco, 8pm. Norwegian songwriter twenty years into a career of restless, jazz-inflected pop that keeps refusing to settle into one idiom. The Saxophones open — an Oakland duo whose becalmed, reverb-heavy ballads are a good tonal match.

**Filipiñana** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8:10pm. Also FiliKula, and it plays four times this week — a class-conscious drama set among the caddies of a Manila golf course.

**Death Guild** — DNA Lounge, San Francisco. The longest-running goth-industrial club night in the country — every Monday since 1993, and still the reason a queue forms on Eleventh Street on a work night. 18+.

**Monday Night Hubba** — DNA Lounge, San Francisco. Hubba Hubba Revue's weekly burlesque, in the smaller room, the night after their 20th-anniversary blowout. 18+.

**Waylon Wyatt** — The Fillmore, San Francisco. Arkansas country singer riding a streaming surge into rooms much larger than his last tour. Time and support not posted at the time of compiling.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Happy Days: Inconvenient Truth, Mankind, Wroht, Spawn, 40 Oz Felony** — 924 Gilman, Berkeley, 6:30pm, $5. Five bands for five dollars, all ages, volunteer-run. The first of five Gilman nights this week as the club works toward its 40th anniversary.

**Music Monday: The Throwback Series** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 8pm, $45–50. Yoshi's Monday residency, this edition built around throwback gospel. Lineup is not announced in advance, which is the format — expect Bay Area church-trained singers working a songbook.

**Bobby McFerrin** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. McFerrin in a 600-seat listening room is the kind of mismatch this digest exists to point at. His improvised solo work depends on hearing every overtone, and the Freight is built for exactly that.

**Pacifica Fog Fest** — Palmetto Avenue, Pacifica, 10am–6pm, free. Second day, with the parade. Free. Filed Peninsula by proximity — Pacifica is not in the region lists.

**SALA: South Asian Literature and Art Festival** — Menlo College, Atherton, 11:30am–7pm, $75 day / $140 weekend. Second day of panels. Filed Peninsula; Atherton is not in the region lists.

### San Francisco

**Fort Mason Center Farmers Market** — Fort Mason Center, San Francisco, 9:30am–1:30pm, free. Sunday morning on the Fort Mason apron with the bay behind it. Free to browse.

**Bodhi Mid-Autumn Festival with Lanterns** — San Francisco County Fair Building, San Francisco, 10am–5pm, free. Lanterns, mooncakes and performance in the old County Fair Building at Ninth and Lincoln. Free, and the quietest of the weekend's several Moon Festival events.

**Cole Valley Fair** — Cole Street, San Francisco, 10am–5pm, free. A small neighbourhood street fair a long way, in every sense, from what is happening on Folsom the same afternoon. Free. The N drops you at Carl and Cole.

**Golden Gate Park Nature Festival** — Music Concourse, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, 10am–2pm, free. Free, on the concourse between the de Young and the Academy — naturalists, live animals, and the park's own restoration crews explaining what they do.

**Art Vending Machine Weekend** — Locali Visitor Center, San Francisco, 11am–7pm, free. Second day. A dollar an item, seven artists, no gallery markup.

**Boof presents MCMLXXXV (Herrensauna)** — The Foundry, San Francisco, 11am. Doors at eleven in the morning. Berlin's Herrensauna crew doing a daytime Folsom party — hard, fast techno while the fair is still setting up eight blocks away.

**Folsom Street Fair** — Folsom Street, 8th to 13th, San Francisco, 11am–6pm, free. **Featured.** Thirteen blocks, seven stages, and something in the region of 400,000 people — the largest leather and fetish event in the world and the close of Leather Week. Free, with a $10–20 donation at the gates that funds the year's grantmaking. SoMa will be impassable by car from mid-morning; BART to Civic Center or Powell and walk in.

**German Day Festival** — Golden Gate Park Bandshell, San Francisco, 11am–4pm, free. Free, at the bandshell in the Music Concourse, and it overlaps the Park Band concert in the same spot — one follows the other, so you get both by not moving.

**Lindy in the Park** — JFK Drive, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, 11am–2pm, free. Free swing dancing on the closed stretch of JFK, every Sunday for close to thirty years. Beginner lesson at the start, no partner required, no charge.

**Golden Gate Park Band: Symphony in the Park** — Spreckels Temple of Music, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, 1pm–2:45pm, free. Free municipal band concerts since 1882, and this is the 144th season. The bandshell acoustics are better than they have any right to be, and the audience brings folding chairs and sandwiches.

**Randall Museum 75th Anniversary Festival** — Randall Museum, San Francisco, 1pm–5pm, free. Seventy-five years of the city's free children's science and art museum, up on Corona Heights. Free, and the view from the hill is the best in central San Francisco.

**Urban Wildlife 101** — San Francisco Main Public Library, San Francisco, 1pm–2pm, free. One hour on the coyotes, raccoons and raptors that actually live here. Free, in the Koret Auditorium, and the timing means you can walk to Folsom afterwards.

**Peter Pan Goes Wrong (preview)** — San Francisco Playhouse, San Francisco, 2pm, from $55. Sunday matinée preview. Union Square is walkable from BART Powell and the show runs into late November, so there is no urgency beyond preview pricing.

**Remedy 7** — Bottom of the Hill, San Francisco, 2pm–5pm, $20. A two o'clock matinée, done by five, which makes it the only rock show this weekend that does not conflict with anything.

**Dollygrass: A Tribute** — The Chapel, San Francisco, 7pm, $24–28. Dolly Parton's catalogue played bluegrass, by Godman, Tucker, Lewis and Gates, with Crying Uncle and the Jimmy Touzel Bluegrass Explosion. A tribute, but the players are the real thing and the material was bluegrass to begin with.

**Simon Boccanegra** — War Memorial Opera House, San Francisco, 7:30pm. Last of the three. Civic Center will be full of people coming back from Folsom; the opera house is a strange and rather wonderful place to end that day.

**Joon** — Café du Nord, San Francisco, 8pm. Sold out. Lauryn Kovacs opens.

**LUCKI** — The Warfield, San Francisco, 8pm. Chicago rap at its most narcotised and interior, with Sk8star. He has been making the same record better for a decade.

**Melt-Banana** — Great American Music Hall, San Francisco, 8pm, $28–33. Thirty-plus years of Tokyo noise-punk compressed into songs that rarely reach ninety seconds. Moon Destroys and RX.PINKNOISE open. The most physically demanding show of the week and easily the funniest.

**guardin** — Brick & Mortar Music Hall, San Francisco, 8:30pm, $12.63–26.74. Emo-rap from the SoundCloud generation, with kennedyxoxo and Fijimar. Cheap, all ages.

**Hot Chip (DJ Set)** — The Independent, San Francisco, 9pm. MPHD opens. A DJ set rather than the band, on the night half the city is already out — this is the Folsom afterparty for people who do not want a circuit party.

**JT** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 10pm, $30–40. Ten o'clock doors, 21+, with Naisha. City Girls' sharper half in a 400-cap room, on a night when the rest of the city is winding down.

**Bravo The Bagchaser** — The Regency Ballroom, San Francisco. Bay Area rap with Peysoh — local bills at this scale are rarer than they should be and this one is worth the ballroom.

**Brooke Candy + Violet Chachki** — DNA Lounge, San Francisco. Candy and Chachki closing Folsom Sunday — rap-pop provocateur and the most technically serious burlesque performer working. 18+. The best-matched double bill of the weekend.

**Eslabón Armado** — The Fillmore, San Francisco. Patterson's own, and one of the acts that turned corridos tumbados into the most-streamed music in the country. A 1,315-cap room is now far too small for them.

**Overmono (DJ Set) + Ben UFO** — Public Works, San Francisco. Ben UFO is one of the best selectors alive and Overmono do not do a bad night. The strongest club booking of Folsom weekend that is not on a pier.

**Portola Festival (day 2)** — Pier 80, San Francisco, $250 day / $380 weekend. Swedish House Mafia, Tiësto, Zara Larsson and Four Tet — a bigger, straighter day than Saturday's, and it collides head-on with Folsom. Doing both means leaving one of them early; the pier is a long, exposed walk from anywhere, so pick one.

**Sindhoor Natya: ENCOUNTER** — Dance Mission Theater, San Francisco. Final performance. Dance Mission is six minutes from BART 24th St, which on Folsom Sunday is the only sane approach to the Mission.

**Studio 456: Daytime Disco** — DNA Lounge, San Francisco, free. Free, in daylight, three blocks from the fair. The cheapest way to be part of Folsom Sunday without paying anyone a cover. 18+.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Bay Area Youth Climate Summit** — Brower Center, Berkeley, 8am–2pm, free. Free, youth-organised, all day. An eight o'clock Sunday start is a real ask and the programme is more substantial than the hour suggests.

**Bay Area Queer Zine Fest** — Oakland Asian Cultural Center, Oakland, 11am–5pm, free. Ninth year, free, dozens of tables of self-published work. The counterweight to Folsom across the bay — same weekend, same communities, entirely different register. BART Lake Merritt is two minutes away.

**Bay Area African Restaurant Week Festival** — For The Culture, Oakland, 12pm–7pm, free. Free to enter, food for sale, closing out a week of African restaurant programming across the region. A genuine food festival rather than a restaurant-week promotion.

**Free Oakland Up Market** — Free Oakland Up, Oakland, 12pm–4pm, free. Free community market. The organisers' listing does not give a street address — check before setting out.

**English Teacher / Bakhmaro 2050** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 1:30pm. Georgian Cinema Now, afternoon double.

**An Incomplete Day in the History of Georgia 10.06.93.** — BAMPFA, Berkeley, 4:30pm. Closes the Georgian series. A single day in 1993 reconstructed from archive footage — the most formally adventurous thing in the programme.

**Gilman Benefit Weekend: The Enemies, Street Eaters** — 924 Gilman, Berkeley, 6:30pm, $5. Closing night of the 40th-anniversary benefit run. Five dollars, again, because that is the entire argument.

**Erykah Badu with The Alchemist and Smino** — Greek Theatre, UC Berkeley, Berkeley, 7pm. **Featured.** Sold out, doors 5:30. Badu has spent the last few years touring a show that is closer to a séance than a concert, and the Alchemist and Smino are a real bill rather than warm-up. An outdoor Greek amphitheatre at the end of September is the best room in the region, and it will be cold by 9 — bring a layer.

**Isaiah Collier: Collier Plays Coltrane** — Yoshi's, Oakland, 7pm, $39–79. Chicago saxophonist in his twenties taking on the most over-attempted repertoire in jazz, and mostly getting away with it because he plays like he means it rather than like he studied it.

**Maestro Nayan Ghosh and Ishaan Ghosh: Synergy** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. Father and son, sitar and tabla, from one of the great Indian classical lineages — Nayan Ghosh is the only musician to perform publicly on both instruments at this level. A seated listening room is the right setting.

**The Australian Ballet: Oscar** — Zellerbach Hall, Berkeley. Closing performance of the North American premiere run. Berkeley Symphony in the pit.
