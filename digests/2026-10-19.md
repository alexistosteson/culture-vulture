# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 19 October 2026

Monday 19 October – Sunday 25 October. 472 listings, ordered by remove
from Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**The week belongs to one man and one piece of music.** Joe Hisaishi conducts the
San Francisco Symphony on Thursday, Friday and Saturday in the West Coast
premiere of his own **Concerto for Orchestra**, which the orchestra commissioned.
Forty years of Miyazaki scores have made him a household name in a way almost no
living composer is, and this is him in the concert hall writing for himself. It is
effectively sold out at face value and the resale is punishing. Nothing else this
week is close.

**The booking that should not exist is forty miles north.** **St. Vincent** plays
Sweetwater Music Hall's annual gala on Thursday — a 300-capacity room in Mill
Valley, for an artist who plays arenas. It clears the brief's bar for the drive
more easily than anything in months, and it is the same night as Hisaishi's first.
That is the week's one genuinely cruel conflict.

**Saturday is the biggest day this digest has recorded: 131 listings, 35 of them
free and 31 of them outdoors.** **Lit Crawl SF** closes Litquake with dozens of
readings running through the Mission from five until nine, free. The **44th Annual
Sandcastle Classic** fills Ocean Beach from eleven. The **de Young** and the
**Legion of Honor** are both free to Bay Area residents. **Bay Beats** launches
with a free concert at the Golden Gate Park Bandshell. There is a Halloween
festival in an Olmsted cemetery, a car festival in an Alameda aircraft hangar and
a costume party on the hangar deck of a haunted aircraft carrier.

**Sunday is the sleeper, and both of its best things are free or close to it.**
**SF Music Day** puts twenty-plus free concerts across four halls of the Veterans
Building from midday. The **Orchestre Symphonique de Montréal** play Davies at two
under Rafael Payare, with Leonidas Kavakos on the Tchaikovsky concerto and
Shostakovich's Tenth after the interval. They overlap entirely; Music Day is free
and all afternoon, and the Montreal orchestra is here once.

**122 of the 472 listings are free, and 68 are outdoors.** The free end of this
week is as strong as the ticketed end: three separate free things in Golden Gate
Park on Saturday, a free street festival on Polk Street on Friday, free rooftop
stargazing at SF State twice, YBCA's galleries free all day Wednesday, a shadow-cast
*Rocky Horror* in the main library for nothing, and **Belmont's hundredth birthday**
on Saturday with a vintage car parade and a drone show.

**It is Halloween-adjacent and Día de los Muertos week, and the two traditions are
doing very different things.** The Halloween end is free, outdoors and neighbourhood
-run: Rockridge's 34th parade, Montclair Village, the Laurel, Alamo Square's painted
ladies, trick-or-treat trails in Foster City and at the Winchester Mystery House.
The Día de los Muertos end is institutional and much better: **SOMArts' 27th In Their
Name** exhibition opens Friday, **Mariachi Herencia de México** play Zellerbach the
same night, **OMCA's 32nd community celebration** takes the whole museum campus on
Sunday, and the **Cal Academy's NightLife** does the Thursday version for $26.

**The theatre is the deepest it has been all autumn and almost none of it is new.**
*Peter Pan Goes Wrong* plays eight performances at the Playhouse, *Dracula* seven at
the Mountain View Center, Word for Word's *Assimilation* five at Z Below, and
*Oh, Mary!* at least five at the Curran. The exception, and the one genuinely new
thing, is **Berkeley Rep's *Liberation***: Bess Wohl's West Coast premiere begins
previews in the Roda Theatre on Friday and runs to the end of November. The one
short run is **Iphigenia** at Z Space — three performances and gone, on a stage
whose schedule exists nowhere but the show's own page.

**The South Bay has 48 listings and two that matter.** **Kalani Pe'a** at Montalvo's
Carriage House on Friday is the better: three Grammys for Hawaiian music, in a
300-seat barn, from a venue that entered this file last week and has now produced
its first listing. The **Mission Chamber Orchestra at the Hammer Theatre** on
Saturday is the other, and it is the first thing that venue has produced in four
weeks. Both of those are the direct result of the sources work done on 18 October.

**The Peninsula has 23 listings and Stanford is carrying most of them.** Stanford
Live read properly for the first time in a month: the Julian Lage Quartet in Bing
Concert Hall on Wednesday, **Jontavious Willis** twice on Friday and **Los Vega's
son jarocho** twice on Saturday, both in a 200-seat studio. Every summer series is
long finished.

**The outer region has two listings and that is a decision, not a gap.** St. Vincent
at Sweetwater clears the bar outright. **Eric Idle in conversation with Dave Eggers**
at Angelico Hall in San Rafael on Sunday is the second — he is eighty-three and does
not tour. Everything else was weighed and did not clear it: Felton Music Hall has
three shows, Sweetwater five, the Catalyst two, and Black Flag and BALTHVS both play
closer to home later in the week, which is exactly why neither Felton date qualifies.
Both calendars were read in full.

**The week's quiet failure is DNA Lounge, for the fourth week running,** and
**Litquake is the week's largest gap** — a festival of eighty-plus events is
represented here by six, because its schedule lives on a platform that refuses this
run. See **Thin this week** and **What could not be reached**.

## Monday 19 October

*26 listings, 10 of them free.* The thinnest night of the week and a real one rather than a research failure. **Best of the night:** the **Julian Lage Quartet** at Davies, with John Medeski on organ — jazz guitar played with a chamber musician's touch and a folk player's repertoire. If you would rather be in a club, **Geese** have sold out both Fox Theater nights and **Bobby McFerrin** is improvising a whole band's worth of music by himself in a 600-seat room in Berkeley, which is the better value of the two. The free pick is **Motown on Mondays** at Madrone, free before eight, fifteen years running.

### South Bay

**San Francisco 49ers vs Washington Commanders** — Levi's Stadium, Santa Clara, 5:15pm. Monday night football in the brief's home region, listed for completeness rather than recommended — the brief deprioritises stadiums and this one is a long way from cheap or walkable.

### Peninsula

**Dinosaur Jr. with Stef Chura** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 8pm. J Mascis at conversational volume is still the loudest thing on the Peninsula this week — three-chord melody buried under a wall of Marshall fuzz, forty years in and unreformed. A 500-cap room is an absurdly small place to hear it.

### San Francisco

**Japanese Tea Garden free admission hour** — Japanese Tea Garden, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, 9am, free. The oldest public Japanese garden in the country, free for the first hour on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays. Non-residents otherwise pay $12-16; San Francisco residents are always free with ID.

**Matinee: Lesser Care + Rotten Charms** — The Knockout, San Francisco, 5pm. A five o'clock darkwave matinee, which is a sentence that only makes sense at this bar. Lesser Care do the cold, reverbed end of post-punk properly.

**SF Politics 101** — SPUR Urban Center, San Francisco, 5:30pm, free. SPUR's plain-language explainer of how the city is actually governed — charter, commissions, where the money goes. Free, and more useful than most paid civics.

**SalsaCrazy Mondays** — Neck of the Woods, San Francisco, 6:30pm, $10-40. Lessons at half six, dancing after, and the floor stays busy until midnight on a weeknight. Ten dollars if you skip the class.

**Dirty Mondays: Pinfall, Driver Coaster, Nothing Special, A Dream About Home** — Kilowatt, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Four local bands for nothing on a Monday in a Mission bar. Unticketed, which is why no aggregator carries it — this listing exists because the venue's DICE calendar was read directly.

**Motown on Mondays** — Madrone Art Bar, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Fifteen years of the same idea done well: soul 45s, all night, free before eight and five dollars after. The longest-running weekly in the Divisadero corridor.

**Hayes Valley Comedy Night** — The Function, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Free with an RSVP, twenty to twenty-five at the door, two-drink minimum. A standing Monday that is reliably better than free comedy has any right to be.

**Open Mic Night** — Hotel Utah Saloon, San Francisco, 7:30pm, free. The oldest open mic in the city, in a saloon that predates the 1906 fire. Sign-up early; the list is long and the stage is small.

**almost monday with Rec Hall** — Castro Theatre, San Francisco, 8pm. San Diego trio making bright, major-key surf-pop that sounds engineered for a car advert and is better than that implies. Doors seven.

**Hovvdy with Emma Ogier** — The Chapel, San Francisco, 8pm, $26-30. Austin duo working the quietest end of American indie — two acoustic guitars, close harmony, drum machines kept low. Ten years of it now and they have got better at the restraint, not worse.

**NEW CONSTELLATIONS with Vox Rea** — The Independent, San Francisco, 8pm. Sold out before the week began. The touring version of a travelling arts-and-conversation project rather than a straight gig, which is why it went so fast.

**Bebe Stockwell with Khatumu** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 8pm, $20-25. Early-career singer-songwriter on a Monday, which is what the Rickshaw is for. All ages.

**DJ X'ces with Turbo Sonidero, Grupo Gallardo, Sonido Aztlan** — Great American Music Hall, San Francisco, 8pm, $24-27. Sonidero night in the old Barbary Coast hall — cumbia records pitched down and stretched out through a sound system, with Turbo Sonidero's digital rebuilds of the form alongside the traditional selectors. The best Monday dance in the city.

**Krazy for Karaoke** — The Knockout, San Francisco, 9pm, free. The Monday fixture, nine until close, free.

**Julian Lage Quartet with Laura Itandehui** — Davies Symphony Hall, San Francisco. **Featured.** Lage plays jazz guitar with the touch of a chamber musician and the repertoire instincts of a folk player, and the quartet with John Medeski on organ is the loosest version of the band. He does this again at Stanford on Wednesday; the Davies room is the bigger occasion, the Bing the better listen.

**Post Sex Nachos with Hannah Geller** — Café du Nord, San Francisco. Jangly Midwestern guitar pop with a band name designed to be unforgettable and unsayable. The basement room suits them better than the name suggests.

**Spencer Krug with Emory Duncan** — Swedish American Hall, San Francisco. The voice and the piano behind Wolf Parade and Sunset Rubdown, solo and seated in a wood-panelled hall built by a fraternal order. Krug's songs were always too strange for the rooms they got; this is the right one.

**Grupo Ju-Juy and Grupo Kl de Edwin Zacary** — Evolution San Francisco, San Francisco. Regional Mexican bill at the Mission nightclub; found only through the ticketing sweep, so the billing order may not be the room's.

**Yoon Chung Han: Dreaming Babies: illi (closing day)** — Gray Area, Grand Theater, San Francisco, free. Last day of Han's installation, which turns biometric data into generative sound and image. Gray Area publishes no gallery hours on its events page; the closing date is the one hard fact it gives.

### Berkeley & Oakland

**Town Biz Comedy Night with HellaFunny** — Mesa Maguey, Oakland, 7pm, free. Free with RSVP in a Fruitvale mezcaleria. HellaFunny run the best free comedy circuit in the East Bay and this is the Monday end of it.

**Free Solo Theater: Untold Stories of America's Dreamers** — Ashby Stage, Berkeley, 7pm, free. Shotgun Players' home stage, given over to solo work about immigration and DACA. Sliding-scale donation and free at the door, which is unusual for anything this side of a reading.

**Geese with Bill Orcutt** — Fox Theater, Oakland, 8pm. Both Oakland nights sold out. Geese have mutated from Brooklyn post-punk kids into something closer to a jam band with a theatre-kid frontman, and Cameron Winter's voice is the reason. Bill Orcutt opening — solo electric guitar at its most abrasive and least accommodating — is one of the best support bookings of the year.

**Bobby McFerrin** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. A ten-time Grammy winner in a 600-seat listening room, improvising with his voice as a whole band. McFerrin at this scale is a much stranger and better proposition than the one song everyone knows.

**Shoshana Bean** — Yoshi's, Oakland. Broadway belter working the cabaret-with-a-rhythm-section format. Big voice, small room.


## Tuesday 20 October

*35 listings, 10 free.* The night splits cleanly: **The Ring with the San Francisco Symphony playing Hans Zimmer live** at Davies, or **Allah-Las** at the Great American, or **The Devil Makes Three** opening two nights at the Chapel. **Best of the night:** Allah-Las, on the grounds that April Magazine opening makes it the only bill of the three where both halves matter. **Conflict worth knowing:** the Litquake readings — Alain Damasio at Telegraph Hill Books, Brian Evenson at City Lights — are both at seven and three blocks apart, so you can only have one. Free and outdoors: **Civic Center Soundtrack** on Fulton Plaza at midday, and **double dutch on Mechanics Monument Plaza**, which is not a thing the Financial District normally offers.

### Peninsula

**The Jerry Douglas Band** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 7:30pm. The most recorded dobro player alive, and the one who moved the instrument out of bluegrass orthodoxy and into something closer to jazz phrasing. Fourteen Grammys. Doors half six.

### San Francisco

**Civic Center Soundtrack** — Fulton Plaza, San Francisco, noon, free. Free lunchtime music and food trucks on the library side of Civic Center, Tuesdays and Thursdays. A small programme doing more for that plaza than any amount of policing has.

**Arthouse50: A Barbara Hammer Tribute** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 6pm. Hammer made the foundational body of lesbian experimental film in the Bay Area in the 1970s. Part of the Arthouse 50 series.

**826 Valencia's Writer's Block Party** — Southern Pacific Brewing, San Francisco, 6pm, $40. Fundraiser for the Mission writing centre, 21+, and the ticket covers the beer and wine. $40.

**Peter Pan Goes Wrong** — San Francisco Playhouse, San Francisco, 7pm, $60-145. The Mischief company's wreck-the-pantomime formula, running at the Playhouse until the end of November. Physical comedy executed with the precision of a stunt sequence. Tuesday is the cheap night of the week.

**Oh, Mary!** — Curran Theatre, San Francisco, 7pm. Cole Escola's Mary Todd Lincoln as a thwarted cabaret diva, eighty minutes, no interval, and the funniest thing to come off Broadway in years. A.C.T. publishes no curtain times anywhere on its site, so this time comes from ticket resellers and is the honest best available.

**Litquake: Alain Damasio, Horde of the Counterwind** — Telegraph Hill Books, San Francisco, 7pm, free. The French science-fiction novelist whose best-known book has only just been translated into English, in a small North Beach bookshop. Free, with a $10-15 suggested donation.

**Litquake: Brian Evenson** — City Lights Booksellers, San Francisco, 7pm, free. Evenson writes short horror fiction with the sentences filed down to nothing, and he is one of the few writers whose work is genuinely improved by being read aloud.

**Mad Props: A Comedy Breakdown of the California Ballot** — KALW, San Francisco, 7pm, free. KALW get comedians to read the ballot propositions so you do not have to. Free, and genuinely the most efficient way to arrive at an informed vote.

**The Ring: film with live orchestra** — Davies Symphony Hall, San Francisco, 7:30pm, from $69. Verbinski's 2002 remake with Hans Zimmer's score played live by the San Francisco Symphony. The score is mostly texture rather than tune, which makes it a genuinely odd thing to watch an orchestra produce. From $69.

**Hayes Valley Comedy Night** — The Function, San Francisco, 7:30pm, free. Free with RSVP, two-drink minimum.

**Shakey Graves with Wild Pink** — Castro Theatre, San Francisco, 8pm. Alejandro Rose-Garcia's one-man-band act grew into a real band some years ago and the songs got better for it. Wild Pink's open-hearted heartland rock is the right opener.

**Allah-Las with April Magazine** — Great American Music Hall, San Francisco, 8pm, $39.50-44. Los Angeles band who have made the same sun-bleached sixties garage record five or six times and are very good at it. San Francisco's own April Magazine open with something hazier and more damaged.

**The Devil Makes Three with Two Runner** — The Chapel, San Francisco, 8pm, $40-46. First of two nights. Santa Cruz-formed trio playing ragtime, country blues and murder ballads with no drummer and a lot of forward momentum. Two Runner are the best new old-time duo in the state.

**Tōth with Sean Nicholas Savage** — Brick & Mortar Music Hall, San Francisco, 8pm, $32.71. Alex Toth left Rubblebucket's horn section to make quieter, sadder records under his own name. Sean Nicholas Savage opening is the wild card — lounge-pop sung with total sincerity and no irony at all.

**Sawyer Hill with Never Ending Fall** — The Independent, San Francisco, 8pm. Young songwriter on a first proper headline run.

**Avery Cochrane with Karly Bowman** — Rickshaw Stop, San Francisco, 8pm, $20-24. All ages, doors at seven.

**Copeathetic with Scissoring, Half Calf and Closegood** — The Knockout, San Francisco, 8pm. Four-band local bill, eight o'clock.

**Lost Highway** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8pm. Lynch's least-loved and most genuinely unnerving nineties film. Robert Blake's Mystery Man scene has not got less frightening.

**This Is Buzz, with Mark Pellington Q&A** — Roxie Theater, San Francisco, 8:20pm. Documentary with the director in the room afterwards.

**Kilowatt Karaoke** — Kilowatt, San Francisco, 9pm. Nine o'clock, Tuesdays, and busier than most Mission bands manage.

**Laura Veirs and Karl Blau** — Café du Nord, San Francisco. Veirs writes plain-spoken songs about weather, geology and marriage and sings them in a voice that never oversells. Karl Blau's countrypolitan baritone is a real pairing, not a warm-up.

**ARTMS** — Regency Ballroom, San Francisco. Five of the former LOONA members, reconstituted after a long contract fight, and one of the more interesting things in K-pop because the group effectively owns its own narrative now.

**Drift Mothership, Manifestiv and Big Empty Zero** — DNA Lounge, San Francisco. The only touring concert either DNA mirror carries this week, and the club's own calendar would not load. Psych-leaning local bill; see Thin this week for what is missing.

## Wednesday 21 October

*50 listings, 12 free.* Wednesday is the week's quiet surprise. **Best of the night:** the **Julian Lage Quartet** again, this time in Bing Concert Hall at Stanford — the same band in a room built for acoustic detail, and the better of the two nights if you care about hearing it rather than being at it. In the city, **DIIV** are the loud choice and **Todd Rundgren** the strange one. **Free:** YBCA's galleries are free all day and open until eight, which is the best free art in the region on any Wednesday; the library is running **Rocky Horror with a full shadow cast** for nothing at five; and **Jazz Mafia play their season finale** free on a downtown plaza at lunchtime. The oddity of the night is **Glue Stick Social**, junk journaling in a West Oakland church by the light of a silent horror film.
